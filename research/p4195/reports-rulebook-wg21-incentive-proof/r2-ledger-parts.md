## Foundation changes

- ax:prefer. Old: "Other things equal, each participant, sponsor, national body, and user prefers whatever brings it more of what it values, sooner and at less cost to it, weighing gains against losses on one scale of its own. For this proof, a participant values adoption of the proposals they support, the interests of their sponsor if they have one, their standing, keeping any chair or convenor post they hold, their time and money, and a good result for users; a sponsor values its interests, which can depend on particular features of a design; a national body values the outcomes its national position supports, the interests concerned at its national level where it has no position yet, and its members' time; and a user values the features they use or would use, how well those features work, and their own time and money." New: "Other things equal, each participant, sponsor, national body, and user prefers more of what it values, sooner and cheaper, weighing gains against losses on one scale of its own. For this proof, a participant values adoption of the proposals they support, the interests of their sponsor if they have one, their standing, keeping any chair or convenor post they hold, their time and money, and a good result for users; a sponsor values its interests, which can depend on particular features of a design; a national body values the outcomes its national position supports, the interests concerned at its national level where it has no position yet, and its members' time; and a user values the features they use or would use, how well those features work, and their own time and money."
- ax:support. Old: "A participant who votes Against a colleague's proposal cannot rule out that the colleague will support the participant's own proposals less readily. A Favor vote gives the colleague no reason for this, and a Neutral vote or none gives no more reason than an Against vote does." New: "A participant who votes Against a colleague's proposal cannot rule out that the colleague will support the participant's own proposals less readily. A Favor vote, or one the colleague cannot attribute, gives no reason for this, and a Neutral vote or none gives no more reason than an Against vote does."
- ax:post-risk. Old: "A chair cannot rule out that a consensus call not following the 2:1 norm, or polling that departs from what SD-4 encourages, puts their post at more risk than following them. New: "A chair cannot rule out that a consensus call departing from the 2:1 norm, or polling departing from what SD-4 encourages, puts their post at more risk than following them. The convenor likewise cannot rule this out for a call departing from SD-4's plenary procedure."
- ax:rework. Old: "Reversing a decision, or changing a design after work is built on it, means redoing or discarding that work, which costs those who built it and takes meeting time from other papers. New: "Reversing a decision, or changing a design after work is built on it, means redoing or discarding that work, which costs its builders and takes meeting time from other papers. After a favorable poll, authors and others build more on the design, such as wording, implementations, and proposals that depend on it."
- ax:schedule. Old: "WG21 plans a new edition of the C++ standard every three years, each with the features approved by its cutoff, and ISO publishes each edition once national bodies approve it." - d:follows-norm. Old: "With Neutral votes short of a majority of those voting, a chair follows the 2:1 norm by declaring consensus on a count above 2:1 after discussion and any re-poll, and on no other count. New: "With Neutral votes short of a majority of those voting, a chair follows the 2:1 norm by declaring consensus exactly on counts above 2:1 after discussion and any re-poll. - d:does-better. - m:moves-chair (12.2R3, 12.2D9). Old: "Any chair can choose the polls, order the agenda by SD-4's priorities, time-box a new proposal, re-poll after discussion, ask Against voters for reasons, and allow limited exploration of alternatives. - m:moves-convenor (follow-on). Old: "The convenor can appoint chairs, form study groups, invite a guest for up to two meetings, and make plenary consensus calls. In plenary the convenor can ask whether Against votes are personal or national body positions."

- l:neutral-outside (12.2D12). Old: "The 2:1 comparison counts only Favor and Against votes, though SD-4 says a Neutral majority of those voting can be concerning."
- p:against-costs-more (12.2D13). Old: "For a regular, an Against vote on a colleague's proposal costs more than a Neutral vote or none. It risks the colleague's later support no less, and SD-4 tells only Against voters to be ready to give reasons, which take time to prepare."

It says the same of a ballot comment repeating an objection decided where its national body was present and heard, unless the comment brings new information or the body warned all along that it would vote No."
- p:late-objection (follow-on). Old: "SD-4 gives an objection weight at plenary only if its holder escalated it by 5pm the evening before, on the email lists and within their national body. There, by default, it is not a national position, and after the deadline even new information goes to the email lists or a ballot comment, with a discussion paper."
- p:fluency (12.2D25). Old: "Every design needs more than merit to be considered: a proposal needs an on-time paper and a presenter, a rival needs a paper to exist, and objections need fixed forms and times. Participants learn how the committee applies these rules partly by attending, some of it only that way, and other things equal, whoever knows more has the better chance of adoption, whatever their design skill."
- p:newcomer (12.2D26). Old: "Other things equal, a newcomer, lacking what earlier meetings teach, spends more effort getting a paper considered than a returning participant, though SD-4 expects everyone to know its procedures. Where a study group incubates proposals on its topic, a newcomer's early proposal can start there."
- l:poll-wording (12.2D28). Old: "A direction poll asks voters only to agree that work should continue, less than a forwarding poll asks, so it can pass where a forwarding poll would fail. Yet passing it in a design room makes the design the incumbent."
- p:roadmap-poll (follow-on). Old: "If a poll places a proposal among the work the committee aims to include in the next IS, the proposal joins a category SD-4 has chairs take up first. A meeting's hours are fixed, so the time it then gets comes out of the time left for papers outside those categories."
- p:light-review (12.2D29, 12.2A4). Old: "Those who have not read a paper or followed its discussion typically abstain, so a proposal costing more to read than most stakes in it is voted on mainly by those with large stakes. Unless those of them who object cast at least a third of the Favor-plus-Against votes, it can advance with no change at all."
- p:late-arrival (12.2D30, 12.2D31). Old: "An objector who arrives after a design has been adopted faces three obstacles at once. Removal needs a paper or ballot comment meeting the 2:1 norm, reversal costs more for every decision built on the design, and the record keeps its case but unwritten objections only in memory."
- p:first-part (12.2D32, 12.2A24, 12.2B12). Old: "A first part that fixes a design's structure can be adopted before reviewers can study in detail what later parts need of it, and then every rival to that structure must replace it. SD-4 asks no paper to state those needs, so a design-bound author's best first paper is the smallest part that fixes the structure and still meets the 2:1 norm against opponents who foresee the rest."
- p:large-position (12.2D34). Old: "A large proposal has more sponsored authors in the room than a small one, and is expected to face more meetings where a poll can make it the incumbent or end its incumbency."
- p:size-attributes (12.2D35). Old: "Each result in this chapter about large or small proposals rests on an attribute their definitions name: who pays the authors' costs, the number of authors, review's expected span, or the paper's length. So it holds for any proposal with that attribute, whatever its size."
- p:sg-sponsor (12.2D36). Old: "Paying participants to attend a study group puts a sponsor's voters where the design the design room starts from is chosen. Where the other voters cast fewer Favor-plus-Against votes, fewer paid participants make up a third of the total, enough to block, or over two thirds, enough to meet the norm."
- l:call-above (12.2D37, 12.2B21). Old: "On a count above 2:1 after discussion, with Neutral votes short of a majority, a design room chair whose stake is worth less than what departing can cost ranks declaring consensus above declining."
- p:chair-confidence (12.2R5, 12.2D40). Old: "Of two papers otherwise alike, a chair does better taking up first the one whose author has a record of following procedures and responding to review, which promises fewer problems. SD-4 expects that record to come from taking part: presenting, answering questions, and making the changes the room directs."
- p:plenary-ratifies (12.2D41). Old: "At plenary, design stays with the design rooms, SD-4 gives no weight to objections not escalated in time, and all present are asked for serious design concerns. Most polls pass by unanimous consent, and when opposition is significant the convenor usually asks whether Against votes are personal or national body positions."
- p:who-takes-part (12.2D42, 12.2D43, 12.2A15, 12.2B14). Old: "Fees are barred and everyone in a subgroup's room has one vote, yet only those who bear the cost of taking part, or whose sponsors do, vote in WG21's polls. ISO can remove inactive national body experts, and participants propose for needs they and their sponsors know, which, since users differ from them in what matters, need not match users'."
- p:reward-timing (12.2D44, 12.2B11). Old: "Authors get the adoption they value at the poll, while a flaw that implementation and use have not yet exposed costs users only later, and can fall on any user of the feature."
- p:merit-gap (12.2D45). Old: "Winning a consensus call and producing the design that serves users best coincide only as far as voters can judge that at the poll and no other value they hold outweighs it. SD-4 assigns no one to judge it for them."
- p:integration (12.2A23). Old: "Papers approved separately at one meeting first come together when the project editor merges their wording, which the editor's report records and a pre-ballot editing committee verifies. No SD-4 step checks whether the designs work together, while each author gains from their own design's adoption and a conflict costs users and later rework."
- p:written-answer (12.2D46). Old: "No rule requires a written answer to an objection beyond three: an author's defense of declining a directed change, ISO's response to enquiry ballot comments, and a TS ballot's Disposition of Comments. For the author, a written answer stating the objection makes it easier to raise again, but also shows later what would not be new, the test SD-4 applies to repeated objections."
- p:cutoff-effect (12.2D47). Old: "At the feature cutoff, a one-meeting delay for a paperless rival sends a proposal to the next planned edition. So does an Against vote keeping it at or below the 2:1 line."
- p:prefer-is (12.2D48). Old: "A TS delays IS adoption by about one to two years, and its experience can bring incompatible changes first: a gain to result-seeking authors, a risk to design-bound ones. It also reaches implementers and users early and can publish while concerns remain, if their holders agree, and the design room can require one, or the lighter white paper ISO and SC22 now recommend."
- l:ballot-rules (12.2D49). Old: "Every national body may vote on enquiry and final ballots, which pass when two thirds of the P-members' counted votes are positive and at most a quarter of all counted votes negative. Abstentions and negative votes without technical comments go uncounted, ISO encourages abstaining without national consensus or expert input, sustained opposition does not apply, and a failed final draft returns to SC22."
- p:nb-comment (12.2D50). Old: "A national body's No opposes every feature in the document at once, including ones its own experts support, while WG21 considers its comments whatever its vote. In the cases the table below describes, a body valuing the rest of a document does better voting Yes with comments, and one wanting the work in no form does better voting No."
- l:rank-oppose-no (12.2L5). Old: "A body that wants the work in no form ranks No above abstaining, since only the No counts against approval, which is what SD-4 says No is for."
- p:credible-threat (12.2D52). Old: "At ballot, a national body whose comments fare the same under any vote does better voting No over one feature only if it values removing that feature above the rest of the document on schedule. SD-4 expects such a body to have told the committee all along, and ISO asks every national body to make its standpoint clear early."
- p:nb-members (12.2D53). Old: "A national body forms its position by its panel's own rules, which SD-4 does not state, so whether a sponsor's paid participants add weight there depends on those rules. In the United States, INCITS gives each voting member one vote, apart from up to four Defense Department votes, bars consultants a voting member funds from voting membership, and binds all delegates to official positions."
- l:incits-votes (12.2D54). Old: "INCITS gives each organization one voting membership, apart from up to four Department of Defense votes on technical committees, and treats an entity another controls as part of it on its Executive Board. It admits a consultant as a voting member only if no voting member funds it, and lets the representatives of standards developing organizations and consortia be employed by other voting members."
- l:settled-design-bound (12.2R9, 12.2D56, 12.2A16). Old: "Whatever the others choose, a design-bound author does best seeking direction polls as early as they can pass, proposing first the smallest part that fixes its features and can pass, and choosing the IS where it can pass. It does best leaving out a trade-off no reviewer would raise when stating it now costs more than later exposure would, seeking no poll it expects to lose to a rival, and answering objections with the cheapest change that keeps the design passing."
- l:settled-result-seeking (12.2D58). Old: "Whatever the others choose, a result-seeking author does best stating a trade-off and granting a change whenever the better design they bring outweighs the votes or work they cost. It does best seeking an early poll or first part only where sooner adoption outweighs the cost it adds to later improvements, and backing any rival that serves users better by more than its share of the reversal's cost."
- l:settled-small-author (12.2D62). Old: "Whatever the others choose, a small proposal's author does best keeping it presented while its chance of adoption is worth more to them than the cost, borne alone, of the meetings still ahead. The longer it must still wait behind priority work, the more those costs come to, and once they outweigh its chance, the author does best letting it stop."
- l:settled-sg-chair (12.2L9). Old: "Whatever the others choose, a study group chair does best polling competing designs, side by side where several compete, and sending on one that meets the 2:1 norm, or none. Only a chair whose stake in the contest is worth more to them than what departing can cost does better departing from this."
- l:settled-design-chair (follow-on). Old: "Whatever the others choose, a design room chair whose stake is worth less than what departing can cost them does best following the 2:1 norm after discussion. It does best taking up first, of papers otherwise alike, the one whose author has a record of following the procedures."
- l:settled-convenor (12.2D68). Old: "Whatever the others choose, the convenor does best following SD-4's plenary procedure unless their stake is worth more than what departing can cost them. That procedure fixes the call in two cases, declaring consensus when no one objects to unanimous consent or when all opposition is unescalated and holds no national position, and otherwise leaves it to the convenor."
- l:settled-nb (12.2D69). Old: "Whatever the others choose, a national body whose comments fare the same under any vote does best voting Yes with comments if it values a document's rest above removing any one feature, and No otherwise. It does best raising its objections early, as SD-4 and ISO ask, and ISO encourages it to abstain when it lacks national consensus or expert input."
- l:settled-sponsor (12.2D71). Old: "Whatever the others choose, a sponsor does best paying for participants in the rooms whose decisions it values, study groups included, while what their votes bring it is worth their cost. Each such participant votes valuing its interests, and no rule limits how many it pays."
- l:settled-user (12.2D72). Old: "Whatever the others choose, a user without a sponsor does best taking part only when their own stake outweighs the full price, which they bear alone while any better design's gain goes to all users. Otherwise the best move is a cheaper one or none: TS feedback, a paper a participant presents, or a request to a national body."
- p:checks (12.2D73). Old: "The rules provide these checks: every view must be heard, a third of the Favor-plus-Against votes can block, rivals with papers can be polled together, and implementation experience informs votes. Plenary invites serious design concerns, national bodies vote on each balloted draft and get answers to enquiry comments, editing committees verify applied changes, minutes can be challenged, and national bodies can appeal."
- l:checks-hearing (follow-on). Old: "SD-4 requires every view to be heard and understood and the majority to listen, and asks for respect, good faith, and no collusion, with conduct concerns reported through ISO's process. It welcomes negative comments, and any group casting a third of the Favor-plus-Against votes as Against keeps a proposal from meeting the 2:1 norm."
- l:checks-alternatives (12.2D74). Old: "Chairs may allow limited exploration of alternatives, and SD-4 encourages polling alternatives side by side and asks a good paper to show the alternatives considered, its design principles, and motivating examples from today's code. Followup and rebuttal papers can be considered, an author who declines a directed change should defend that decision, and SD-4 does not significantly delay concrete proposals to wait for alternatives."
- l:checks-record (12.2D75). Old: "SD-4 asks those with a concern to tell chairs and authors early, plenary solicits serious design concerns, national bodies vote on each enquiry and final ballot, every enquiry comment gets a written response, and editing committees verify applied changes. Minutes can be challenged, national bodies can appeal, on drafts only over principle or harm to ISO's or IEC's reputation, ISO says consensus requires resolving substantial objections and ends the committee stage when all technical issues are resolved, and SD-4 works in addition to the Directives."

- c:pred-rival-polls (12.2D103). - c:pred-removals (12.2D104). Old: "The model predicts rare removals after adoption, by paper or ballot comment, likelier when national bodies whose No votes could fail a ballot oppose the design; working drafts, ballot comments, and poll results are the evidence. - c:pred-small-fade (12.2D107). Old: "The model predicts that small proposals stop advancing without a negative poll more often than large ones; the mailings and poll results are the evidence, and no such difference would count against it. - c:pred-nb-positions (12.2D109). - c:pred-written-answers (12.2D110). - c:pred-defects (12.2D111). No difference would count against it, and comparing features matched on scope, novelty, prior implementation, and use would tell it apart from another explanation, that contested features draw more scrutiny."
- c:pred-competing (12.2D113). - c:lever-anonymous (follow-on). Old: "If design polls were anonymous, the standing part of an Against vote's cost would shrink for voters who do not speak against the proposal. - c:lever-sponsor-limit (12.2D116). Old: "If the rules limited how many participants one sponsor pays may vote in a room, the votes cast there by participants valuing its interests would be capped. The model predicts smaller differences in poll margins between large and small proposals."
- c:lever-nb-more (12.2D118).
