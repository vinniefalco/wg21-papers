## 5. Momentum: incumbents and the cost of reversal

1. **Induction.** Each decision built on a design adds work that reversing the design would redo or discard, and more is built after each favorable poll. So the cost of reversing a design grows as it advances, whether or not the design is wrong. *[from ax:rework, ax:prefer, d:reversal, f:design-forward, f:wording-forward, f:plenary-owns]* {#ind:reversal-grows}
1. **Proposition.** A design passing a design room's direction poll becomes the incumbent, a paperless rival cannot block it, and the chair may delay it one meeting for a paper to arrive. A rival with a paper can be polled alongside it, the room can direct changes, and by default a TS merge proceeds with it unless an alternative is actively progressing. *[from f:no-paper, f:no-wait-alternatives, f:explore-alternatives, f:direction-implement, f:multiple-polls, f:ts-default, d:incumbent, d:polls]* {#p:incumbent-start}
1. **Proposition.** A design can become the incumbent at its first direction poll, when it has had less review than at any later meeting. From then on, every rival and every later change to it bears a reversal cost that grows as more is built on it. *[proved by l:first-meeting-poll, l:rival-reversal]* *[from ax:first-review, ax:unknown, d:incumbent, d:polls, p:incumbent-start, ind:reversal-grows, d:reversal]* {#p:early-polls}
   1. **Lemma.** When a proposal is first presented to the committee in a design room, the chair may take a direction poll on it at that meeting. *[from m:moves-chair, f:chair-polls, d:polls, d:rooms]* {#l:first-meeting-poll}
   1. **Lemma.** Adopting a rival in an incumbent's place, or later changing it, replaces all or part of what a design room approved in a direction poll, so each is a reversal. *[from d:incumbent, d:polls, d:reversal]* {#l:rival-reversal}
1. **Proposition.** A rival wins a voter from an incumbent only with an advantage worth more to that voter than their share of the reversal's cost. That hurdle grows with each decision built on the incumbent, which faces no such hurdle itself. *[proved by l:only-rival-reverses, l:rival-net]* *[from ax:prefer, ind:reversal-grows, p:incumbent-start, d:incumbent, d:reversal, d:does-better]* {#p:challenger-bar}
   1. **Lemma.** Keeping an incumbent removes or replaces nothing, so of the two choices only adopting a rival in its place is a reversal. *[from l:rival-reversal, d:reversal]* {#l:only-rival-reverses}
   1. **Lemma.** A participant does better with a rival than with the incumbent only when the rival's advantage to them outweighs their share of the reversal's cost, which keeping the incumbent avoids. *[from l:only-rival-reverses, ax:rework, ax:prefer, d:does-better]* {#l:rival-net}
1. **Lemma.** A direction poll asks voters only to agree that work should continue, less than a forwarding poll asks, so it can pass where a forwarding poll would fail. Yet passing it in a design room makes the design the incumbent. *[from d:polls, d:incumbent, f:design-forward, ax:prefer, p:early-polls]* {#l:poll-wording}
1. **Proposition.** If a poll places a proposal among the work the committee aims to include in the next IS, the proposal joins a category SD-4 has chairs take up first. A meeting's hours are fixed, so the time it then gets comes out of the time left for papers outside those categories. *[from f:prioritization, ax:hours]* {#p:roadmap-poll}
1. **Proposition.** Close study takes longer the bigger a design is, and a bigger design has more places where a defect can go unnoticed. For a reviewer whose stake does not grow with size, close study therefore stops being worth its time sooner on a big design than on a small one. *[proved by l:study-worth]* *[from ax:review-cost, ax:attention, ax:unknown, ax:prefer, d:stake, d:does-better]* {#p:review-share}
   1. **Lemma.** Studying a proposal affects its poll only through what the participant learns, which can change their vote or what they say in discussion. So what studying brings them through that poll is worth no more than their stake in it. *[from ax:attention, f:negative-welcome, d:stake]* {#l:study-worth}
1. **Proposition.** Participants who do not study a proposal typically abstain, so one costing more to study than most stakes in it is voted on mainly by those with large stakes. Unless those of them who object cast at least a third of the Favor-plus-Against votes, it can advance with no change at all. *[proved by l:skip-study, l:under-third]* *[from f:not-vote, p:review-share, ax:review-cost, ax:prefer, l:vote-count, p:least-cost-response]* {#p:light-review}
   1. **Lemma.** A participant whose stake in a proposal is worth less than the time studying it takes does better not studying it. *[from l:study-worth, ax:review-cost, ax:prefer, d:does-better]* {#l:skip-study}
   1. **Lemma.** When fewer than a third of the Favor-plus-Against votes are Against, Favor votes are more than twice the Against votes, which meets the 2:1 norm. *[from f:two-to-one, d:positions]* {#l:under-third}
1. **Proposition.** A design normally needs the 2:1 norm to advance, and once adopted, a paper or ballot comment to remove it normally needs the same, switching the default to keeping it. After a successful final ballot, design changes are expected to have near-unanimous consent, ISO bars technical changes at approval, and SD-4 calls published features much harder to update incompatibly. *[proved by l:removal-routes, l:removal-polls]* *[from f:two-to-one, f:no-paper, f:comment-content, f:near-unanimous, f:late-changes, f:is-hard-to-change, d:reversal]* {#p:burden-flip}
   1. **Lemma.** Removing an adopted design is a design change, so a proposal to do it needs a paper, and a national body can also request it in a ballot comment. *[from f:no-paper, f:comment-content, m:moves-nb]* {#l:removal-routes}
   1. **Lemma.** Design rooms own selecting designs and plenary does not change them, so a removal, by paper or comment, normally needs the 2:1 norm in a design room. *[from f:vehicle, f:plenary-no-design, f:two-to-one, l:removal-routes]* {#l:removal-polls}
1. **Proposition.** An objector who arrives after a design has been adopted faces three obstacles at once. Removal needs a paper meeting the 2:1 norm, reversal costs more for every decision built on the design, and the record keeps its case but unwritten objections only in memory. *[from p:burden-flip, ind:reversal-grows, p:challenger-bar, p:reasons-unrecorded, p:late-objection]* {#p:late-arrival}

In plain terms: a design can become the incumbent at its first direction poll, when it has had less review than at any later meeting, and every rival or later change then bears a reversal cost that grows with each decision built on it. A proposal that costs more to study than most stakes in it is voted on mainly by those with large stakes, and it can advance with no change at all unless its objectors cast at least a third of the Favor-plus-Against votes. After adoption, removing a design normally needs the same 2:1 norm it needed to advance, and an objector who arrives then also faces the grown reversal cost and a record that keeps the design's case but unwritten objections only in memory.
