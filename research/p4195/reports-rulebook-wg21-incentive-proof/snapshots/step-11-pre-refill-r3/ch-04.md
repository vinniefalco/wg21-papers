## 4. What the record keeps and how objections count

1. **Proposition.** A proposal can advance in WG21's rooms and plenary with objections unresolved, since it needs a consensus call, which the 2:1 norm normally supports, not every objector's persuasion. *[proved by l:address-not-resolve, p:advance-unanswered]* {#p:persuasion-not-required}
   1. **Lemma.** Under ISO, the obligation to address a sustained opposition is no obligation to resolve it, and a sustained opposition is not a veto. Still, ISO's general principles say consensus requires the resolution of substantial objections. *[from f:not-veto, f:resolve-substantial]* {#l:address-not-resolve}
   1. **Proposition.** A proposal can advance while discussed objections to it remain unresolved, because advancing requires a consensus call, which the 2:1 norm normally supports, and consensus need not imply unanimity. *[from l:address-not-resolve, f:design-forward, f:wording-forward, f:plenary-owns, f:two-to-one, f:consensus-def]* {#p:advance-unanswered}
1. **Proposition.** The record SD-4 and the searched ISO clauses require of a WG21 poll need not show whether an objection was answered or outvoted, or why consensus was called over it. *[proved by l:record-content, l:record-checks, l:iso-register-level]* *[from f:iso-no-reason-record]* {#p:record-indistinct}
   1. **Lemma.** SD-4 requires minutes and lets poll questions and numeric results be quoted publicly, but requires no record of how an objection was answered or why a consensus call was made. Plenary may ask a subgroup chair to summarize the reason for a decision. *[from f:minutes, f:protected, f:no-reason-record, f:plenary-reason]* {#l:record-content}
   1. **Lemma.** ISO lets participants comment on or object to minutes before they are confirmed, and has the chair of SC22 see that points of view at SC22's meetings are summarized. It bars participants from commenting publicly on others' contributions. *[from f:minutes-objections, f:views-summarized, f:no-public-comment]* {#l:record-checks}
   1. **Lemma.** The chair of SC22 judges consensus at SC22's committee stage, and ISO 2.5.7 recommends steps for a sustained opposition, including registering it, to the leadership. It does not say whether a convenor applies them inside a working group, and under either reading SD-4 adds no step for assessing or registering an opposition in subgroup polls. *[from f:chair-judges, f:pmember-consensus, f:committee-stage, f:iso-register, f:leadership-judges, f:sustained-def, f:no-sustained-procedure, d:national-body]* {#l:iso-register-level}
1. **Proposition.** SD-4 adopts ISO's definition of consensus, which turns on sustained opposition, but names no step for judging whether an opposition is sustained or for registering one, and no criteria for an important part of the concerned interests. Whoever ISO's leadership is for a working group, SD-4's own steps are the 2:1 norm after discussion, reasons from Against voters on request, escalation, and plenary's 3-way poll and question about national positions. *[proved by l:sd4-iso-definition]* *[from f:wg-consensus, f:consensus-def, f:sustained-def, f:two-to-one, f:against-reasons, f:escalate-deadline, f:plenary-consent, f:plenary-nb, f:iso-register, f:no-sustained-procedure]* {#p:definition-without-procedure}
   1. **Lemma.** SD-4 quotes a definition of consensus that requires the absence of sustained opposition to substantial issues by any important part of the concerned interests. ISO 2.5.7 has the chair of SC22 judge consensus bearing in mind the ISO/IEC Guide 2 definition, and explains what sustained opposition and the concerned interests mean. *[from f:consensus-def, f:chair-judges, f:sustained-def, f:concerned-interests, d:national-body]* {#l:sd4-iso-definition}
1. **Lemma.** Under SD-4's definition, an objection prevents consensus only if it concerns a substantial issue, is maintained, and comes from an important part of the concerned interests. The leadership judges that part case by case, none of the three conditions asks whether the objection is right, and every view must still be heard. *[proved by l:important-part]* *[from f:consensus-def, f:sustained-def, f:concerned-interests, f:majority-listen, f:views-heard]* {#l:consensus-test}
   1. **Lemma.** ISO has the committee leadership determine the concerned interests case by case, and recommends that the leadership assess whether an important part of them sustains an opposition. *[from f:concerned-interests, f:iso-register]* {#l:important-part}
1. **Proposition.** When an objection's Against votes are too few to stop the 2:1 norm, the cheapest response keeping it met at that poll is no change or a partial change. Finding out whether those objectors are right leaves that count unchanged, and SD-4 expects any change the room directs to be made or defended with new information. *[proved by l:change-cost, l:study-count]* *[from ax:prefer, ax:redesign-cost, ax:review-cost, l:vote-count, f:direction-implement]* {#p:least-cost-response}
   1. **Lemma.** A change to a design costs its authors the time to write a revised paper, and revising the design throughout costs them more than a change confined to one part. *[from f:no-paper, ax:review-cost, ax:redesign-cost]* {#l:change-cost}
   1. **Lemma.** Studying whether objectors are right takes time, while the 2:1 comparison counts only how many vote Favor and how many vote Against. *[from ax:review-cost, l:vote-count]* {#l:study-count}
1. **Proposition.** SD-4 asks an Against voter to be ready to name the changes that would win them over, or to say that none would. Concerns stated as changes give authors items to grant one at a time, while opposition in any form offers none and weighs in the poll only as a vote. *[from f:against-reasons, m:moves-author]* {#p:change-list}
1. **Proposition.** Of minutes, non-public email lists, meeting records, ballot comments, and papers, only a paper puts an objection's argument outside the password-protected materials without anyone else's help. Writing one takes time. *[from p:record-indistinct, f:protected, f:minutes-objections, f:comments-national, f:late-info, f:escalate-paper, m:moves-regular, ax:review-cost]* {#p:dissent-needs-paper}
1. **Proposition.** A proposal's case lasts for decades in its required paper, while an unwritten reason survives only with those who heard it and, once they all leave, only by rediscovery. Outside the protected records, an objection's argument survives only in a paper that states it: the objector's own, or an author's paper showing the alternative or defending a declined change. *[from f:no-paper, ax:turnover, p:dissent-needs-paper, p:record-indistinct, f:show-alternatives, f:direction-implement]* {#p:reasons-unrecorded}
   1. **Lemma.** A proposal's case lasts for decades in its required paper, while an unwritten reason survives only with those who heard it and, once they all leave, only by rediscovery. Outside the protected records, an objection's argument outlasts those who heard it only in writing, such as the objector's own paper or an author's paper showing the alternative or defending a declined change. *[from f:no-paper, ax:turnover, p:dissent-needs-paper, f:show-alternatives, f:direction-implement]* {#l:reasons-in-writing weakened}
1. **Proposition.** SD-4 describes an objector who presses on after a consensus call, without new information or allowed escalation, as not accepting a group decision, whether or not anyone answered it. It says the same of a national body comment repeating a decided objection, unless the comment brings new information or the body warned all along that it would vote No. *[from f:minority-accept, f:accept-decisions, f:escalation-allowed, f:timely, f:ballot-decided, f:ballot-new-info, f:no-surprise]* {#p:repeat-objection}
1. **Proposition.** SD-4 gives an objection weight at plenary only if its holder escalated it by 5pm the evening before, on the email lists and within their national body. There, by default, it is not a national position, and after the deadline even new information goes to the email lists or a ballot comment, with a discussion paper. *[from f:plenary-weight, f:escalate-deadline, f:escalate-nb, f:late-info, f:escalate-paper]* {#p:late-objection}
1. **Proposition.** No design is considered on merit alone: a proposal needs an on-time paper and a presenter, a rival needs a paper to exist, and objections need fixed forms and times. Participants learn these rules partly by attending, some only that way, and other things equal, whoever knows them has the better chance of adoption, whatever their design skill. *[from f:late-paper, f:present-required, f:no-wait-alternatives, f:one-vote, p:late-objection, f:ballot-new-info, ax:learning, m:moves]* {#p:fluency}
1. **Proposition.** Other things equal, a newcomer, lacking what earlier meetings teach, spends more effort getting a paper considered than a returning participant, though SD-4 expects everyone to know its procedures. Where WG21 runs study groups that incubate early proposals, such as EWGI and LEWGI, a newcomer's early proposal can start there. *[from ax:learning, d:newcomer, f:familiar, f:sg-initial-proposal, d:rooms]* {#p:newcomer}

In plain terms: a proposal can advance with objections unresolved, since it needs a consensus call, not every objector's persuasion; the record SD-4 and the searched ISO clauses require need not show whether an objection was answered or why consensus was called over it. SD-4 adopts ISO's definition of consensus but names no step for judging whether an opposition is sustained, and when an objection's Against votes are too few to stop the 2:1 norm, the cheapest response keeping it met at that poll is no change or a partial change. SD-4 gives an objection weight at plenary only if its holder escalated it by 5pm the evening before, and other things equal, whoever knows its rules on papers, presenters, and objections has the better chance of adoption.
