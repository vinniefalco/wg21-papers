## 4. What the record keeps and how objections count

1. **Proposition.** A proposal can advance through WG21 with objections unresolved, since it needs consensus calls, under the 2:1 norm in the rooms and by the convenor at plenary, not every objector's persuasion. *[proved by l:address-not-resolve, p:advance-unanswered]* {#p:persuasion-not-required}
   1. **Lemma.** Under ISO, the obligation to address a sustained opposition is no obligation to resolve it, and a sustained opposition is not a veto. Still, ISO's general principles say consensus requires the resolution of substantial objections. *[from f:not-veto, f:resolve-substantial]* {#l:address-not-resolve}
   1. **Proposition.** A proposal can advance while discussed objections to it remain unresolved, because it advances on consensus calls, under the 2:1 norm in the rooms and by the convenor at plenary, and consensus need not imply unanimity. *[from l:address-not-resolve, f:design-forward, f:wording-forward, f:plenary-owns, f:two-to-one, f:consensus-def]* {#p:advance-unanswered}
1. **Proposition.** The record SD-4 and the searched ISO clauses require of a WG21 poll need not show whether an objection was answered or outvoted, or why consensus was called over it. *[proved by l:record-content, l:record-checks, l:iso-register-level]* *[from f:iso-no-reason-record]* {#p:record-indistinct}
   1. **Lemma.** SD-4 requires minutes and lets poll questions and numeric results be quoted publicly, but requires no record of how an objection was answered or why a consensus call was made. Plenary may ask a subgroup chair to summarize the reason for a decision. *[from f:minutes, f:protected, f:no-reason-record, f:plenary-reason]* {#l:record-content}
   1. **Lemma.** ISO lets participants comment on or object to minutes before they are confirmed, and has the chair of SC22 see that points of view at SC22's meetings are summarized. It bars participants from commenting publicly on others' contributions. *[from f:minutes-objections, f:views-summarized, f:no-public-comment]* {#l:record-checks}
   1. **Lemma.** The chair of SC22 judges consensus at SC22's committee stage, and ISO 2.5.7 recommends steps for a sustained opposition, including registering it, to the leadership. It does not say whether a convenor applies them inside a working group, and under either reading SD-4 adds no step for assessing or registering an opposition in subgroup polls. *[proved by f:iso-no-convenor]* *[from f:chair-judges, f:pmember-consensus, f:committee-stage, f:iso-register, f:leadership-judges, f:sustained-def, f:no-sustained-procedure, d:national-body]* {#l:iso-register-level}
      1. Clause 2.5.7 of ISO/IEC Directives Part 1 does not mention a convenor (ISO/IEC Directives Part 1, 2.5.7; searched: convenor, convener). {#f:iso-no-convenor}
1. **Proposition.** SD-4 adopts ISO's definition of consensus, marked by the absence of sustained opposition, but names no step for judging or registering one, nor criteria for an important part of the concerned interests. Whoever ISO's leadership is for a working group, SD-4's own steps for an objection include the 2:1 norm, Against voters' reasons on request, escalation, and plenary's 3-way poll and national-position question. *[proved by f:consensus-source]* *[from f:wg-consensus, f:consensus-def, f:sustained-def, f:two-to-one, f:against-reasons, f:escalate-deadline, f:plenary-consent, f:plenary-nb, f:iso-register, f:no-sustained-procedure]* {#p:definition-without-procedure}
   1. **Fact.** "Consensus is defined as follows in the ISO/IEC Guide 2:2004, as quoted in the ISO/IEC Directives:" (SD-4, "Consensus") {#f:consensus-source}
1. **Lemma.** Under SD-4's definition, an objection prevents consensus as a sustained opposition only if it concerns a substantial issue, is maintained, and comes from an important part of the concerned interests. The leadership judges that part case by case, none of the three conditions asks whether the objection is right, and the process must seek to reconcile conflicting arguments. *[proved by l:leadership-assesses]* *[from f:consensus-def, f:sustained-def, f:majority-listen, f:views-heard]* {#l:consensus-test}
   1. **Lemma.** ISO has the committee leadership determine the concerned interests case by case and assess whether an opposition has been sustained by an important part of them. *[from f:concerned-interests, f:iso-register, f:leadership-judges]* {#l:leadership-assesses}
1. **Proposition.** When an objection's Against votes are too few to stop the 2:1 norm, the cheapest response keeping it met at that poll is no change or a partial change. The count measures votes, not whether the objectors are right, and SD-4 expects any change the room directs to be made or defended with new information. *[proved by l:change-cost, l:study-count]* *[from ax:prefer, ax:redesign-cost, ax:review-cost, l:vote-count, f:direction-implement]* {#p:least-cost-response}
   1. **Lemma.** A change to a design costs its authors the time to write a revised paper, and revising the design throughout costs them more than a change confined to one part. *[from f:no-paper, ax:review-cost, ax:redesign-cost]* {#l:change-cost}
   1. **Lemma.** Studying whether objectors are right takes time, while the 2:1 comparison counts only how many vote Favor and how many vote Against. *[from ax:review-cost, l:vote-count]* {#l:study-count}
1. **Proposition.** SD-4 asks an Against voter to be ready to name the changes that would win them over, or to say that none would. Concerns stated as changes give authors items to grant one at a time, while opposition in any form offers none and counts in the poll only as a vote. *[from f:against-reasons, m:moves-author]* {#p:change-list}
1. **Proposition.** Of minutes, non-public email lists, records of subgroup discussion, ballot comments, and papers, only a paper puts an objection's argument outside the password-protected materials without anyone else's help. Writing one takes time. *[proved by l:records-protected, l:minutes-others, l:comment-needs-caucus, l:paper-unprotected]* *[from p:record-indistinct, f:protected, f:minutes-objections, f:comments-national, f:late-info, f:escalate-paper, m:moves-regular, ax:review-cost]* {#p:dissent-needs-paper}
   1. **Lemma.** SD-4 keeps meeting records, meeting wikis, and non-public email lists password-protected, and allows quoting from them publicly only poll questions, numeric results, and words a person consents to have attributed. *[from f:protected]* {#l:records-protected}
   1. **Lemma.** Under ISO, the secretary or convenor sees that meeting outcomes are documented and addresses comments on the minutes, so what minutes keep of an objection's argument rests with that officer. *[from f:minutes-objections]* {#l:minutes-others}
   1. **Lemma.** A ballot comment counts as a national body position, and a participant's concern becomes one only if enough members of their national body's caucus agree. *[from f:comments-national, f:late-info]* {#l:comment-needs-caucus}
   1. **Lemma.** A regular can write a paper stating an objection's argument, and SD-4 lists no paper among the materials it keeps password-protected. *[from m:moves-regular, f:protected]* {#l:paper-unprotected}
1. **Proposition.** A proposal's case lasts for decades in its required paper, while an unwritten reason survives only in the memory of those who heard it. Outside the protected records, an objection's argument outlasts its hearers only in writing, such as the objector's paper or an author's paper showing the alternative or defending a declined change. *[proved by l:case-lasts, l:reason-fades, l:argument-writing]* *[from f:no-paper, ax:turnover, p:dissent-needs-paper, p:record-indistinct, f:show-alternatives, f:direction-implement]* {#p:reasons-unrecorded}
   1. **Lemma.** Every significant proposal needs a paper, and a published paper stays available to later readers for decades. *[from f:no-paper, ax:turnover]* {#l:case-lasts}
   1. **Lemma.** An unwritten reason reaches someone absent only through those present who remember it. Once all of them have stopped attending, a later participant can voice it only by rediscovering it. *[from ax:turnover]* {#l:reason-fades}
   1. **Lemma.** Once its hearers have left, an objection's argument survives outside the protected records only in writing. That writing includes the objector's own paper and an author's paper showing the alternatives considered or defending a declined change. *[proved by inf:writing-only, inf:objector-paper, inf:author-paper]* *[from ax:turnover, p:dissent-needs-paper, m:moves-regular, f:show-alternatives, f:direction-implement]* {#l:argument-writing}
      1. Someone absent learns what was said only from writing or from those present who remember it, so once all the hearers have left, only writing remains. *[from ax:turnover]* {#inf:writing-only}
      1. Outside the protected records, a paper is the writing that states an objection's argument without anyone else's help, and a regular can write one. *[from p:dissent-needs-paper, m:moves-regular]* {#inf:objector-paper}
      1. An author's paper can also state it, since SD-4 asks a good paper to show the alternatives considered and an author declining a directed change to defend that. *[from f:show-alternatives, f:direction-implement]* {#inf:author-paper}
1. **Proposition.** SD-4 describes an objector who presses on after a consensus call, without new information or allowed escalation, as not accepting a group decision, whether or not anyone answered it. It says the same of a ballot comment repeating an objection decided where its national body was present and heard, unless the comment brings new information or the body warned all along that it would vote No. *[from f:minority-accept, f:accept-decisions, f:escalation-allowed, f:timely, f:ballot-decided, f:ballot-new-info, f:no-surprise]* {#p:repeat-objection}
1. **Proposition.** SD-4 gives an objection weight at plenary only if its holder escalated it by 5pm the evening before, on the email lists and within their national body. There, by default, it is not a national position, and after the deadline even new information goes to the email lists or a ballot comment, with a discussion paper. *[from f:plenary-weight, f:escalate-deadline, f:escalate-nb, f:late-info, f:escalate-paper]* {#p:late-objection}
1. **Proposition.** Every design needs more than merit to be considered: a proposal needs an on-time paper and a presenter, a rival needs a paper to exist, and objections need fixed forms and times. Participants learn how the committee applies these rules partly by attending, some of it only that way, and other things equal, whoever knows more has the better chance of adoption, whatever their design skill. *[proved by l:paper-and-presenter, l:rival-needs-paper, l:objection-forms, l:rules-by-attending]* *[from f:late-paper, f:present-required, f:no-wait-alternatives, f:one-vote, p:late-objection, f:ballot-new-info, ax:learning, m:moves, f:late-documents]* {#p:fluency}
   1. **Lemma.** A proposal is considered at a meeting only with an on-time paper and an author or another qualified and prepared presenter to present it. *[from f:late-paper, f:present-required]* {#l:paper-and-presenter}
   1. **Lemma.** A competing approach without a paper does not exist for SD-4 and does not block a proposal before the room. *[from f:no-wait-alternatives]* {#l:rival-needs-paper}
   1. **Lemma.** An objection counts in a subgroup poll only as a vote cast in the room, and gets weight at plenary only if escalated by the deadline. On a ballot, new information behind a decided objection needs an on-time paper. *[from f:one-vote, p:late-objection, f:ballot-new-info]* {#l:objection-forms}
   1. **Lemma.** Participants learn these rules partly by attending, some only that way, and other things equal, those who know more have a better chance of adoption, whatever their design skill. *[from ax:learning, m:moves]* {#l:rules-by-attending}
1. **Proposition.** Other things equal, a newcomer, lacking what earlier meetings teach, spends more effort getting a paper considered than a returning participant, though SD-4 expects everyone to know its procedures. Where a study group incubates proposals on its topic, a newcomer's early proposal can start there. *[from ax:learning, d:newcomer, f:familiar, f:sg-initial-proposal, d:rooms]* {#p:newcomer}

In plain terms: a proposal can advance with objections unresolved, and the record SD-4 and the searched ISO clauses require of a poll need not show whether an objection was answered or why consensus was called over it. When objectors are too few to stop the 2:1 norm, the cheapest response that keeps it met at that poll is no change or a partial one, and outside the protected records an objection's argument outlasts its hearers only in writing. Objections count only in fixed forms and at fixed times, and other things equal, whoever knows these rules, some of which only attending teaches, has the better chance of adoption.
