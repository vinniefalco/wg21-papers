## 8. Chairs, the convenor, and plenary

1. **Proposition.** Every contested consensus call has a cost: declaring consensus over Against votes risks later national body opposition, and declining delays work SD-4 says subgroup polls should favor. Departing from SD-4's stated practice can cost the caller's post: chairs serve at the convenor's choice with no fixed term, and the convenor for terms SC22 may decline to renew. *[proved by l:objection-to-nb, l:advance-needs-call]* *[from f:favor-progress, f:plenary-nb, f:iso-ballot, f:escalate-nb, m:moves-nb, f:chairs-appointed, f:convenor-term, ax:prefer, d:national-body, ax:post-risk]* {#p:chair-threshold}
   1. **Lemma.** SD-4 asks a participant with a serious objection to a subgroup decision to raise it within their national body, which can oppose that decision at plenary or on a ballot. *[from f:escalate-nb, m:moves-nb, f:plenary-nb, f:iso-ballot, d:national-body]* {#l:objection-to-nb}
   1. **Lemma.** Design and wording rooms forward a proposal, and plenary adopts one, only on a consensus call, so declining the call keeps the proposal at its current stage. *[from f:design-forward, f:wording-forward, f:plenary-owns, d:rooms]* {#l:advance-needs-call}
1. **Proposition.** A chair gains from departing from the 2:1 norm at most their stake in the poll, and cannot rule out added risk to their post. So a chair whose stake is worth less than that risk does better following the norm, re-polling first if they choose. *[proved by l:call-above, l:call-below]* *[from f:two-to-one, l:vote-count, f:against-reasons, p:chair-threshold, d:follows-norm, ax:post-risk, d:stake]* {#p:chair-default}
   1. **Lemma.** On a count above 2:1 after discussion, with Neutral votes short of a majority, a design room chair whose stake is worth less than what departing can cost ranks declaring consensus above declining. *[proved by inf:call-above-norm, inf:call-above-post, inf:call-above-gain]* *[from f:two-to-one, p:chair-threshold, ax:prefer, d:does-better, d:follows-norm]* {#l:call-above}
      1. On a count above 2:1 after discussion, the 2:1 norm is met, and when Neutral votes are not a majority of those voting, declining or re-polling departs from it. *[from f:two-to-one, d:positions]* {#inf:call-above-norm}
      1. Declining or re-polling on such a count can cost the chair their post. *[from inf:call-above-norm, p:chair-threshold]* {#inf:call-above-post}
      1. What declining or re-polling can gain the chair over declaring comes from the poll's outcome, so it is at most their stake in the poll. *[from d:stake]* {#inf:call-above-gain}
   1. **Lemma.** On a count at or below 2:1 after discussion, such a chair ranks declining above declaring consensus. *[proved by inf:call-below-norm, inf:call-below-post, inf:call-below-gain]* *[from f:two-to-one, p:chair-threshold, ax:prefer, d:does-better, d:follows-norm]* {#l:call-below}
      1. On a count at or below 2:1 after discussion, the 2:1 norm is unmet, so declaring consensus departs from it, while declining or a re-poll, which SD-4 allows, does not. *[from f:two-to-one, d:positions]* {#inf:call-below-norm}
      1. Declaring consensus on such a count can cost the chair their post. *[from inf:call-below-norm, p:chair-threshold]* {#inf:call-below-post}

1. **Proposition.** Of two papers otherwise alike, a chair does better taking up first the one whose author has a record of following procedures and responding to review, which promises fewer problems. SD-4 expects that record to come from taking part: presenting, answering questions, and making the changes the room directs. *[proved by l:first-gets-time, l:review-needs-part]* *[from ax:repeat, ax:prefer, ax:hours, f:prioritization, d:does-better]* {#p:chair-confidence}
   1. **Lemma.** A meeting's hours are fixed, and SD-4 has chairs reach some papers only as time allows, so a paper taken up first can get time a later one does not. *[from ax:hours, f:prioritization]* {#l:first-gets-time}
   1. **Lemma.** SD-4 expects an author to respond to review by taking part in the design room, presenting the proposal, answering questions, and making the changes the room directs. *[from f:direction-implement]* {#l:review-needs-part}
1. **Proposition.** A participant who thinks a new proposal should not take up extended subgroup time can tell the chair privately and in advance. The chair may then time-box it and poll whether to go on, and nothing in SD-4 requires a record of the request. *[from f:private-concern, f:timebox, f:no-concern-record]* {#p:private-channel}
1. **Proposition.** At plenary, design stays with the design rooms, SD-4 gives no weight to objections not escalated in time, and all present are asked for serious design concerns. Most polls pass by unanimous consent, and when opposition is significant the convenor usually asks whether Against votes are personal or national body positions. *[proved by l:plenary-no-redesign, l:plenary-late-weight, l:plenary-concerns, l:plenary-polls]* *[from f:plenary-consent, f:plenary-no-design, p:late-objection, f:design-concerns-solicited, f:plenary-nb, p:who-decides]* {#p:plenary-ratifies}
   1. **Lemma.** Plenary decides whether to adopt the changes the design and wording rooms recommend, and does not change a design or hold extended design discussion. *[from p:who-decides, f:plenary-owns, f:plenary-no-design]* {#l:plenary-no-redesign}
   1. **Lemma.** Objections not escalated by 5pm the evening before plenary, on the email lists and within a national body, get no weight there. *[from p:late-objection]* {#l:plenary-late-weight}
   1. **Lemma.** Design room chairs present the designs approved at the meeting, asking all present for any serious design concerns the subgroups may not have found. *[from f:design-concerns-solicited]* {#l:plenary-concerns}
   1. **Lemma.** Plenary first asks whether anyone objects to unanimous consent, which most polls pass, and on significant opposition the convenor usually asks whether Against votes are personal or national body positions. *[from f:plenary-consent, f:plenary-nb]* {#l:plenary-polls}

In plain terms: every consensus call has a cost, and departing from the 2:1 norm can cost the caller's post, so a chair whose stake is worth less than what departing can cost makes the call the norm gives. Of two papers otherwise alike, a chair does better taking up first the one whose author has a record of following procedures and responding to review, a record that comes only from taking part. At plenary, design stays with the design rooms, objections not escalated in time get no weight, and most polls pass by unanimous consent.
