## 7. Study groups

1. **Lemma.** A participant does better attending a study group's session than a concurrent session when the study group's topic matters more to them. *[proved by l:sg-topic-weight]* *[from ax:parallel-rooms, d:does-better]* {#l:sg-attendance}
   1. **Lemma.** Other things equal, attending a session whose topic matters more to a participant brings them more of what they value than attending one whose topic matters less. *[from ax:prefer, d:stake]* {#l:sg-topic-weight}
1. **Proposition.** On a topic with a study group, a contest between competing designs is first polled there, among the participants who attend it, and the design that leaves the group enters the design room as the initial proposal the room starts from, where the design room, which owns selecting designs, can still poll a rival that has a paper. *[proved by l:sg-purpose, l:sg-start]* *[from f:vehicle, f:multiple-polls]* {#p:sg-decides}
   1. **Lemma.** SD-4 has the convenor form a study group, at a design room chair's recommendation, when a topic draws broad interest, has no unified initial proposal, and has a strong candidate to chair the group, and gives the study group the role of producing an initial proposal that can then enter the design room. *[proved by inf:sg-formed, inf:sg-incubates]* {#l:sg-purpose}
      1. SD-4 says the convenor forms a study group at a design room chair's recommendation. It does so when a topic draws broad interest, lacks a unified initial proposal, and has a strong candidate to chair the group. *[from f:sg-formation]* {#inf:sg-formed}
      1. SD-4 says study groups own incubating to produce an initial proposal that can then enter the design room's usual processing. *[from f:sg-initial-proposal]* {#inf:sg-incubates}
   1. **Lemma.** The design room starts from the study group's initial proposal, which a rival without a paper does not block while it is an active proposal making progress, and the study group's polls are cast by the participants who attend it. *[proved by inf:sg-enters, inf:sg-no-block, inf:sg-voters]* {#l:sg-start}
      1. The initial proposal a study group produces can then enter the design room's usual processing, so the design room starts from that proposal. *[from l:sg-purpose]* {#inf:sg-enters}
      1. A rival without a paper does not block an active proposal making progress, though a design room chair may delay the proposal one meeting for an objector to bring one. *[from f:no-wait-alternatives]* {#inf:sg-no-block}
      1. Each person in a study group's room can cast one vote in its polls, so those polls are cast by the participants who attend it. *[from p:who-votes]* {#inf:sg-voters}
1. **Proposition.** When each of two groups casts at least one third of the Favor-plus-Against votes on the other's design as Against, neither design meets the 2:1 norm, and under that norm the deadlock lasts until voters change position, a design changes until enough Against voters accept it, or the voters present change. A chair may still make a consensus call below the norm, and the design room may send both designs to competing Technical Specifications. *[proved by l:deadlock-lasts, l:deadlock-call-below-norm, l:deadlock-competing-ts]* *[from l:one-third-against]* {#p:deadlock}
   1. **Lemma.** Under the 2:1 norm, a deadlock lasts until voters change position, a design changes until enough Against voters accept it, or the voters present change. *[proved by inf:deadlock-count-inputs, inf:deadlock-count-change]* {#l:deadlock-lasts}
      1. Whether a design meets the 2:1 norm in a poll depends only on who votes and which position each voter takes on the design as polled. *[from f:two-to-one, p:who-votes, d:positions]* {#inf:deadlock-count-inputs}
      1. So the count on a design changes only if the voters present change or some voters take a different position, either on the same design or on a changed one. *[from inf:deadlock-count-inputs]* {#inf:deadlock-count-change}
   1. **Lemma.** SD-4 says a proposal normally advances when it meets the 2:1 norm, and it has the subgroup chair determine whether the design room has consensus. So a design room chair may still make a consensus call below the norm. *[from f:two-to-one, f:design-forward, p:chair-decides]* {#l:deadlock-call-below-norm}
   1. **Lemma.** The design rooms own selecting ship vehicles, and ISO allows competing Technical Specifications offering different technical solutions where they do not conflict with existing International Standards. So the design room may send both designs to competing Technical Specifications. *[from f:vehicle, f:competing-ts]* {#l:deadlock-competing-ts}
1. **Proposition.** A design changed to end a deadlock changes in a way enough of its Against voters accept, for example by adding elements they want or removing elements they oppose. *[from p:deadlock]* {#p:design-changes}
   1. **Proposition.** A design changed to end a deadlock, while the voters present stay the same and only its Against voters change position, changes in a way enough of them accept. Such a change can, for example, add elements they want or remove elements they oppose. *[proved by inf:design-change-against, inf:design-change-named]* {#p:design-changes-held weakened}
      1. In a deadlock, the group opposing a design casts at least one third of its Favor-plus-Against votes as Against, which keeps it from meeting the 2:1 norm. While the voters present and every other voter's position stay the same, it meets the norm only once enough of those Against voters stop voting Against it. *[from p:deadlock, l:one-third-against]* {#inf:design-change-against}
      1. SD-4 asks an Against voter to be prepared to name the changes that would change their position. Such a change can add an element they want or remove one they oppose. *[from p:change-list]* {#inf:design-change-named}
1. **Proposition.** In a deadlock lasting many meetings, members of a group whose costs sponsors pay bear less personal cost per meeting than members of a group paying their own way. *[proved by l:deadlock-attend]* *[from ax:cost, p:money-vs-presence]* {#p:attrition}
   1. **Lemma.** A deadlock lasts across meetings only while enough members of each group keep attending and voting, since a change in the voters present can end it. *[from p:deadlock, p:who-votes]* {#l:deadlock-attend}
1. **Proposition.** SD-4 ties a study group's purpose to producing a unified initial proposal, and a deadlock produces none. *[proved by l:sg-unified-purpose, l:deadlock-not-unified]* {#p:sg-unified}
   1. **Lemma.** Under SD-4, a study group is formed when its topic lacks a unified initial proposal and exists to produce an initial proposal, so its purpose is producing a unified one. *[from l:sg-purpose]* {#l:sg-unified-purpose}
   1. **Lemma.** While a deadlock lasts, its two designs remain competing and neither meets the 2:1 norm, so it produces no unified initial proposal. *[from p:deadlock, f:sg-formation]* {#l:deadlock-not-unified}
1. **Proposition.** A participant attending one room cannot raise a concern in a concurrent room. *[proved by l:concern-in-room]* *[from ax:parallel-rooms]* {#p:concern-presence}
   1. **Lemma.** A concern raised in a subgroup's session comes from someone attending it, as do the votes cast in its polls. *[from p:who-votes]* {#l:concern-in-room}
1. **Lemma.** When one deadlocked group yields, the other holds, and no one else votes Against the holder's design, the deadlock ends with that design advancing. The holder gains its whole stake, the yielder none of its own, and neither bears the cost of holding through a deadlock. *[from p:deadlock, l:one-third-against, d:stake]* {#l:hold-yield-holder-gains}
1. **Lemma.** When both groups hold and the deadlock ends with either design equally likely, each can expect half its stake on average, less its cost of holding through it. Members whose costs sponsors pay bear less of that cost per meeting. *[from p:deadlock, p:attrition, l:deadlock-attend, ax:cost, d:stake]* {#l:hold-yield-both-hold}
1. **Lemma.** Each group does better holding while the other yields, gaining its whole stake at no cost of holding, than when both yield to a compromise worth half its stake. *[from l:hold-yield-holder-gains, ax:prefer, d:does-better]* {#l:hold-yield-whole-over-half}
1. **Lemma.** Each group does better when both yield to a compromise worth half its stake than when both hold. A deadlock both hold brings it half its stake on average, less a cost of holding. *[from l:hold-yield-both-hold, ax:cost, ax:prefer, d:does-better]* {#l:hold-yield-half-over-deadlock}
1. **Lemma.** Each group does better when both yield to a compromise worth half its stake than when it yields while the other holds, which brings none of its stake. *[from l:hold-yield-holder-gains, ax:prefer, d:does-better]* {#l:hold-yield-half-over-none}
1. **Lemma.** A group whose cost of holding is less than half its stake does better holding through a deadlock both hold than yielding while the other holds. Half its stake on average, less that cost, leaves it better off than gaining none of its stake. *[from l:hold-yield-both-hold, l:hold-yield-holder-gains, ax:prefer, d:does-better]* {#l:hold-yield-cheap-holds}
1. **Lemma.** A group whose cost of holding is more than half its stake does better yielding while the other holds than holding through a deadlock both hold. Half its stake on average, less that cost, leaves it worse off than gaining none of its stake. *[from l:hold-yield-both-hold, l:hold-yield-holder-gains, ax:prefer, d:does-better]* {#l:hold-yield-costly-yields}

In plain terms: on a topic with a study group, competing designs are first polled among the participants who attend it, and the design room starts from the design that leaves the group, though it can still poll a rival that has a paper. When each of two groups casts at least one third of the Favor-plus-Against votes on the other's design as Against, neither design meets the 2:1 norm, and under that norm the deadlock produces no unified initial proposal and lasts until voters change position, a design changes, or the voters present change. Holding through such a deadlock costs members whose sponsors pay their costs less per meeting than members paying their own way, and a group whose cost of holding is less than half its stake does better holding than yielding to a group that holds, while a group whose cost is more than half does better yielding.
