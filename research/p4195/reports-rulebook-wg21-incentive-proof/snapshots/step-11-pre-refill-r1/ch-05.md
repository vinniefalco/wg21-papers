1. **Proposition.** Within WG21's rooms and plenary, the rules let a proposal advance while objections to it remain unresolved, since advancing needs a consensus call and not the persuasion of every objector. *[proved by l:address-not-resolve, p:advance-unanswered]* {#p:persuasion-not-required}
   1. **Lemma.** Under ISO, the obligation to address a sustained opposition is not an obligation to resolve it, and a sustained opposition is not a veto, though ISO's general principles say consensus requires the resolution of substantial objections. *[proved by inf:try-not-resolve, inf:not-veto-right, inf:principle-resolve]* {#l:address-not-resolve}
      1. ISO 2.5.7 has the leadership try in good faith to resolve a sustained opposition it finds. It says the obligation to address such oppositions does not imply an obligation to resolve them successfully. *[from f:not-veto]* {#inf:try-not-resolve}
      1. ISO 2.5.7 says a sustained opposition is not akin to a right to veto. *[from f:not-veto]* {#inf:not-veto-right}
      1. ISO's clause 0.7 calls consensus an essential procedural principle and says it requires the resolution of substantial objections. *[from f:resolve-substantial]* {#inf:principle-resolve}
   1. **Proposition.** A proposal can advance while discussed objections to it remain unresolved, because advancing requires a consensus call, which the 2:1 norm normally supports, and consensus need not imply unanimity. *[proved by inf:norm-normally, inf:still-against, inf:call-not-unanimity, inf:iso-procedure-allows]* {#p:advance-unanswered}

      1. Under ISO a sustained opposition is not a veto and need not be resolved successfully, so ISO's procedure for one does not require its resolution before the work goes on. *[from l:address-not-resolve]* {#inf:iso-procedure-allows}

1. **Proposition.** SD-4 gives no weight at plenary to an objection not escalated by the deadline, including one that brings new information, and sends such information to the email lists or to a national body comment with a discussion paper. *[proved by l:no-weight, l:night-before, l:late-route]* {#p:late-objection}

In plain terms: the record that SD-4 and the searched ISO clauses require of a WG21 poll need not show whether an objection was answered or outvoted, and SD-4 names no step for assessing whether an opposition is sustained or for registering one. A proposal can advance while objections to it remain unresolved, and unless the room has directed a change, the least costly response that keeps the 2:1 norm met is a change confined to part of the design, or no change. Of minutes, non-public email lists, meeting records, ballot comments, and papers, a paper is the only one outside the protected materials that an objection's holder can produce alone, and SD-4 gives no weight at plenary to an objection not escalated by the deadline.
