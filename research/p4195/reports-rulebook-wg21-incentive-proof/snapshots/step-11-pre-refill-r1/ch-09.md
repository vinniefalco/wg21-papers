1. **Proposition.** At ballot, a national body votes on the whole document, SD-4 asks that No mean the body does not want the work at all, and ISO forbids a Yes that is conditional on changes, so a national body cannot vote against one feature without voting against the whole document. *[proved by l:fdis-one-document, l:no-means-whole, l:yes-not-conditional, l:abstain-not-counted]* *[from m:moves-nb]* {#p:bundling}
   1. **Lemma.** ISO decides whether a final draft International Standard is approved by counting the votes cast on it, and refers the whole document back to the committee if it is not. *[from f:iso-ballot, f:failed-fdis]* {#l:fdis-one-document}
   1. **Lemma.** SD-4 asks that a national body vote No only when it does not want the work to progress in any form, since enough No votes block every part of it. *[from f:ballot-no, f:no-means-no]* {#l:no-means-whole}
   1. **Lemma.** ISO requires a national body that finds a final draft unacceptable to vote No with technical comments, and forbids a Yes vote conditional on the acceptance of modifications. *[from f:no-conditional-yes]* {#l:yes-not-conditional}
   1. **Lemma.** ISO excludes abstentions when it counts the votes on a final draft, so a national body's abstention counts neither for nor against the draft. *[from f:iso-ballot]* {#l:abstain-not-counted}
1. **Proposition.** Before adoption, a proposal normally advances once it meets the 2:1 norm; after adoption, a paper or national body comment proposing its removal normally advances once it meets the same norm; after a successful final ballot, a design change is expected to have near-unanimous consent, and no technical change is accepted at the approval stage; once published, SD-4 says the feature is much more difficult to update with breaking changes. *[proved by l:advance-before-adoption, l:removal-is-proposal, l:removal-same-norm, l:change-after-final-ballot, l:published-harder]* {#p:burden-flip}
   1. **Lemma.** Before adoption, a proposal must be forwarded by subgroups, where SD-4's 2:1 norm says it normally advances when Favor votes are more than twice the Against votes. *[from p:path, f:two-to-one, d:positions]* {#l:advance-before-adoption}
   1. **Lemma.** After adoption, removing a design is a reversal and a design change, which SD-4 says needs a paper. A national body can also propose the removal in a ballot comment, which SD-4 expects to contain an actionable proposed resolution. *[from d:reversal, f:no-paper, f:comment-content]* {#l:removal-is-proposal}
   1. **Lemma.** A paper proposing a removal, or a ballot comment's proposed resolution for one, is a proposal, so in a subgroup poll it normally advances once it meets the 2:1 norm. *[from l:removal-is-proposal, f:two-to-one, d:positions]* {#l:removal-same-norm}
   1. **Lemma.** After a successful final approval ballot, SD-4 expects any design change made before publication to have near-unanimous consent in subgroups and in plenary. ISO accepts no technical change at the approval stage. *[from f:near-unanimous, f:late-changes]* {#l:change-after-final-ballot}
   1. **Lemma.** SD-4 says a feature, once in an IS, is much more difficult to update with breaking changes. *[from f:is-hard-to-change]* {#l:published-harder}
1. **Proposition.** Because WG21 plans each edition for a set time, an unfinished feature need not delay the features that are ready. *[proved by l:edition-time-set, l:plenary-each-change, l:edition-need-not-wait]* {#p:schedule-protects}
   1. **Lemma.** WG21 plans a new edition every three years, so each edition's planned time is set by the schedule and not by when any one feature is finished. *[from ax:schedule, d:edition]* {#l:edition-time-set}
   1. **Lemma.** Plenary decides separately whether to adopt each proposed change that the subgroups recommend, so a feature they have recommended can be adopted while another is unfinished. *[from f:plenary-owns]* {#l:plenary-each-change}
   1. **Lemma.** An edition need not wait for a feature not approved by its feature cutoff, since that feature waits for a later edition. *[from p:miss-cost]* {#l:edition-need-not-wait}

In plain terms: a proposal not approved by an edition's feature cutoff, including one delayed there for a rival without a paper or stopped there by an Against vote, waits at least for the next edition, planned three years later, though it can be published sooner as a white paper. An author whose design could reach the IS without a TS does better seeking the IS unless they value the TS's experience for users above its delay, and at ballot a national body cannot vote against one feature without voting against the whole document. Once a design is adopted, a proposal to remove it normally advances under the same 2:1 norm, a design change after a successful final ballot is expected to have near-unanimous consent, and because each edition is planned for a set time, an unfinished feature need not delay the features that are ready.
