## 1. Starting points

### Axioms

1. **Axiom.** Attending a WG21 meeting in person costs money and time. {#ax:cost}
1. **Axiom.** A sponsor can pay a participant's travel and salary, but cannot attend, speak, or vote in the participant's place. {#ax:who-pays}
1. **Axiom.** A participant who attends most meetings expects to meet many of the same colleagues at future meetings. A chair expects fewer problems from advancing a paper whose author has a record of following the procedures and responding to review. {#ax:repeat}
1. **Axiom.** Other things equal, each participant, sponsor, national body, and user prefers whatever brings it more of what it values, sooner and at less cost to it, weighing gains against losses on one scale of its own. For this proof, a participant values adoption of the proposals they support, the interests of their sponsor if they have one, their standing, keeping any chair or convenor post they hold, their time and money, and a good result for users; a sponsor values its interests, which can depend on particular features of a design; a national body values the outcomes its national position supports, the interests concerned at its national level where it has no position yet, and its members' time; and a user values the features they use or would use, how well those features work, and their own time and money. {#ax:prefer}
1. **Axiom.** A participant who votes Against a colleague's proposal cannot rule out that the colleague will support the participant's own proposals less readily. A Favor vote gives the colleague no reason for this, and a Neutral vote or none gives no more reason than an Against vote does. {#ax:support}
1. **Axiom.** A chair cannot rule out that a consensus call not following the 2:1 norm, or polling that departs from what SD-4 encourages, puts their post at more risk than following them. The convenor likewise cannot rule this out for a call departing from SD-4's plenary procedure. {#ax:post-risk}
1. **Axiom.** No participant can study every paper closely, so each chooses which papers to study, and what a participant learns from a paper or its discussion can change their vote. {#ax:attention}
1. **Axiom.** Writing or reviewing a paper takes time, and, other things equal, a longer or more complex paper takes more; preparing to give reasons for a vote also takes time. {#ax:review-cost}
1. **Axiom.** Until a design is implemented and used, nobody can be sure how well it works, and even then those voting on it cannot fully judge how well it serves users. A design with more parts and more interactions with existing wording has more places where a defect can go unnoticed. {#ax:unknown}
1. **Axiom.** When a proposal is first presented, it has received less committee review than at any later meeting. When a design is presented in parts, reviewers of an early part cannot study in detail what the later parts will require of it until those parts are presented. {#ax:first-review}
1. **Axiom.** Reversing a decision, or changing a design after work is built on it, means redoing or discarding that work, which costs those who built it and takes meeting time from other papers. After a favorable poll, authors and others build more on the design, such as wording, implementations, and proposals that depend on it. {#ax:rework}
1. **Axiom.** Revising a design throughout costs its authors more work than a change confined to one part of it. {#ax:redesign-cost}
1. **Axiom.** A participant learns how the committee works partly by attending, some of it only that way, and this knowledge differs from skill at design. Participants can use only procedural options they know, and, other things equal, those who know more spend less effort getting a paper considered and have a better chance of adoption. {#ax:learning}
1. **Axiom.** Over the years, some participants stop attending and others join, while a published paper stays available to later readers for decades. Someone absent from a meeting learns what was said there only from writing or from those present who remember it, and a clear written objection is easier to raise again. {#ax:turnover}
1. **Axiom.** A meeting has a fixed number of hours, so time given to one paper is not available to another. {#ax:hours}
1. **Axiom.** WG21 plans a new edition of the C++ standard every three years, each with the features approved by its cutoff, and ISO publishes each edition once national bodies approve it. {#ax:schedule}
1. **Axiom.** When several subgroups meet at the same time, a participant can attend only one of them. {#ax:parallel-rooms}
1. **Axiom.** A design in the standard applies to every user of its feature, so a better design's gain is spread across them all, while participating costs the participant or their sponsor. Users differ from each other and from participants in the features they care about and what they can spend taking part, and one user's effort seldom changes a committee outcome. {#ax:users-affected}

### Definitions

1. **Definition.** A participant is a registered expert attending a WG21 meeting. Guests a convenor invites are not participants. {#d:participant}
1. **Definition.** A regular is a participant who expects to attend future meetings with many of the same colleagues. {#d:regular}
1. **Definition.** A newcomer is a participant attending for the first time; a newcomer who expects to attend future meetings with many of the same colleagues is also a regular. {#d:newcomer}
1. **Definition.** An author is a participant who presents a proposal paper as one of its authors. {#d:author}
1. **Definition.** A sponsor is an organization that pays some or all of a participant's costs of taking part. {#d:sponsor}
1. **Definition.** A user is a person who writes C++ and does not participate. {#d:user}
1. **Definition.** A design room is EWG or LEWG, a study group is any WG21 study group, a wording room is CWG or LWG, and a subgroup is any of these. Plenary is a WG21 meeting's closing plenary session, a chair chairs a subgroup, the convenor convenes WG21, and a consensus call is a chair's or the convenor's determination of consensus. {#d:rooms}
1. **Definition.** A national body is an ISO member body that is a P-member of SC22, the committee WG21 belongs to. In quotations from the Directives, "the committee" is SC22, "the Chair" its chair, and "National Body" any ISO member body, which on enquiry and final ballots includes non-P-members of SC22. {#d:national-body}
1. **Definition.** In this proof, the rules are SD-4 and the clauses of ISO/IEC Directives Part 1 that chapter 1 quotes, in the edition that replaces the JTC 1 Supplement. A claim that the rules require or ask for nothing of a kind covers SD-4 whole and those clauses. {#d:rules}
1. **Definition.** Favor, Neutral, and Against are the poll positions as SD-4 capitalizes them, with Strongly Favor and Weakly Favor counting as Favor and likewise for Against. The 2:1 norm is SD-4's statement that a proposal normally advances when, after discussion of Against voters' concerns, its Favor votes in a subgroup poll exceed twice its Against votes. {#d:positions}
1. **Definition.** With Neutral votes short of a majority of those voting, a chair follows the 2:1 norm by declaring consensus on a count above 2:1 after discussion and any re-poll, and on no other count. In this proof, what advances in a subgroup, and what Against votes block there, is what happens under a chair who follows the norm. {#d:follows-norm}
1. **Definition.** A participant's standing is the readiness of colleagues to support the participant's future proposals. {#d:standing}
1. **Definition.** A player's stake in a poll is the difference the poll's outcome makes to what the player values. {#d:stake}
1. **Definition.** A player does better with one option than another when it is worth more to them, by their weighing of the values a statement names, and does best with an option when no other is worth more. Every value a statement does not name is held equal. {#d:does-better}
1. **Definition.** A direction poll is a poll on whether to continue work on a design, and a forwarding poll is a poll to send a proposal to the next room. {#d:polls}
1. **Definition.** An incumbent on a topic is a design that has passed a direction poll in a design room and has not failed a later direction poll there. A rival is a design on the same topic that is not an incumbent. {#d:incumbent}
1. **Definition.** An edition is one published version of the C++ standard, and its feature cutoff is the last meeting at which a new feature can be approved for it. {#d:edition}
1. **Definition.** A reversal is the removal or replacement of a design or decision that WG21 or one of its subgroups has approved. {#d:reversal}
1. **Definition.** A player is a person or body whose choices the proof considers. A settled pattern, which game theory calls a Nash equilibrium, is a set of choices, one per player, in which no player gains by changing only their own choice. {#d:settled-pattern}
1. **Definition.** A small proposal is a proposal by one author who pays their own costs and expects its design to be reviewed within one meeting. {#d:small}
1. **Definition.** A large proposal is a proposal whose two or more authors have their costs paid by one or more sponsors and expect its review to span more than one edition. Its paper is longer and more complex than the paper of any small proposal. {#d:large}
1. **Definition.** A result-seeking author is an author whose gain from a proposal depends on how well the adopted design serves its users, not on whether it keeps any particular feature. {#d:result-seeking}
1. **Definition.** A design-bound author is one whose gain from a proposal depends on the adopted design keeping particular features fixed before review, for example because a sponsor's interests depend on them. {#d:design-bound}
