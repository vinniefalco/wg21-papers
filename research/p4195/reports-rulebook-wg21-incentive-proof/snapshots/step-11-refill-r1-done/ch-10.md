## 10. The schedule and national bodies

1. **Proposition.** A proposal that misses an edition's feature cutoff waits at least for the next edition, planned three years later. It can ship sooner as a white paper, which has no prescribed schedule, and the same schedule keeps an unfinished feature from delaying the features that are ready. *[proved by l:cutoff]* *[from f:white-paper-process, f:plenary-owns, ax:schedule, d:edition]* {#p:miss-cost}
   1. **Lemma.** Once an edition's draft goes to ballot, SD-4 treats a ballot comment that requests adding a feature as out of scope, and WG21 plans the next edition three years later. *[from f:ballot-no-new-features, d:edition, ax:schedule]* {#l:cutoff}
1. **Proposition.** At the feature cutoff, a one-meeting delay for a paperless rival, or an Against vote keeping a proposal below the 2:1 line, sends it to the next planned edition. *[proved by l:cutoff-next]* *[from p:miss-cost, f:no-wait-alternatives, l:vote-count, f:plenary-owns, d:edition]* {#p:cutoff-effect}
   1. **Lemma.** At the feature cutoff, a one-meeting delay for a paperless rival sends a proposal to the next planned edition. An Against vote keeping it below the 2:1 line does the same unless the chair calls consensus below the norm. *[proved by inf:cutoff-must-advance, inf:cutoff-delay, inf:cutoff-below-line]* *[from p:miss-cost]* {#l:cutoff-next weakened}
      1. A proposal in a subgroup at an edition's feature cutoff can be approved for that edition only if it advances at that meeting, since plenary adopts only what subgroups recommend. *[from d:edition, f:plenary-owns]* {#inf:cutoff-must-advance}
      1. A design room chair who delays a proposal one meeting for a paperless rival at the feature cutoff keeps it from advancing at that meeting. *[from f:no-wait-alternatives]* {#inf:cutoff-delay}
      1. A proposal whose count stays below the 2:1 line at a meeting advances there only if the chair calls consensus below the norm, since advancing needs a consensus call. *[from p:persuasion-not-required, f:design-forward, f:wording-forward, d:positions]* {#inf:cutoff-below-line}
1. **Proposition.** A TS delays IS adoption by about one to two years, and its experience can bring incompatible changes first: a gain to result-seeking authors, a risk to design-bound ones. The design room can still require a TS, and ISO and SC22 now recommend the lighter white paper. *[from f:ts-lag, f:ts-experience, f:ts-feedback, f:is-hard-to-change, f:vehicle, f:white-paper, f:white-paper-process, l:implementation-informs, d:result-seeking, d:design-bound, ax:prefer]* {#p:prefer-is}
1. **Lemma.** Every national body may vote on enquiry and final ballots, which pass when two thirds of the P-members' votes are positive and at most a quarter of all votes negative, not counting abstentions or negative votes without technical comments. ISO encourages a body without national consensus or expert input to abstain, sustained opposition does not apply to these votes, and a final draft that fails goes back to SC22. *[from f:all-nbs-vote, f:enquiry-count, f:iso-ballot, f:no-conditional-yes, f:nb-abstain, f:not-ballots, f:failed-fdis, d:national-body]* {#l:ballot-rules}
1. **Proposition.** A national body's No opposes every feature in the document at once, including ones its own experts support, while WG21 considers its comments whatever its vote. A body valuing the rest of a document above blocking one feature does better voting Yes with comments, and one wanting the work in no form does better voting No. *[proved by l:rank-rest-yes, l:rank-rest-abstain, l:rank-oppose-no, l:rank-oppose-abstain]* *[from p:who-decides, f:experts-personal, f:no-means-no, f:respond-comments, f:comments-noted, l:ballot-rules, ax:prefer, d:does-better]* {#p:nb-comment}
   1. **Lemma.** A body that values the rest of a document above blocking any one feature ranks Yes with comments above abstaining. That holds because only the Yes counts toward the approval it prefers, and its comments are considered either way. *[from l:ballot-rules, f:no-means-no, ax:prefer]* {#l:rank-rest-yes}
   1. **Lemma.** Such a body ranks abstaining above No, since only the No counts against the approval it prefers and its comments gain nothing from a No. *[from l:ballot-rules, f:no-means-no, ax:prefer]* {#l:rank-rest-abstain}
   1. **Lemma.** A body that wants the work in no form ranks No above abstaining, since only the No counts against approval, which is what SD-4 says No is for. *[from l:ballot-rules, f:ballot-no, ax:prefer]* {#l:rank-oppose-no}
   1. **Lemma.** Such a body ranks abstaining above Yes with comments, since only the Yes counts toward approval of work it wants in no form. *[from l:ballot-rules, f:ballot-no, ax:prefer]* {#l:rank-oppose-abstain}

1. **Proposition.** At ballot, only a national body that values blocking one feature above the whole rest of the document does better voting No over it. SD-4 expects such a body to have told the committee all along, and ISO asks every national body to make its standpoint clear early. *[from p:nb-comment, f:ballot-no, f:no-surprise, f:timely, f:iso-early, ax:prefer, d:does-better]* {#p:credible-threat}
1. **Proposition.** A national body forms its position by its panel's own rules, which SD-4 does not state, so whether a sponsor's paid participants add weight there depends on those rules. In the United States, INCITS gives a voting member's representatives one vote, apart from up to four Defense Department votes, and bars consultants a voting member funds from voting membership. *[proved by l:incits-votes]* *[from f:late-info, f:escalate-nb, ax:who-pays]* {#p:nb-members}
   1. **Lemma.** INCITS, whose Executive Board approves the United States position that goes to ANSI, gives each organization one voting membership, apart from up to four Department of Defense votes on technical committees, admits a consultant as a voting member only if no voting member funds it, and lets the representatives of standards developing organizations and consortia be employed by other voting members. A United States delegation may also decide a new issue at an international plenary by caucus consensus. *[from f:incits-one-vote, f:incits-organization, f:incits-limits, f:incits-position, f:incits-caucus]* {#l:incits-votes}

In plain terms: a proposal that misses an edition's feature cutoff waits at least for the next edition, planned three years later, and at the cutoff a one-meeting delay for a paperless rival is enough to send it there. A TS delays IS adoption by about one to two years, and the incompatible changes its experience can bring are a gain to result-seeking authors and a risk to design-bound ones. At ballot, a national body that values the rest of a document above blocking one feature does better voting Yes with comments, and only one that values blocking it above the whole rest does better voting No.
