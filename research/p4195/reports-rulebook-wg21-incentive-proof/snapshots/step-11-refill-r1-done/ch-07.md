## 7. Study groups and deadlocks

1. **Proposition.** On a topic with a study group, rival designs are first polled there, among those who chose that room over the rooms meeting with it. The design it sends on is where the design room starts, and the design room, which owns selecting designs, can still poll a rival that has a paper. *[proved by l:sg-purpose, l:sg-attendance, l:sg-start]* *[from f:vehicle, f:multiple-polls]* {#p:sg-decides}
   1. **Lemma.** SD-4 has the convenor form a study group on a design room chair's recommendation when a topic has broad interest, no unified initial proposal, and a strong chair candidate. It gives the study group the role of producing an initial proposal that can then enter the design room. *[from f:sg-formation, f:sg-initial-proposal]* {#l:sg-purpose}
   1. **Lemma.** A participant can attend only one of the rooms meeting at the same time, and votes and speaks only there. So a study group's voters are those for whom its topic outweighs the rooms meeting with it. *[from ax:parallel-rooms, ax:prefer, d:stake, f:one-vote]* {#l:sg-attendance}
   1. **Lemma.** The design room starts from the study group's initial proposal, which a rival without a paper does not block while it is an active proposal making progress. *[from l:sg-purpose, l:sg-attendance, f:no-wait-alternatives, f:sg-initial-proposal]* {#l:sg-start}
1. **Proposition.** Paying participants to attend a study group puts a sponsor's voters where the design the design room starts from is chosen. In a room with fewer voters, fewer paid participants make up a third of the Favor-plus-Against votes, enough to block, or over two thirds, enough to meet the norm. *[proved by l:two-thirds-favor, l:sg-paid-share]* *[from p:sg-decides, p:sponsor-votes]* {#p:sg-sponsor}
   1. **Lemma.** Any group casting over two thirds of the Favor-plus-Against votes as Favor ensures the 2:1 norm is met, since Against votes are then fewer than half the Favor votes. *[from f:two-to-one, d:positions]* {#l:two-thirds-favor}
   1. **Lemma.** In a room where the other voters cast fewer Favor-plus-Against votes, fewer paid participants make up a third of all such votes, enough to block. Fewer of them also make up over two thirds, enough to meet the norm. *[proved by inf:sg-paid-third, inf:sg-paid-two-thirds]* *[from l:one-third-against, l:two-thirds-favor]* {#l:sg-paid-share weakened}
      1. Paid participants voting Favor or Against make up at least a third of all such votes once they number at least half the other voters' Favor-plus-Against votes. *[from f:one-vote, d:positions]* {#inf:sg-paid-third}
      1. They make up over two thirds once they number more than twice the other voters' Favor-plus-Against votes. *[from f:one-vote, d:positions]* {#inf:sg-paid-two-thirds}
1. **Proposition.** When each of two groups casts at least a third of the Favor-plus-Against votes against the other's design, neither design meets the 2:1 norm. The deadlock lasts until voters change position, a design changes, the voters present change, a chair calls consensus below the norm, or the design room sends both to competing TSs. *[proved by l:deadlock-repeat, l:deadlock-call, l:deadlock-ts]* *[from l:vote-count]* {#p:deadlock}
   1. **Lemma.** While the voters present, their positions, and both designs stay the same, every later poll on either design again has at least a third of its Favor-plus-Against votes cast Against. *[from f:one-vote, d:positions]* {#l:deadlock-repeat}
   1. **Lemma.** Since chairs send designs on only by consensus calls, a design whose count falls below the 2:1 norm advances only if the chair calls consensus below the norm. *[from p:who-decides, m:moves-chair, f:design-forward, f:two-to-one]* {#l:deadlock-call}
   1. **Lemma.** The design room, which owns the choice of ship vehicle, can send both designs to competing TSs, which ISO allows when they do not conflict with an existing International Standard. *[from f:vehicle, f:competing-ts]* {#l:deadlock-ts}
1. **Proposition.** In a long deadlock, a group whose sponsors pay its costs bears less per meeting than a group paying its own way. In the case the table below describes, the first holds and the second yields, whichever design is better. *[proved by l:hold-yield-holder-gains, l:hold-yield-both-hold, l:hold-yield-whole-over-half, l:hold-yield-half-over-deadlock, l:hold-yield-half-over-none, l:hold-yield-cheap-holds, l:hold-yield-costly-yields]* *[from ax:who-pays, ax:cost, p:deadlock]* {#p:attrition}
   1. **Lemma.** When one deadlocked group yields, the other holds, and no one else votes Against the holder's design, the deadlock ends with that design advancing. The holder gains its whole stake, the yielder none of its own, and neither bears the cost of holding through a deadlock. *[proved by inf:holder-no-against, inf:holder-advances, inf:holder-stakes, inf:holder-no-cost]* {#l:hold-yield-holder-gains}
      1. With no one voting Against the holder's design, the yielder included, that design's Favor votes, the holder's among them, are more than twice its Against votes, which number none. *[from f:two-to-one, d:positions]* {#inf:holder-no-against}
      1. Meeting the 2:1 norm, under which chairs call consensus, the holder's design advances, and the deadlock ends. *[from inf:holder-no-against, p:who-decides, p:deadlock]* {#inf:holder-advances}
      1. The holder's design advancing brings the holder its whole stake in that design and brings the yielder none of its stake in its own. *[from inf:holder-advances, d:stake]* {#inf:holder-stakes}
      1. With the deadlock over, neither group needs to attend more meetings to hold through it, so neither bears that cost in money and time. *[from inf:holder-advances, ax:cost]* {#inf:holder-no-cost}
   1. **Lemma.** When both groups hold and the deadlock ends with either design equally likely, each can expect half its stake on average, less its cost of holding through it. Members whose costs sponsors pay bear less of that cost per meeting. *[proved by inf:both-hold-cost, inf:both-hold-half, inf:both-hold-sponsor]* {#l:hold-yield-both-hold}
      1. Holding through the deadlock means attending every meeting until it ends, and attending costs money and time. *[from p:deadlock, ax:cost]* {#inf:both-hold-cost}
      1. With either design equally likely to advance, each group gains its whole stake or none of it with equal chance, which is half its stake on average. *[from d:stake]* {#inf:both-hold-half}
      1. A sponsor can pay a participant's travel and salary, so members whose costs sponsors pay bear less of each meeting's cost than members paying their own way. *[from ax:who-pays, ax:cost, d:sponsor]* {#inf:both-hold-sponsor}
   1. **Lemma.** Each group does better holding while the other yields, gaining its whole stake at no cost of holding, than when both yield to a compromise worth half its stake. *[from l:hold-yield-holder-gains, ax:prefer, d:does-better]* {#l:hold-yield-whole-over-half}
   1. **Lemma.** Each group does better when both yield to a compromise worth half its stake than when both hold. That holds because a deadlock both hold brings half its stake on average, less a cost of holding. *[from l:hold-yield-both-hold, ax:cost, ax:prefer, d:does-better]* {#l:hold-yield-half-over-deadlock}
   1. **Lemma.** Each group does better when both yield to a compromise worth half its stake than when it yields while the other holds, which brings none of its stake. *[from l:hold-yield-holder-gains, ax:prefer, d:does-better]* {#l:hold-yield-half-over-none}
   1. **Lemma.** A group whose cost of holding is less than half its stake does better holding through a deadlock both hold than yielding while the other holds. That holds because half its stake on average, less that cost, leaves it better off than none of its stake. *[from l:hold-yield-both-hold, l:hold-yield-holder-gains, ax:prefer, d:does-better]* {#l:hold-yield-cheap-holds}
   1. **Lemma.** A group whose cost of holding is more than half its stake does better yielding while the other holds than holding through a deadlock both hold. That holds because half its stake on average, less that cost, leaves it worse off than none of its stake. *[from l:hold-yield-both-hold, l:hold-yield-holder-gains, ax:prefer, d:does-better]* {#l:hold-yield-costly-yields}

A deadlock both hold ends with either design equally likely, and both yielding means a compromise worth half its stake to each. First number: group A's rank. Second number: group B's rank. 4 is best. † marks a Pareto-optimal cell.* {#t:hold-or-yield}

In plain terms: on a topic with a study group, rival designs are first polled among those who chose that room over the rooms meeting with it, the design the study group sends on is where the design room starts, and the fewer Favor-plus-Against votes a room's other voters cast, the fewer paid participants it takes to block a design or to meet the norm. When each of two groups casts at least a third of the Favor-plus-Against votes against the other's design, neither design meets the 2:1 norm, and the deadlock lasts until voters change position, a design changes, the voters present change, a chair calls consensus below the norm, or the design room sends both to competing TSs. In a long deadlock, a group whose sponsors pay its costs bears less per meeting than a group paying its own way, and in the case the table describes, the first holds and the second yields, whichever design is better.
