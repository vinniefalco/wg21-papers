## 8. Chairs and the convenor

1. **Lemma.** Subgroup chairs are appointed by the convenor with no fixed term, and the convenor is appointed by SC22 for terms of up to three years, renewable without limit after a call for candidates. *[from f:chairs-appointed, f:convenor-term, d:national-body]* {#l:appointments}
1. **Lemma.** SD-4 makes the subgroup chair the judge of consensus and assigns no one in WG21 the role of ruling which side of a technical dispute is correct. *[from f:design-forward, f:no-merit-ruling]* {#l:chair-role}
1. **Proposition.** A chair who declares consensus while some participants vote Against risks national body opposition later, and a chair who declines delays work that SD-4 says subgroup polls should favor. *[proved by l:declare-risks, l:decline-delays]* {#p:chair-costs}
   1. **Lemma.** A chair who declares consensus while some participants vote Against cannot rule out that national bodies oppose the proposal later, in plenary or at ballot. *[from f:escalate-nb, m:moves-nb]* {#l:declare-risks}
   1. **Lemma.** A chair who declines to declare consensus keeps the proposal from advancing on that poll, while SD-4 says subgroup polls should favor progress. *[from p:chair-decides, f:favor-progress]* {#l:decline-delays}
1. **Proposition.** When, after discussion of the Against voters' concerns, more than twice as many vote Favor as Against, declaring consensus follows SD-4's norm and declining departs from it, though SD-4 says a Neutral majority can be concerning. *[proved by l:declare-above]* *[from l:neutral-outside]* {#p:chair-default}
   1. **Lemma.** On such a count, SD-4 says the proposal normally advances, and declaring consensus lets it advance on that count while declining keeps it from advancing. *[from f:two-to-one, p:chair-decides]* {#l:declare-above}
1. **Proposition.** When Favor votes are not more than twice the Against votes, SD-4 allows a re-poll after discussion of the Against voters' concerns, and only Against voters are named for questions in that discussion. *[from f:two-to-one, l:reasons-against]* {#p:repoll}
1. **Proposition.** A chair or the convenor does better departing from SD-4's procedure for a consensus call, such as by declaring consensus below the 2:1 norm or declining above it, only when their stake in the proposal is worth more to them than what the departure can cost them, which can include the post they hold by appointment, with no fixed term for a chair and for renewable terms for the convenor. *[proved by l:declare-below, l:departure-gain, l:departure-weigh, l:post-at-risk]* *[from p:chair-default, l:appointments]* {#p:chair-threshold}
   1. **Lemma.** Declaring consensus on a count not above 2:1 after discussion advances a proposal that has not met the 2:1 norm, and so departs from the norm. *[from d:positions, p:chair-decides]* {#l:declare-below}
   1. **Lemma.** The effect of a departure on the outcome of a consensus call is worth to the chair or the convenor no more than their stake in the proposal. *[from d:stake, p:chair-decides]* {#l:departure-gain}
   1. **Lemma.** A chair or the convenor does better departing from SD-4's procedure only when the departure's effect on the outcome is worth more to them than what it can cost them. When that effect is worth less to them than that cost, they do better following the procedure. *[from ax:prefer, d:does-better]* {#l:departure-weigh}
   1. **Lemma.** What a departure can cost a chair or the convenor can include the post they hold by appointment. *[proved by inf:chair-post, inf:convenor-post, inf:post-open]* *[from ax:prefer]* {#l:post-at-risk}
      1. Since a chair is appointed by the convenor to match the subgroup's current needs, with no fixed term, whether a chair keeps the post depends on the convenor's choice. *[from f:chairs-appointed, l:appointments]* {#inf:chair-post}
      1. Since SC22 appoints the convenor for terms of up to three years, renewed after a call for candidates, whether the convenor keeps the post depends on SC22's choice. *[from l:appointments]* {#inf:convenor-post}
      1. So neither a chair nor the convenor can rule out that a departure from SD-4's procedure affects whether they keep the post. *[from inf:chair-post, inf:convenor-post]* {#inf:post-open}
1. **Proposition.** Neither SD-4 nor ISO/IEC Directives Part 1 clauses 1.8.2, 2.5.7, and 5.5 require the record to say why a chair declared consensus while some participants voted Against, though plenary may ask the subgroup chair to summarize the reason, and recording reasons takes meeting time. *[proved by l:sd4-call-reason, l:iso-call-reason, l:reasons-hours]* *[from l:record-content]* {#p:chair-no-reasons}
   1. **Lemma.** SD-4 does not require the record to show why a consensus call was made, including a call declaring consensus while some participants voted Against. *[from f:no-reason-record]* {#l:sd4-call-reason}
   1. **Lemma.** ISO/IEC Directives Part 1 clauses 1.8.2, 2.5.7, and 5.5 do not require a record of why consensus was judged to exist, including when some participants voted Against. *[from f:iso-no-reason-record]* {#l:iso-call-reason}
   1. **Lemma.** Recording the reasons for a consensus call at a meeting uses some of its fixed hours, which are then not available to another paper. *[from ax:hours]* {#l:reasons-hours}
1. **Proposition.** SD-4 asks a participant who thinks a new proposal should not take up extended subgroup time to tell the chair privately and in advance, the chair may then time-box it after a representative overview and poll whether the subgroup wants to discuss its details further, and SD-4 does not require a record of the request. *[from f:private-concern, f:timebox, f:no-concern-record]* {#p:private-channel}
1. **Proposition.** Plenary does not redesign, gives no weight to an objection not escalated in time, solicits serious design concerns about designs approved at that meeting, and, when opposition is significant, has the convenor usually ask whether the Against votes are personal or national body positions. *[from p:design-in-rooms, f:plenary-no-design, f:plenary-weight, f:design-concerns-solicited, f:plenary-consent, f:plenary-nb]* {#p:plenary-ratifies}
1. **Proposition.** A wording room refers design questions back to the design room, so the design room decides them. *[from f:wording-refer, f:wording-forward]* {#p:wording-rooms}
1. **Lemma.** On a count above 2:1 after discussion, with Neutral votes not a majority of those voting, declaring consensus follows SD-4's norm, while declining or re-polling departs from it. A design room chair whose stake is worth less to them than what departing can cost them does better declaring consensus on such a count than declining or re-polling. *[proved by l:above-repoll]* *[from p:chair-default, l:departure-gain, l:departure-weigh]* {#l:call-above}
   1. **Lemma.** Like declining, a re-poll in place of a consensus call on a count above 2:1 after discussion keeps the proposal from advancing where SD-4 says it normally would. *[from l:declare-above, p:chair-decides]* {#l:above-repoll}
1. **Lemma.** On a count not above 2:1 after discussion, declaring consensus departs from SD-4's norm, while declining or re-polling does not. A design room chair whose stake is worth less to them than what departing can cost them does better declining or re-polling on such a count than declaring consensus. *[proved by l:below-follow]* *[from l:declare-below, l:departure-gain, l:departure-weigh]* {#l:call-below}
   1. **Lemma.** On such a count, declining keeps a proposal that has not met the 2:1 norm from advancing, and SD-4 allows a re-poll, so neither departs from the norm. *[from d:positions, p:repoll]* {#l:below-follow}

Columns: the count after discussion of the Against voters' concerns. Each cell: the chair's rank of the call. 2 is best within each column.* {#t:chair-call}

| | Above 2:1 | Not above 2:1 |
|---|---|---|
| **Declare consensus** | **2** | 1 |

*[from l:call-above, l:call-below]*

In plain terms: the convenor appoints subgroup chairs with no fixed term, SC22 appoints the convenor for renewable terms, and SD-4 makes the chair the judge of consensus and assigns no one the role of ruling which side of a technical dispute is correct. A chair or the convenor does better departing from SD-4's procedure for a consensus call only when their stake in the proposal is worth more to them than what the departure can cost them, which can include their post. Neither SD-4 nor ISO/IEC Directives Part 1 clauses 1.8.2, 2.5.7, and 5.5 require the record to say why a chair declared consensus while some participants voted Against, SD-4 does not require a record of a concern raised privately with a chair, and plenary does not redesign or give weight to an objection not escalated in time.
