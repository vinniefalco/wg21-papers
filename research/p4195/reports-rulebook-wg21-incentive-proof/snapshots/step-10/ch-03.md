## 3. What each player can do

1. **Proposition.** Each player has these moves under the rules. *[proved by m:moves-author, m:moves-regular, m:moves-chair, m:moves-convenor, m:moves-nb, m:moves-sponsor, m:moves-user]* {#m:moves}
   1. **Lemma.** An author can submit one or more papers, including papers that each propose part of a design, present them or have another qualified and prepared presenter do so, revise them, ask the chair for a poll, which the chair may take or not, and ask the design room for a ship vehicle, which the design rooms select. *[proved by inf:author-submit, inf:author-parts, inf:author-present, inf:author-revise, inf:author-poll, inf:author-vehicle]* {#m:moves-author}
      1. An author can submit one or more papers before a meeting's pre-meeting mailing deadline, and the meeting's agenda includes every paper in the mailings since the previous meeting. *[from f:agenda-papers, f:late-paper, d:author]* {#inf:author-submit}
      1. A proposal of one part of a design is a design addition or change, and SD-4 requires a paper for each such proposal. *[from f:no-paper]* {#inf:author-parts}
      1. A proposal can be presented by one of its authors or by another qualified and prepared presenter, and a proposal with neither is not considered during official meeting time. *[from f:present-required, d:author]* {#inf:author-present}
      1. An author can revise a paper, and SD-4 expects its presenter to produce a new revision based on feedback or to pass the feedback to the author to do so. *[from f:invested, d:author]* {#inf:author-revise}
      1. An author can ask the chair for a poll, and the chair, who may take any polls they choose, decides whether to take it. *[from f:chair-polls, f:direction-implement, d:author]* {#inf:author-poll}
      1. An author can ask the design room for a ship vehicle, TS or IS, and the design rooms own selecting and recommending ship vehicles. *[from f:vehicle, f:direction-implement, d:rooms, d:author]* {#inf:author-vehicle}
   1. **Lemma.** A regular can attend or not, study a paper or not, speak, vote Favor, Neutral, or Against or not vote, write a paper, raise a concern privately with a chair, and ask their national body to take a national position. *[proved by inf:regular-attend, inf:regular-study, inf:regular-speak, inf:regular-vote, inf:regular-paper, inf:regular-private, inf:regular-national]* {#m:moves-regular}
      1. A regular can attend a meeting in person, which costs money and time, or not attend it. *[from ax:cost, ax:turnover, d:regular]* {#inf:regular-attend}
      1. A regular, like every participant, cannot study every paper closely and chooses which papers to study. *[from ax:attention, d:regular]* {#inf:regular-study}
      1. A regular can speak in discussion, where SD-4 welcomes both positive and negative comments that are constructive and respectful. *[from f:negative-welcome, d:regular]* {#inf:regular-speak}
      1. In a subgroup poll a regular can vote Favor, Neutral, or Against, or not vote at all. *[from p:who-votes, f:not-vote, d:positions, d:regular]* {#inf:regular-vote}
      1. A regular can write a proposal paper, which SD-4 requires for any significant proposal, or a followup paper, such as a rebuttal of an on-time paper. *[from f:no-paper, f:followup-papers, d:regular]* {#inf:regular-paper}
      1. A regular with a concern that a new proposal should not take up extended subgroup time can share it privately and in advance with the chair, as SD-4 asks. *[from f:private-concern, d:regular]* {#inf:regular-private}
      1. A regular can raise an objection within their own national body, which can then determine whether it is a national position. *[from f:escalate-nb, f:late-info, d:regular]* {#inf:regular-national}
   1. **Lemma.** A chair can choose the polls, order the agenda by SD-4's priorities, time-box a proposal and then poll whether to discuss it further, re-poll after discussion, ask Against voters for reasons, and allow limited exploration of alternatives. A design room or wording room chair also makes the consensus call and forwards a proposal, a design room chair can delay a proposal one meeting for a rival without a paper, and a study group chair takes the polls on which design leaves the group as its initial proposal. *[proved by inf:chair-polls, inf:chair-priorities, inf:chair-agenda, inf:chair-timebox, inf:chair-repoll, inf:chair-reasons, inf:chair-explore, inf:chair-forward, inf:chair-delay, inf:chair-sg-role, inf:chair-sg-polls]* {#m:moves-chair}
      1. A chair may take any polls they choose. *[from f:chair-polls, d:rooms]* {#inf:chair-polls}
      1. SD-4 has a chair first take up papers related to the direction group's priorities, work aimed at the next edition, or work that can make plenary progress at the meeting. *[from f:prioritization, f:direction-group, d:edition]* {#inf:chair-priorities}
      1. A meeting's agenda includes all papers in the mailings since the previous meeting and possibly earlier papers that, in the chair's judgment, a previous meeting did not sufficiently address. *[from f:agenda-papers]* {#inf:chair-agenda}
      1. A chair may time-box the initial discussion of a new proposal and then poll whether the subgroup is interested in discussing its details further. *[from f:timebox]* {#inf:chair-timebox}
      1. A chair can re-poll after discussion of the concerns of those voting Against, to see whether opinions have improved. *[from f:two-to-one, f:chair-polls]* {#inf:chair-repoll}
      1. A chair may ask anyone voting Against for their reasons. *[from f:against-reasons]* {#inf:chair-reasons}
      1. At early discussion phases, a chair may allow limited exploration of alternatives to an active proposal, to decide whether a second paper is warranted. *[from f:explore-alternatives]* {#inf:chair-explore}
      1. A proposal on which a design room or wording room chair declares consensus is forwarded, from a design room to the wording rooms and from a wording room to plenary. *[from p:chair-decides, f:design-forward, f:wording-forward, d:rooms]* {#inf:chair-forward}
      1. A design room chair may delay an active proposal by one meeting to let an objector bring an on-time paper for a competing approach that has none. *[from f:no-wait-alternatives, d:rooms]* {#inf:chair-delay}
      1. A study group is responsible for producing an initial proposal that can then enter the design rooms. *[from f:sg-initial-proposal, d:rooms]* {#inf:chair-sg-role}
      1. A study group chair may take any polls they choose, including polls on which design leaves the group as its initial proposal. *[from inf:chair-polls, inf:chair-sg-role, d:rooms]* {#inf:chair-sg-polls}
   1. **Lemma.** The convenor can appoint chairs, form study groups, invite a guest for up to two meetings, make plenary consensus calls, and ask whether Against votes in plenary are personal or national body positions. *[proved by inf:convenor-chairs, inf:convenor-sg, inf:convenor-guest, inf:convenor-call, inf:convenor-consent, inf:convenor-nb-ask]* {#m:moves-convenor}
      1. The convenor appoints each chair, selected to match the current needs of the subgroup, with no fixed term. *[from f:chairs-appointed, d:rooms]* {#inf:convenor-chairs}
      1. The convenor forms a study group at the recommendation of a design room chair. *[from f:sg-formation, d:rooms]* {#inf:convenor-sg}
      1. The convenor may invite a specific guest to up to two meetings to provide relevant expertise to WG21. *[from f:guests, p:who-votes]* {#inf:convenor-guest}
      1. In plenary the convenor makes the consensus call on whether to adopt each change that the design and wording rooms recommend. *[from p:chair-decides, f:plenary-owns, d:rooms]* {#inf:convenor-call}
      1. In plenary, SD-4's default is to ask whether there is objection to unanimous consent, and after an objection the convenor takes a 3-way poll. *[from f:plenary-consent, f:plenary-nb]* {#inf:convenor-consent}
      1. The convenor can ask whether Against votes in plenary are personal or national body positions, which SD-4 says the convenor usually does when opposition is significant. *[from f:plenary-nb]* {#inf:convenor-nb-ask}
   1. **Lemma.** A national body can appoint experts, vote Yes, No, or abstain on ballots, comment, state a national position in plenary, object in SC22 and JTC 1 to action taken on a topic not on the agenda, and appeal. *[proved by inf:nb-experts, inf:nb-vote, inf:nb-comment, inf:nb-plenary, inf:nb-agenda, inf:nb-appeal]* {#m:moves-nb}
      1. A national body, as a P-member of SC22, can individually appoint experts to WG21. *[from f:experts-appointed, d:national-body]* {#inf:nb-experts}
      1. Every national body may vote Yes or No, or abstain, on enquiry and final drafts. *[from f:all-nbs-vote, f:iso-ballot, f:nb-abstain, d:national-body]* {#inf:nb-vote}
      1. A national body can submit ballot comments, which ISO and WG21 treat as national body positions. *[from f:comments-national, f:comment-content]* {#inf:nb-comment}
      1. A national body can state a national position in plenary when the convenor asks whether Against votes are personal or national body positions. *[from inf:convenor-nb-ask, f:plenary-nb, f:escalate-nb]* {#inf:nb-plenary}
      1. If a WG21 meeting took action on a topic not on its agenda, a national body could formally escalate an objection in SC22 and JTC 1 to the entire meeting. *[from f:agenda-notice]* {#inf:nb-agenda}
      1. A national body has the right of appeal, within eight weeks of the decision in question. *[from f:appeal, d:national-body]* {#inf:nb-appeal}
   1. **Lemma.** A sponsor can pay for participation and choose whom it pays and how many, and a sponsor that is a category C liaison organization appoints representatives who act for it. *[proved by inf:sponsor-pays, inf:sponsor-whom, inf:sponsor-how-many, inf:sponsor-liaison]* {#m:moves-sponsor}
      1. A sponsor can pay some or all of a participant's costs of taking part, such as travel and salary. *[from d:sponsor, ax:who-pays]* {#inf:sponsor-pays}
      1. A sponsor decides which participants' costs it pays. *[from d:sponsor, ax:who-pays]* {#inf:sponsor-whom}
      1. Neither SD-4 nor the clauses of ISO/IEC Directives Part 1 that chapter 1 searched limit the number of participants whose costs one sponsor pays. *[from f:no-sponsor-cap]* {#inf:sponsor-how-many}
      1. A category C liaison organization appoints representatives to WG21 who act for it. *[from f:experts-appointed, p:sponsor-no-vote]* {#inf:sponsor-liaison}
   1. **Lemma.** A user casts no vote in WG21's polls or ballots, but can give feedback on a TS or white paper, write a paper that a qualified and prepared participant presents, raise their interests with a national body, which ISO asks to take account of all interests concerned at the national level, or become a participant. *[proved by inf:user-no-vote, inf:user-feedback, inf:user-paper, inf:user-national, inf:user-join]* {#m:moves-user}
      1. A user casts no vote in WG21's polls, and ballots are cast by national bodies, which a user is not. *[from p:who-votes, p:sponsor-no-vote, d:user, d:national-body]* {#inf:user-no-vote}
      1. A user can give feedback on a TS or white paper, which asks implementations and users for feedback to identify its shortcomings. *[from f:ts-feedback, d:user]* {#inf:user-feedback}
      1. A user can write a paper that a qualified and prepared participant presents in the user's place. *[from f:present-required, f:invested, d:user, d:participant]* {#inf:user-paper}
      1. A user can raise their interests with a national body, which ISO makes responsible for a technical standpoint that takes account of all interests concerned at the national level. *[from f:iso-early, d:national-body, d:user]* {#inf:user-national}
      1. A user can become a participant, a registered expert attending a WG21 meeting, through appointment as an expert by a national body or liaison organization. *[from d:participant, d:user, f:experts-appointed, ax:turnover]* {#inf:user-join}

In plain terms: authors can submit, present, and revise papers and ask for polls and ship vehicles, and regulars can attend, study papers, speak, vote or not, write papers, and raise concerns with a chair or their national body. Chairs choose the polls and order the agenda, design room and wording room chairs make the consensus calls that forward proposals, and the convenor appoints chairs, forms study groups, invites guests, and makes the plenary consensus call. National bodies appoint experts, vote on ballots, comment, and appeal, sponsors choose whom and how many to pay, and users, who cast no vote, can give feedback, write papers that participants present, raise their interests with a national body, or become participants.
