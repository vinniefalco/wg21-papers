## 6. Sponsors, proposal size, and parts

1. **Proposition.** A sponsor adds a voter to a room for each participant it pays to attend, and neither SD-4 nor the searched ISO clauses limit how many it pays. SC22 may cap only the experts each national body or liaison appoints, and at SC22 itself each national body counts once. *[from p:who-votes, f:no-sponsor-cap, f:one-vote, ax:who-pays, f:experts-appointed, f:registered, f:expert-cap, f:pmember-consensus]* {#p:sponsor-votes}
1. **Proposition.** Changing the features a sponsor's interests depend on costs that sponsor rework of what it built on them, lost fit with its requirements, and more review, all still to come. The gain of a better design is spread across all the feature's users, and the same 2:1 norm decides every proposal, however many users will rely on it. *[from ax:prefer, ax:rework, ax:review-cost, ax:users-affected, f:two-to-one, d:sponsor]* {#p:sponsor-features}
1. **Induction.** Each part of a design adopted in parts becomes work every later part builds on, so a rival to the whole design must replace every part already adopted. *[proved by l:parts-build, l:rival-replaces-parts]* *[from m:moves-author, ind:reversal-grows, d:reversal]* {#ind:adopted-parts}
   1. **Lemma.** An author can propose a design in papers that each propose part of it, and each later part builds on the parts already adopted. *[from m:moves-author, ax:rework]* {#l:parts-build}
   1. **Lemma.** Adopting a rival to the whole design in its place replaces every part already adopted, a reversal of each, which redoes or discards the work built on it. *[from d:reversal, ind:reversal-grows]* {#l:rival-replaces-parts}
1. **Proposition.** Presenting a design in parts lets the first parts be adopted before the whole is reviewed, and each adopted part adds to what a rival to the whole must replace. The cost is a paper and a presentation for each part. *[proved by l:part-advances-alone, l:part-adds-hurdle, l:part-cost]* *[from m:moves-author, f:design-forward, f:wording-forward, f:plenary-owns, ind:adopted-parts, p:challenger-bar, ax:review-cost, f:present-required]* {#p:parts}
   1. **Lemma.** Each part's paper advances on its own consensus calls in the design and wording rooms and at plenary, so an early part can be adopted before later parts are presented. *[from m:moves-author, f:design-forward, f:wording-forward, f:plenary-owns]* {#l:part-advances-alone}
   1. **Lemma.** Each adopted part is one more part a rival to the whole must replace, raising the advantage the rival needs to win a voter. *[proved by inf:part-replace, inf:part-voter-share]* *[from ind:adopted-parts, p:challenger-bar]* {#l:part-adds-hurdle}
      1. A rival to the whole design must replace every part already adopted, so each adopted part adds work its reversal must redo or discard. *[from ind:adopted-parts, d:reversal]* {#inf:part-replace}
      1. A rival wins a voter only with an advantage worth more than that voter's share of the reversal's cost, so a costlier reversal raises the advantage it needs. *[from p:challenger-bar, inf:part-replace]* {#inf:part-voter-share}
   1. **Lemma.** Each part needs its own paper, which takes time to write, and its own presenter at each meeting where it is considered. *[from ax:review-cost, f:present-required]* {#l:part-cost}
1. **Proposition.** Reviewers cannot study what later parts need of a first part that fixes a design's structure, SD-4 asks no paper to say, and once adopted, every rival must replace it. So a design-bound author's best first paper is the smallest part that fixes that structure and still meets the 2:1 norm against opponents who foresee the rest. *[proved by l:first-part-fix, l:first-part-foresight, l:first-part-oppose, l:first-part-smallest]* *[from ax:first-review, f:no-parts-step, ind:adopted-parts, l:vote-count]* {#p:first-part}
   1. **Lemma.** A design-bound author does better when its first part fixes the features its gain depends on, as the structure later parts build on, than when a later part fixes them. The first part is adopted sooner, and every later part built on it adds to the cost of the reversal a rival would need. *[proved by inf:fix-sooner, inf:fix-hurdle, inf:fix-rank]* *[from d:design-bound, p:parts, ind:adopted-parts, ind:reversal-grows, p:challenger-bar, ax:prefer, d:does-better]* {#l:first-part-fix}
      1. Parts advance one paper at a time, so the first part can be adopted before any later part, fixing its features sooner. *[from p:parts]* {#inf:fix-sooner}
      1. Every later part builds on the first, so each adds to the cost of the reversal that changing the first part's features would need. *[from ind:adopted-parts, ind:reversal-grows, p:challenger-bar]* {#inf:fix-hurdle}
      1. A design-bound author's gain depends on those features staying fixed, so it does better when they are fixed sooner and changing them needs a costlier reversal. *[from inf:fix-sooner, inf:fix-hurdle, d:design-bound, ax:prefer, d:does-better]* {#inf:fix-rank}
   1. **Lemma.** At the first part's polls, only those who foresee the rest can base their vote on what the later parts will need of it. Others cannot study that in detail until those parts are presented, and SD-4 asks no paper to state it. *[from ax:first-review, ax:attention, f:no-parts-step]* {#l:first-part-foresight}
   1. **Lemma.** Against votes on the first part can keep it below the 2:1 line, while removing it once adopted normally needs a paper or ballot comment meeting the norm. So an opponent who foresees the rest does better voting Against the first part than waiting to vote Against a later part. *[proved by inf:oppose-early-count, inf:oppose-late-burden, inf:oppose-early-rank]* *[from l:vote-count, p:burden-flip, ax:prefer, d:does-better]* {#l:first-part-oppose}
      1. Against votes making up at least a third of the Favor-plus-Against votes on the first part keep it from meeting the 2:1 norm. *[from l:vote-count]* {#inf:oppose-early-count}
      1. Once the first part is adopted, removing it normally needs a paper or ballot comment that itself meets the 2:1 norm. *[from p:burden-flip]* {#inf:oppose-late-burden}
      1. Blocking the first part takes a third of the Favor-plus-Against votes, while removing it later takes over two thirds, so the opponent does better voting Against early. *[from inf:oppose-early-count, inf:oppose-late-burden, l:vote-count, ax:prefer, d:does-better]* {#inf:oppose-early-rank}
   1. **Lemma.** Of two first parts that fix the same features and meet the 2:1 norm against opponents who foresee the rest, the smaller takes less time to write and to review. So a design-bound author whose gain depends on those features does better with the smaller, which can fix them sooner and at less cost. *[from ax:review-cost, ax:hours, d:design-bound, ax:prefer, d:does-better]* {#l:first-part-smallest}
1. **Proposition.** A large proposal has more sponsored authors in the room than a small one, and more meetings where a poll can make it the incumbent or end that standing. Each step it advances brings it nearer to work that can make plenary progress, which SD-4 has chairs take up first. *[from d:large, d:small, d:incumbent, p:sponsor-votes, f:prioritization, f:design-forward, f:wording-forward, f:plenary-owns]* {#p:large-position}
1. **Proposition.** A small proposal's author bears every meeting's cost alone, and outside SD-4's priority categories the proposal gets agenda time only after the priority work. Where WG21 runs study groups that incubate early proposals, such as EWGI and LEWGI, SD-4's statement that study groups own incubating applies to them. *[from d:small, ax:cost, f:prioritization, f:agenda-papers, f:sg-initial-proposal, d:rooms]* {#p:small-position}
1. **Proposition.** Each result about large or small proposals rests on an attribute their definitions name: who pays the authors' costs, the number of authors, review's expected span, or the paper's length. So it holds for any proposal with that attribute, whatever its size. *[from d:small, d:large, p:large-position, p:small-position]* {#p:size-attributes}

In plain terms: a sponsor adds a voter to a room for each participant it pays to attend, and neither SD-4 nor the searched ISO clauses limit how many it pays. A design-bound author's best first paper is the smallest part that fixes the design's structure and still meets the 2:1 norm against opponents who foresee the rest. A large proposal has more sponsored authors in the room than a small one, whose author bears every meeting's cost alone, and each such result holds for any proposal with the attribute it rests on.
