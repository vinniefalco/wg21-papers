## 3. What a vote costs

1. **Lemma.** Under the 2:1 norm each Against vote raises by two the Favor votes a proposal needs, while Neutral votes and abstentions leave the count unchanged. So one Against vote changes the result only when the count sits one or two votes above the line, and at least a third of the Favor-plus-Against votes cast Against keeps a proposal below it. *[proved by l:against-double, l:neutral-outside, l:room-size, l:one-third-against]* {#l:vote-count}
   1. **Lemma.** Under the 2:1 norm, each Against vote raises by two the number of Favor votes a proposal needs. *[from f:two-to-one, d:positions]* {#l:against-double}
   1. **Lemma.** Neutral votes and abstentions do not enter the 2:1 comparison, though SD-4 says a Neutral majority of those voting can be concerning. *[from f:two-to-one, f:not-vote, d:positions]* {#l:neutral-outside}
   1. **Lemma.** One more Against vote changes whether the 2:1 norm is met only when the Favor votes exceed twice the other Against votes by one or two. *[from f:two-to-one, d:positions]* {#l:room-size}
   1. **Lemma.** Any group casting at least one third of the Favor-plus-Against votes as Against prevents the 2:1 norm from being met. *[from f:two-to-one, d:positions]* {#l:one-third-against}
1. **Proposition.** For a regular, an Against vote on a colleague's proposal costs more than a Neutral vote or none. It risks the colleague's later support no less, and only Against voters can be asked for reasons, which take time to prepare. *[from ax:support, ax:repeat, ax:prefer, ax:review-cost, f:against-reasons, f:no-reasons-other, d:regular, d:standing]* {#p:against-costs-more}
1. **Proposition.** A regular's doubt about a colleague's proposal enters the 2:1 count only if it is worth the extra cost of an Against vote. That vote changes the result only when the count sits one or two votes above the line, though it can also win changes to the design. *[proved by l:vote-below, l:vote-above]* *[from l:vote-count, p:against-costs-more, ax:prefer, f:against-reasons, f:direction-implement, d:stake, d:does-better]* {#p:doubt-outside-count}

   1. **Lemma.** When an Against vote is worth more to the regular than its extra cost, the regular ranks it above a Neutral vote or none. *[from p:against-costs-more, ax:prefer, d:does-better]* {#l:vote-above}

## 4. What the record keeps and how objections count

1. **Proposition.** In WG21's rooms and plenary, a proposal can advance while objections to it stay unresolved: it needs a consensus call, which the 2:1 norm normally supports, not the persuasion of every objector. *[proved by l:address-not-resolve, p:advance-unanswered]* {#p:persuasion-not-required}
   1. **Lemma.** Under ISO, the obligation to address a sustained opposition is not an obligation to resolve it, and a sustained opposition is not a veto, though ISO's general principles say consensus requires the resolution of substantial objections. *[from f:not-veto, f:resolve-substantial]* {#l:address-not-resolve}
   1. **Proposition.** A proposal can advance while discussed objections to it remain unresolved, because advancing requires a consensus call, which the 2:1 norm normally supports, and consensus need not imply unanimity. *[from l:address-not-resolve, f:design-forward, f:wording-forward, f:plenary-owns, f:two-to-one, f:consensus-def]* {#p:advance-unanswered}
1. **Proposition.** The record that SD-4 and the searched ISO clauses require of a WG21 poll need not show whether an objection was answered or outvoted, or why consensus was called over it. *[proved by l:record-content, l:record-checks, l:iso-register-level]* *[from f:iso-no-reason-record]* {#p:record-indistinct}
   1. **Lemma.** SD-4 requires minutes and permits public quotation of poll questions and numeric results, requires no record of how an objection was answered or why a consensus call was made, and lets plenary ask a subgroup chair to summarize the reason for a decision. *[from f:minutes, f:protected, f:no-reason-record, f:plenary-reason]* {#l:record-content}
   1. **Lemma.** ISO lets participants comment on or object to minutes before they are confirmed, has the chair of SC22 see that points of view at SC22's meetings are summarized, and bars participants from commenting publicly on others' contributions. *[from f:minutes-objections, f:views-summarized, f:no-public-comment]* {#l:record-checks}
   1. **Lemma.** At SC22's committee stage the chair of SC22 judges consensus; ISO 2.5.7 recommends steps for a sustained opposition, including registering it, and gives them to the leadership without saying whether a working group convenor applies them inside the group; and under either reading SD-4 adds no step for assessing or registering an opposition in subgroup polls. *[from f:chair-judges, f:pmember-consensus, f:committee-stage, f:iso-register, f:leadership-judges, f:sustained-def, f:no-sustained-procedure, d:national-body]* {#l:iso-register-level}
1. **Proposition.** SD-4 adopts ISO's definition of consensus, which turns on sustained opposition, but names no step for judging whether an opposition is sustained or for registering one, and no criteria for an important part of the concerned interests. Whoever ISO's leadership is for a working group, SD-4's own steps are the 2:1 norm after discussion, reasons from Against voters on request, escalation, and plenary's 3-way poll and question about national positions. *[from f:wg-consensus, f:consensus-def, f:sustained-def, f:two-to-one, f:against-reasons, f:escalate-deadline, f:plenary-consent, f:plenary-nb, f:iso-register, f:no-sustained-procedure]* {#p:definition-without-procedure}
1. **Lemma.** Under the definition SD-4 adopts, an objection prevents consensus only if it concerns a substantial issue, is maintained, and comes from an important part of the concerned interests, as the leadership judges case by case. None of the three asks whether the objection is right, and every view must still be heard. *[from f:consensus-def, f:sustained-def, f:concerned-interests, f:majority-listen, f:views-heard]* {#l:consensus-test}
1. **Proposition.** When an objection's Against votes are too few to stop the 2:1 norm, the cheapest response at that poll that keeps the norm met is no change, or a change to one part. Finding out whether those objectors are right leaves that count unchanged, and SD-4 expects any change the room directs to be made or defended with new information. *[from ax:prefer, ax:redesign-cost, ax:review-cost, l:vote-count, f:direction-implement]* {#p:least-cost-response}
1. **Proposition.** SD-4 asks an Against voter to be ready to name the changes that would win them over, or to say that none would. Concerns stated as changes give the author items to grant one at a time, while opposition in any form offers nothing to grant and weighs in the poll only as a vote. *[from f:against-reasons, m:moves-author]* {#p:change-list}
1. **Proposition.** Of minutes, non-public email lists, meeting records, ballot comments, and papers, only a paper puts an objection's argument outside the password-protected materials without anyone else's help, and writing one takes time. *[from p:record-indistinct, f:protected, f:minutes-objections, f:comments-national, f:late-info, f:escalate-paper, m:moves-regular, ax:review-cost]* {#p:dissent-needs-paper}
1. **Proposition.** A proposal's case lasts for decades in its required paper, while an objection's argument lasts outside the protected records only in a paper its holder writes. A reason no written record holds survives only with those who heard it, and once they have all stopped attending, a later participant can voice it only by thinking of it anew. *[from f:no-paper, ax:turnover, p:dissent-needs-paper, p:record-indistinct]* {#p:reasons-unrecorded}
1. **Proposition.** After a consensus call, an objector who presses on without new information and outside the escalation SD-4 allows is, in SD-4's terms, not accepting a group decision, whether or not anyone answered the objection. SD-4 says the same of a national body comment repeating a decided objection, unless it brings new information or the body warned all along that it would vote No. *[from f:minority-accept, f:accept-decisions, f:escalation-allowed, f:timely, f:ballot-decided, f:ballot-new-info, f:no-surprise]* {#p:repeat-objection}
1. **Proposition.** SD-4 gives an objection weight at plenary only if its holder escalated it by 5pm the evening before, on the email lists and within their national body, where by default it is not a national position. After that deadline, even new information goes to the email lists or a national body ballot comment, with a discussion paper. *[from f:plenary-weight, f:escalate-deadline, f:escalate-nb, f:late-info, f:escalate-paper]* {#p:late-objection}
1. **Proposition.** No design is considered on merit alone: a proposal needs an on-time paper and a presenter, a rival without a paper does not exist, and objections count only in set forms and times. Participants learn these rules partly by attending, some only that way, and, other things equal, the one who knows them has the better chance of adoption, whatever their skill at design. *[from f:late-paper, f:present-required, f:no-wait-alternatives, f:one-vote, p:late-objection, f:ballot-new-info, ax:learning, m:moves]* {#p:fluency}
1. **Proposition.** Other things equal, a newcomer, lacking the learning earlier meetings give, spends more effort getting a paper considered than a participant who has attended before, though SD-4 expects everyone to know its procedures. Where WG21 runs study groups that incubate early proposals, such as EWGI and LEWGI, a newcomer's early proposal can start there. *[from ax:learning, d:newcomer, f:familiar, f:sg-initial-proposal, d:rooms]* {#p:newcomer}

## 5. Momentum: incumbents and the cost of reversal

1. **Induction.** Each decision built on a design adds work that reversing the design would redo or discard, and more is built after each favorable poll. So the cost of reversing a design grows as it advances, whether or not the design is wrong. *[from ax:rework, ax:prefer, d:reversal, f:design-forward, f:wording-forward, f:plenary-owns]* {#ind:reversal-grows}
1. **Proposition.** A design that passes a direction poll becomes the incumbent, and a rival without a paper cannot block it; the chair can at most delay it one meeting for one to arrive. A rival with a paper can be polled alongside it, the room can direct changes to it, and at a TS merge the default is to go ahead with it unless an alternative is actively progressing. *[from f:no-paper, f:no-wait-alternatives, f:explore-alternatives, f:direction-implement, f:multiple-polls, f:ts-default, d:incumbent, d:polls]* {#p:incumbent-start}
1. **Proposition.** A design can become the incumbent at its first direction poll, when it has had less review than at any later meeting. From then on, every rival and every later change to it bears a reversal cost that grows as more is built on it. *[from ax:first-review, ax:unknown, d:incumbent, d:polls, p:incumbent-start, ind:reversal-grows, d:reversal]* {#p:early-polls}
1. **Proposition.** A rival wins a voter from an incumbent only with an advantage worth more to that voter than their share of the reversal's cost. That hurdle grows with each decision built on the incumbent, which faces no such hurdle itself. *[from ax:prefer, ind:reversal-grows, p:incumbent-start, d:incumbent, d:reversal, d:does-better]* {#p:challenger-bar}
1. **Lemma.** A direction poll asks voters only to agree that work should continue, less than a forwarding poll asks, so it can pass where a forwarding poll would fail. Yet passing it makes the design the incumbent. *[from d:polls, d:incumbent, f:design-forward, ax:prefer, p:early-polls]* {#l:poll-wording}
1. **Proposition.** If a poll places a proposal among the work the committee aims to include in the next IS, the proposal joins a category SD-4 has chairs take up first. A meeting's hours are fixed, so the time it then gets comes out of the time left for papers outside those categories. *[from f:prioritization, ax:hours]* {#p:roadmap-poll}
1. **Proposition.** A longer, more complex design costs more to study and has more places where a defect can go unnoticed. A reviewer whose stake does not grow with a design's size does better giving it less close study than a smaller design with the same stake, so the design with more places for defects gets less study from that reviewer. *[from ax:review-cost, ax:attention, ax:unknown, ax:prefer, d:stake, d:does-better]* {#p:review-share}
1. **Proposition.** Participants who do not study a proposal typically do not vote on it, so one that costs more to study than most participants' stakes in it is voted on mainly by those with large stakes. Unless those of them who object cast at least a third of the Favor-plus-Against votes, it can advance with no change at all. *[from f:not-vote, p:review-share, ax:review-cost, ax:prefer, l:vote-count, p:least-cost-response]* {#p:light-review}
1. **Proposition.** Before adoption a design normally needs the 2:1 norm to advance, and after adoption a paper or ballot comment removing it normally needs the same norm, so the default flips to keeping the design. After a successful final ballot a change is expected to have near-unanimous consent, ISO allows no technical change at the approval stage, and SD-4 calls a published feature much more difficult to update with breaking changes. *[from f:two-to-one, f:no-paper, f:comment-content, f:near-unanimous, f:late-changes, f:is-hard-to-change, d:reversal]* {#p:burden-flip}
1. **Proposition.** An objector who arrives after a design has been adopted faces three obstacles at once. Removal needs a paper meeting the 2:1 norm, the cost of reversal has grown with each decision built on the design, and the record keeps the design's case but unwritten objections only in memory. *[from p:burden-flip, ind:reversal-grows, p:challenger-bar, p:reasons-unrecorded, p:late-objection]* {#p:late-arrival}

## 6. Sponsors, proposal size, and parts

1. **Proposition.** A sponsor adds a voter to a room for each participant it pays to attend, and neither SD-4 nor the searched ISO clauses limit how many it pays. SC22 may cap only the experts each national body or liaison appoints, and at SC22 itself each national body counts once. *[from p:who-votes, f:no-sponsor-cap, f:one-vote, ax:who-pays, f:experts-appointed, f:registered, f:expert-cap, f:pmember-consensus]* {#p:sponsor-votes}
1. **Proposition.** A change to the features a sponsor's interests depend on costs that sponsor rework of what it has built on them, lost fit with its own requirements, and more review, all still to come. The gain of a better design is spread across all the feature's users, and the same 2:1 norm decides every proposal, however many users will rely on it. *[from ax:prefer, ax:rework, ax:review-cost, ax:users-affected, f:two-to-one, d:sponsor]* {#p:sponsor-features}
1. **Induction.** Each part of a design adopted in parts becomes work every later part builds on, so a rival to the whole design must replace every part already adopted. *[from m:moves-author, ind:reversal-grows, d:reversal]* {#ind:adopted-parts}
1. **Proposition.** Presenting a design as a sequence of papers lets its first parts be adopted before the whole has been reviewed, and each part adopted adds to what a rival to the whole must replace. The cost is a paper and a presentation for each part. *[from m:moves-author, f:design-forward, f:wording-forward, f:plenary-owns, ind:adopted-parts, p:challenger-bar, ax:review-cost, f:present-required]* {#p:parts}
1. **Proposition.** Reviewers of a first part that fixes a design's structure cannot study what the later parts will need of it, SD-4 asks no paper to say, and once the part is adopted every rival must replace it. So a design-bound author's best first paper is the smallest part that fixes that structure and still meets the 2:1 norm against opponents who foresee the rest. *[from ax:first-review, ax:attention, ax:prefer, d:stake, f:no-parts-step, p:parts, ind:adopted-parts, p:challenger-bar, l:vote-count, d:design-bound]* {#p:first-part}
1. **Proposition.** Compared with a small proposal, a large one has more authors in the room with their costs paid and more meetings in which a poll can make it the incumbent or end its standing as one. Each step it advances brings it nearer to work that can make plenary progress, which SD-4 has chairs take up first. *[from d:large, d:small, d:incumbent, p:sponsor-votes, f:prioritization, f:design-forward, f:wording-forward, f:plenary-owns]* {#p:large-position}
1. **Proposition.** A small proposal's author bears every meeting's cost alone, and outside SD-4's priority categories the proposal gets agenda time only after the priority work. Where WG21 runs study groups that incubate early proposals, such as EWGI and LEWGI, SD-4's statement that study groups own incubating applies to them. *[from d:small, ax:cost, f:prioritization, f:agenda-papers, f:sg-initial-proposal, d:rooms]* {#p:small-position}
1. **Proposition.** Each result about large or small proposals rests on an attribute their definitions name: who pays the authors' costs, how many authors there are, how long review is expected to last, or the paper's length. So it holds for any proposal with that attribute, whatever its size. *[from d:small, d:large, p:large-position, p:small-position]* {#p:size-attributes}

## 7. Study groups and deadlocks

1. **Proposition.** On a topic with a study group, rival designs are first polled among those who chose that room over the rooms meeting with it, and the design it sends on is where the design room starts. The design room, which owns selecting designs, can still poll a rival that has a paper. *[proved by l:sg-purpose, l:sg-attendance, l:sg-start]* *[from f:vehicle, f:multiple-polls]* {#p:sg-decides}
   1. **Lemma.** SD-4 has the convenor form a study group, at a design room chair's recommendation, when a topic draws broad interest, has no unified initial proposal, and has a strong candidate to chair the group, and gives the study group the role of producing an initial proposal that can then enter the design room. *[from f:sg-formation, f:sg-initial-proposal]* {#l:sg-purpose}
   1. **Lemma.** A participant can attend only one of the rooms meeting at the same time, and votes and speaks only there. So a study group's voters are those for whom its topic outweighs the rooms meeting with it. *[from ax:parallel-rooms, ax:prefer, d:stake, f:one-vote]* {#l:sg-attendance}
   1. **Lemma.** The design room starts from the study group's initial proposal, which a rival without a paper does not block while it is an active proposal making progress. *[from l:sg-purpose, l:sg-attendance, f:no-wait-alternatives, f:sg-initial-proposal]* {#l:sg-start}
1. **Proposition.** Paying participants to attend a study group puts a sponsor's voters where the design the design room starts from is chosen. The fewer voters a room has, the fewer paid participants it takes to cast a third of its Favor-plus-Against votes, enough to block a design, or over two thirds, enough to meet the norm. *[from p:sg-decides, p:sponsor-votes, l:vote-count]* {#p:sg-sponsor}
1. **Proposition.** When each of two groups casts at least a third of the Favor-plus-Against votes against the other's design, neither design meets the 2:1 norm. The deadlock ends only when voters change position, a design changes, or the voters present change, unless a chair calls consensus below the norm or the design room sends both designs to competing TSs. *[from l:vote-count, f:two-to-one, f:design-forward, f:vehicle, f:competing-ts]* {#p:deadlock}
1. **Proposition.** In a long deadlock, a group whose sponsors pay its costs bears less per meeting than a group paying its own way. In the case the table below describes, the first holds and the second yields, whichever design is better. *[proved by l:hold-yield-holder-gains, l:hold-yield-both-hold, l:hold-yield-whole-over-half, l:hold-yield-half-over-deadlock, l:hold-yield-half-over-none, l:hold-yield-cheap-holds, l:hold-yield-costly-yields]* *[from ax:who-pays, ax:cost, p:deadlock]* {#p:attrition}
   1. **Lemma.** When one deadlocked group yields, the other holds, and no one else votes Against the holder's design, the deadlock ends with that design advancing. The holder gains its whole stake, the yielder none of its own, and neither bears the cost of holding through a deadlock. *[from p:deadlock, l:vote-count, d:stake]* {#l:hold-yield-holder-gains}

1. **Theorem.** Under SD-4 and the clauses of ISO/IEC Directives Part 1 that chapter 1 quotes, in the edition that replaces the JTC 1 Supplement, and given the axioms, the following choices form a settled pattern, in which no player improves their position by changing only their own choice. A design-bound author seeks direction polls as early as they can pass, proposes first the smallest part that fixes the features its gain depends on, seeks the IS over a TS, leaves out trade-offs no reviewer would raise, and answers objections with the cheapest change that keeps the 2:1 norm met. A result-seeking author states trade-offs and makes changes whenever the better design they bring is worth their cost to it, and backs a rival that serves users better by more than its share of the reversal's cost; an author who values both chooses, at each two-way choice, as the type whose value differs more. A sponsor pays for participants where the decisions it values are made, study groups included. Chairs and the convenor follow SD-4's procedure unless their stake outweighs what departing can cost them. A national body votes Yes with comments unless blocking one feature is worth as much to it as the rest of the document. A user takes part only when their own stake outweighs a cost borne alone, and otherwise uses a cheaper move or none.

   In this pattern a design's content can be fixed when it is least reviewed. Its first favorable poll can make it the incumbent, every later change and rival then pays a cost of reversal that grows whether or not the design is wrong, objections count only in the forms and times that attending teaches, and in a long deadlock the group whose sponsors pay its costs can hold longest, whichever design is better. A proposal that costs more to study than most participants' stakes in it can advance with no change at all, and an objector who arrives after adoption meets these obstacles together. Other things equal, a proposal advances more readily when its authors keep presenting it, a sponsor pays for participants who hold its position, its authors know the procedures, the chair expects fewer problems from it, a poll has placed it among the work aimed at the next IS, or it is already the incumbent. No user votes, and the needs that reach a vote are those participants and their sponsors know, which need not match those of users at large. The participants present decide content, and a sponsor can add voters without limit. A change to a design costs the sponsors who have built on it while its gain is spread across all users, and an author gains at adoption while a hidden flaw costs users later. Technical merit enters through what participants value, including a good result for users, and through the discussion, papers, and implementation experience that inform their votes, while SD-4 assigns no one to rule on it and the consensus test never asks whether an objection is right. No step checks whether separately adopted designs work together, review whose gain is spread across all users goes undone, and the record need not show whether objections were answered. The rules ask no author to state a trade-off, so leaving one out costs a design-bound author nothing the rules impose, while stating one can cost a result-seeking author votes. A first part that fixes a structure is reviewed before anyone can study what the later parts need of it, and every rival must then replace it. The rules also contain checks on each of these effects, and each result about large or small proposals holds for any proposal with the attributes it rests on. *[from l:settled-regular, l:settled-design-bound, l:settled-result-seeking, l:weighs-both, p:same-moves, l:settled-small-author, l:settled-sg-chair, l:settled-design-chair, l:settled-convenor, l:settled-nb, l:settled-sponsor, l:settled-user, p:checks, p:who-decides, p:incumbent-start, p:early-polls, ind:reversal-grows, p:challenger-bar, p:fluency, p:light-review, p:late-arrival, p:roadmap-poll, p:chair-confidence, p:attrition, p:users-out, p:who-takes-part, p:sponsor-votes, p:sponsor-features, l:consensus-test, p:reward-timing, p:merit-gap, l:implementation-informs, p:integration, p:review-shortfall, p:record-indistinct, p:disclosure, p:first-part, p:size-attributes, m:players, f:supplements-replaced, f:no-merit-ruling, f:present-required, ax:learning, ax:prefer, d:settled-pattern]* {#thm:settled}
