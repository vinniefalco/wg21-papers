## 10. National bodies

1. **Lemma.** At enquiry and final ballots every national body may vote, and at the final ballot abstentions are excluded from the count and a negative vote must give technical reasons; ISO encourages a body without national consensus or expert input to abstain, and the concept of sustained opposition does not apply to these votes. A draft that fails the final ballot goes back to the committee, which decides whether to resubmit a modified draft, publish a Technical Specification, or cancel the project. *[proved by l:nb-all-vote, l:ballot-count, l:no-needs-reasons, l:abstain-encouraged, l:ballots-not-sustained, l:failed-final]* {#l:ballot-rules}
   1. **Lemma.** Every national body may vote at enquiry and final ballots, since ISO gives that right to all ISO member bodies, whatever their status within a committee. *[from f:all-nbs-vote, d:national-body]* {#l:nb-all-vote}
   1. **Lemma.** At enquiry and final ballots alike, a draft is approved if two thirds of the P-members' votes are positive and at most one quarter of all votes are negative. Abstentions, and negative votes without technical comments, are left out of the count. *[proved by f:enquiry-count]* *[from f:iso-ballot]* {#l:ballot-count}
      1. "An enquiry draft is approved if" "a) a two-thirds majority of the votes cast by the P-members are in favour, and" "b) not more than one-quarter of the total number of votes cast are negative." "Abstentions are excluded when the votes are counted, as well as negative votes not accompanied by any comments of a technical nature." (ISO/IEC Directives Part 1, 2.6.3) {#f:enquiry-count}
   1. **Lemma.** At a final ballot, a national body that finds the draft unacceptable must vote No with its reasons in technical comments, and may not cast a Yes conditional on changes. *[from f:no-conditional-yes]* {#l:no-needs-reasons}
   1. **Lemma.** ISO encourages a national body to abstain on a ballot when it lacks national consensus or expert input. *[from f:nb-abstain]* {#l:abstain-encouraged}
   1. **Lemma.** The concept of sustained opposition does not apply to national body votes at enquiry or final ballots, since ISO subjects those votes to its voting rules instead. *[proved by inf:approval-final]* *[from f:not-ballots]* {#l:ballots-not-sustained}
      1. The final ballot is the approval-stage ballot, since the Directives treat votes on final drafts in clause 2.7, whose voting period falls at the approval stage. *[from f:all-nbs-vote, f:late-changes]* {#inf:approval-final}
   1. **Lemma.** A final draft that fails its ballot is referred back to SC22, which decides whether to resubmit a modified draft, publish a Technical Specification, or cancel the project. *[from f:failed-fdis, d:national-body]* {#l:failed-final}
1. **Lemma.** A No vote on a document opposes every feature in it, including features the body's own experts support. *[proved by l:no-whole, l:experts-own]* {#l:nb-no-scope}
   1. **Lemma.** Since a national body votes on the whole document at ballot, its No vote counts against the whole document and so against each feature in it. *[from p:bundling]* {#l:no-whole}
   1. **Lemma.** The experts a national body appoints act in a personal capacity and not as its official representatives, so a feature they support may still be one its ballot vote opposes. *[from f:experts-appointed, f:experts-personal]* {#l:experts-own}
1. **Proposition.** A national body that values the rest of a document above blocking one feature in it does better voting Yes with comments than voting No over that feature, since WG21 considers all national body feedback whatever the vote, ISO requires a response to every comment on an enquiry draft, and comments on a final draft are kept for the next review. *[proved by l:comment-consideration, l:comment-record, l:no-priority, l:no-blocks-rest, l:approval-preferred]* *[from d:does-better]* {#p:nb-comment}
   1. **Lemma.** SD-4 says that WG21 considers all national body feedback whatever the vote, and that voting No only to raise the priority of a body's comments is therefore ineffective. *[from f:no-means-no]* {#l:comment-consideration}
   1. **Lemma.** ISO requires a response to every comment on an enquiry draft and keeps every comment on a final draft for the next review, whatever vote it comes with. *[proved by inf:enquiry-response, inf:final-kept]* {#l:comment-record}
      1. The Directives treat enquiry drafts in clause 2.6, under which SC22 must respond to all comments received and report each one with its secretariat's observations. *[from f:all-nbs-vote, f:respond-comments, f:comment-report, d:national-body]* {#inf:enquiry-response}
      1. The Directives treat final drafts in clause 2.7, under which all comments received are retained for the next review and recorded as noted for future consideration. *[from f:all-nbs-vote, f:comments-noted]* {#inf:final-kept}
   1. **Lemma.** ISO requires every attempt to resolve negative votes on an enquiry draft, but under SD-4 a No vote gets a body's comments no more consideration than a Yes vote. *[from f:respond-comments, l:comment-consideration]* {#l:no-priority}
   1. **Lemma.** So beyond what its comments bring with a Yes vote, a No vote over one feature adds only a vote against the whole document. That vote can help block the feature only by helping block the rest with it. *[from l:comment-record, l:no-priority, l:nb-no-scope]* {#l:no-blocks-rest}
   1. **Lemma.** A body that values the rest of a document above blocking one feature ranks the document's approval above its failure, which would block the feature only together with the rest. *[from p:bundling, ax:prefer]* {#l:approval-preferred}
1. **Lemma.** SD-4 treats as out of scope a comment that adds a feature, and as not in keeping with the ISO Code of Conduct's commitment to accept group decisions a comment that repeats a change already decided at a meeting the body attended, unless new information has arisen or the body told the committee all along that it would vote No. *[proved by l:comment-new-feature, l:comment-decided, l:comment-new-info, l:comment-all-along]* {#l:nb-comment-limits}
   1. **Lemma.** SD-4 treats a ballot comment that asks to add a feature not already in the document as out of scope, and directs new features to proposal papers. *[from f:ballot-no-new-features]* {#l:comment-new-feature}
   1. **Lemma.** SD-4 treats as out of harmony with the commitment to accept group decisions a ballot comment asking for a change already decided otherwise at a WG21 meeting the body attended. *[proved by inf:decided-condition, inf:present-heard]* {#l:comment-decided}
      1. SD-4 says so when the requesting body was present at the meeting and had an opportunity to have its objections heard and considered. *[from f:ballot-decided]* {#inf:decided-condition}
      1. SD-4 requires that the views of all be heard and understood, so under its rules a national body present at a meeting has that opportunity. *[from f:majority-listen, f:views-heard]* {#inf:present-heard}
   1. **Lemma.** SD-4 excepts a comment bringing new information that makes it reasonable to believe enough people could change their opinions to affect the previous consensus, with an on-time paper. *[from f:ballot-new-info]* {#l:comment-new-info}
   1. **Lemma.** SD-4 also excepts a body willing to vote No on the whole document over an item, if it has told WG21 all along how strongly it objects. *[from f:no-surprise]* {#l:comment-all-along}
1. **Proposition.** A serious objection raised or re-raised in plenary has weight there only if escalated in time on the email lists and within its holder's national body, where SD-4's default is that it is not a national position; after the deadline, SD-4 directs new information to the email lists or a national body ballot comment, with a discussion paper. *[proved by l:escalation-steps, l:unescalated-no-weight, l:nb-default]* *[from p:late-objection]* {#p:nb-route}
   1. **Lemma.** SD-4 asks the holder of a serious objection to a subgroup decision that will be brought to plenary that week to raise it during the week. It asks that the objection be raised on the email lists, no later than 5pm the evening before plenary, and within the holder's own national body. *[from f:escalate-deadline, f:escalate-nb]* {#l:escalation-steps}
   1. **Lemma.** SD-4 says an objection not escalated in these ways but raised or re-raised in plenary should not be given weight there. *[from f:plenary-weight, l:escalation-steps]* {#l:unescalated-no-weight}
   1. **Lemma.** Within the holder's national body, SD-4's default is that the objection is not a national position unless discussed before or during the meeting and found to be one. *[from f:escalate-nb]* {#l:nb-default}
1. **Proposition.** A national body decides its position by whatever internal rules its panel uses, which SD-4 does not state, and ISO asks it to take account of all interests concerned at the national level, so whether participants whose costs one sponsor pays add weight to its interests depends on those rules. Under INCITS's rules for the United States, participants whose costs a voting member pays add no voting membership beyond that member's own. *[proved by l:incits-votes]* *[from f:late-info, f:escalate-nb, f:iso-early, ax:who-pays]* {#p:nb-members}
   1. **Lemma.** INCITS, whose Executive Board approves the United States position that goes to ANSI, gives each organization one voting membership, apart from up to four Department of Defense votes on technical committees, and admits a consultant as a voting member only if no voting member funds the consultant's participation; a United States delegation may also decide a new issue at an international plenary by caucus consensus. *[proved by inf:incits-board, inf:incits-one-org, inf:incits-dod, inf:incits-consultant, inf:incits-caucus, inf:incits-international]* {#l:incits-votes}
      1. INCITS's Executive Board, the United States TAG to JTC 1, approves the United States voting position, which then goes to ANSI for transmission to JTC 1 or ISO. *[from f:incits-position]* {#inf:incits-board}
      1. INCITS allows one voting membership per organization, which may be a corporation, federal or military agency, partnership, association, self-employed or self-financed individual, or other legal or commercial entity. *[from f:incits-one-vote, f:incits-organization]* {#inf:incits-one-org}
      1. On INCITS technical committees, and on the other groups the rule names, the Department of Defense may have up to four votes from separate and unrelated entities. *[from f:incits-limits]* {#inf:incits-dod}
      1. A consultant organization can be a voting member only if it declares that no organization already having voting membership funds its participation. *[from f:incits-limits]* {#inf:incits-consultant}
      1. When a new issue comes before a Plenary meeting, the United States delegation may set the official United States position by caucus consensus, as its head of delegation determines. *[from f:incits-caucus]* {#inf:incits-caucus}
      1. An official United States position is one that goes to JTC 1 or ISO, so a Plenary meeting at which a United States delegation sets one is an international meeting. *[from f:incits-position, f:incits-caucus]* {#inf:incits-international}
   1. **Proposition.** Under INCITS's rules for the United States, participants who represent one voting member share its one voting membership, apart from up to four Department of Defense votes on technical committees. A consultant whose participation a voting member funds cannot itself be a voting member. *[from l:incits-votes]* {#p:nb-members-incits weakened}
1. **Proposition.** SD-4 and ISO both ask national bodies to make objections known early. *[proved by l:sd4-timely, l:sd4-no-surprise, l:iso-early-stage]* {#p:nb-early}
   1. **Lemma.** SD-4 says escalation becomes inappropriate when a serious objection is withheld until plenary, or until formal ballot time when national bodies vote, instead of being raised as early as possible. *[from f:timely, l:nb-all-vote]* {#l:sd4-timely}
   1. **Lemma.** SD-4 says a national body's No vote on a matter already discussed and decided should never arise for the first time as a surprise on a ballot. *[from f:no-surprise]* {#l:sd4-no-surprise}
   1. **Lemma.** ISO gives national bodies the responsibility to make their technical standpoint clear at an early stage of the work, rather than at the final approval stage. *[from f:iso-early]* {#l:iso-early-stage}
1. **Proposition.** A national body may appeal within eight weeks, an appeal on a draft is eligible only on questions of principle or harm to the reputation of ISO or IEC, and work continues during an appeal up to and including the approval stage. *[proved by l:appeal-right, l:appeal-eligible, l:appeal-work]* {#p:appeal-continues}
   1. **Lemma.** ISO gives every national body the right to appeal a decision to the body above the one that made it, within eight weeks of the decision. *[from f:appeal, d:national-body]* {#l:appeal-right}
   1. **Lemma.** An appeal on a decision about a draft is eligible only on questions of principle or where the draft's contents may be detrimental to the reputation of ISO or IEC. *[from f:appeal-technical]* {#l:appeal-eligible}
   1. **Lemma.** When an appeal is against a decision on work in progress, the work continues up to and including the approval stage. *[from f:appeal-continues]* {#l:appeal-work}
1. **Lemma.** A body that values the rest of a document above blocking any one feature in it ranks Yes with comments above abstaining. Only the Yes counts toward the approval it prefers, and WG21 considers its feedback either way. *[from l:approval-preferred, l:ballot-count, l:comment-consideration, ax:prefer]* {#l:rank-rest-yes}
1. **Lemma.** A body that values the rest ranks abstaining above No, since only the No counts against the approval it prefers and its comments gain nothing from a No. *[from l:approval-preferred, l:ballot-count, l:no-priority, ax:prefer]* {#l:rank-rest-abstain}
1. **Lemma.** A body that does not want the work at all ranks No above abstaining, since only the No counts against approval, which is what SD-4 says No is for. *[from l:ballot-count, f:ballot-no, ax:prefer]* {#l:rank-oppose-no}
1. **Lemma.** A body that does not want the work at all ranks abstaining above Yes with comments, since only the Yes counts toward approval of work it wants in no form. *[from l:ballot-count, f:ballot-no, ax:prefer]* {#l:rank-oppose-abstain}

In plain terms: a national body's No vote opposes a whole document, so a body that values the rest of a document above blocking one feature in it does better voting Yes with comments, which WG21 considers whatever the vote. SD-4 gives weight in plenary only to serious objections escalated in time, and SD-4 and ISO both ask national bodies to make objections known early. A national body may appeal within eight weeks, an appeal on a draft only on questions of principle or harm to the reputation of ISO or IEC, and work under appeal continues up to and including the approval stage.
