## 4. What a vote costs the voter

1. **Lemma.** Under the 2:1 norm, each Against vote raises by two the number of Favor votes a proposal needs. *[proved by l:norm-count, l:fewest-favor]* {#l:against-double}
   1. **Lemma.** The 2:1 norm is met when, and only when, the Favor votes are more than twice the Against votes. *[from f:two-to-one, d:positions]* {#l:norm-count}
   1. **Lemma.** Vote counts are whole numbers, so the fewest Favor votes meeting the norm is one more than twice the Against votes, which rises by two with each added Against vote. *[from l:norm-count]* {#l:fewest-favor}
1. **Lemma.** Neutral votes and abstentions do not enter the 2:1 comparison, though SD-4 says a Neutral majority can be concerning. *[proved by l:abstain-count]* *[from f:two-to-one, d:positions]* {#l:neutral-outside}
   1. **Lemma.** SD-4 says not everyone in the room needs to vote, and a participant who abstains by not voting adds to neither the Favor votes nor the Against votes. *[from f:not-vote, d:positions]* {#l:abstain-count}
1. **Lemma.** SD-4 names Against voters as the voters a chair may ask for reasons, and names no such step for any other position. *[from f:against-reasons, f:no-reasons-other]* {#l:reasons-against}
1. **Proposition.** For a regular, voting Against a colleague's proposal costs more than voting Neutral or not voting, since it brings no less risk to the colleague's later support and adds the time needed to prepare reasons, while voting Favor brings neither cost and so costs no more than voting Neutral. *[proved by l:standing-matters, l:reasons-time, l:favor-no-risk, l:favor-no-reasons]* *[from ax:support]* {#p:against-costs-more}
   1. **Lemma.** A regular expects to meet many of the same colleagues again and values their standing, so a risk to their standing with a colleague is a cost to them. *[from ax:repeat, ax:prefer, d:regular, d:standing]* {#l:standing-matters}
   1. **Lemma.** Voting Against adds the time needed to prepare reasons, since SD-4 names only Against voters as voters a chair may ask for reasons and asks them to be prepared. *[from f:against-reasons, l:reasons-against, ax:review-cost, ax:prefer]* {#l:reasons-time}
   1. **Lemma.** The proof's starting points name the risk of less ready support only for an Against vote, a Neutral vote, or not voting, so a Favor vote brings none of it. *[from ax:support]* {#l:favor-no-risk}
   1. **Lemma.** SD-4 names no step asking Favor voters for reasons, so a Favor vote adds no time to prepare them. *[from l:reasons-against]* {#l:favor-no-reasons}
1. **Proposition.** A regular does better voting Against than voting Neutral when, and only when, what the Against vote is worth to them, through its effect on the poll's outcome or on the changes made in answer to it, exceeds the added cost of voting Against. *[proved by l:worth-outcome, l:worth-changes, l:weigh, l:weigh-result]* {#p:stake-threshold}
   1. **Lemma.** An Against vote can be worth something to the regular through the poll's outcome, since it raises by two the Favor votes the proposal needs. *[from l:against-double, d:stake]* {#l:worth-outcome}
   1. **Lemma.** An Against vote can also be worth something to the regular through changes made in answer to it. SD-4 asks Against voters to be prepared to say what changes would address their concern, and expects authors to implement changes the group directs. *[from f:against-reasons, f:direction-implement]* {#l:worth-changes}
   1. **Lemma.** The regular weighs what an Against vote is worth to them through the outcome and the changes against its added cost, on one scale of their own. *[from ax:prefer, p:against-costs-more]* {#l:weigh}
   1. **Lemma.** With other values held equal, voting Against is therefore worth more to the regular than voting Neutral, or not voting, exactly when that worth exceeds that added cost. *[from l:weigh, d:does-better]* {#l:weigh-result}
1. **Proposition.** A regular who doubts a proposal, and for whom an Against vote is worth less than its added cost, does better voting Neutral or not voting, and either choice leaves the doubt outside the 2:1 comparison. *[proved by l:doubt-refrain]* *[from l:neutral-outside]* {#p:doubt-outside-count}
   1. **Lemma.** When an Against vote is worth less to a regular than its added cost, the regular does better voting Neutral, or not voting, than voting Against. *[from l:weigh, d:does-better]* {#l:doubt-refrain}
1. **Lemma.** One more Against vote changes whether the 2:1 norm is met only when the Favor votes exceed twice the other Against votes by one or two. *[proved by l:room-before, l:room-after]* {#l:room-size}
   1. **Lemma.** Without the added vote, the 2:1 norm is met exactly when the Favor votes exceed twice the other Against votes by at least one. *[from l:norm-count]* {#l:room-before}
   1. **Lemma.** With the added vote, twice the Against votes grows by two, so the norm is met exactly when Favor votes exceed twice the other Against votes by at least three. *[from l:norm-count]* {#l:room-after}
1. **Proposition.** A participant can state a preference for another design in discussion and vote Neutral, which leaves the 2:1 count unchanged and is not a position SD-4 names for questions. *[proved by l:speak-then-neutral]* *[from l:neutral-outside, l:reasons-against]* {#p:prefer-vs-stop}
   1. **Lemma.** SD-4 welcomes negative comments, and each person in the room can cast one vote, so a participant can state a preference for another design and then vote Neutral. *[from f:negative-welcome, p:who-votes, d:positions]* {#l:speak-then-neutral}
1. **Lemma.** Any group casting at least one third of the Favor-plus-Against votes as Against prevents the 2:1 norm from being met. *[proved by l:third-split, l:third-double]* *[from l:norm-count]* {#l:one-third-against}
   1. **Lemma.** When a group casts at least one third of the Favor-plus-Against votes as Against, at least one third of those votes are Against, so at most two thirds are Favor. *[from d:positions]* {#l:third-split}
   1. **Lemma.** Twice the Against votes is then at least two thirds of the Favor-plus-Against votes, and so at least the Favor votes. *[from l:third-split]* {#l:third-double}
1. **Lemma.** When an Against vote is worth less to the regular than its added cost, the regular ranks voting Neutral or not voting above voting Against. *[from p:doubt-outside-count]* {#l:vote-below}
1. **Lemma.** When an Against vote is worth more to the regular than its added cost, the regular ranks voting Against above voting Neutral or not voting. *[from p:stake-threshold, l:weigh-result]* {#l:vote-above}

|---|---|---|

1. **Proposition.** Two regulars with pending proposals each do better not opposing the other's proposal than opposing it, unless an Against vote on it is worth more to the regular than its added cost, which includes the risk that the other then supports the regular's own proposal less readily. *[from ax:support, ax:prefer, p:against-costs-more, p:stake-threshold, d:does-better]* {#p:mutual-support}
   1. **Proposition.** Each of two regulars with pending proposals does better not opposing the other's proposal than opposing it when an Against vote on it is worth less than its added cost. That cost includes the risk that the other then supports the regular's own proposal less readily, and neither does better opposing unless the vote is worth more than that cost. *[proved by inf:mutual-risk, inf:mutual-less, inf:mutual-no-gain]* {#p:mutual-support-weak weakened}
      1. For each regular, the other's proposal is a colleague's and their own is pending, so an Against vote's added cost includes the risk that the other supports it less readily. *[from p:against-costs-more, ax:support, l:standing-matters]* {#inf:mutual-risk}
      1. When the Against vote is worth less than that added cost, voting Neutral or not voting, both ways of not opposing, is worth more to the regular than opposing. *[from l:doubt-refrain]* {#inf:mutual-less}
      1. Unless the vote is worth more than that added cost, the regular does no better voting Against than voting Neutral. *[from p:stake-threshold]* {#inf:mutual-no-gain}
1. **Lemma.** No single Against vote is sure to make a proposal stop advancing or to bring the change it asks for. SD-4 says a proposal normally advances when the 2:1 norm is met, whether it advances depends on the chair's consensus call, and an author may decline a directed change. *[from f:two-to-one, p:chair-decides, f:direction-implement]* {#l:vote-unsure}
1. **Lemma.** In the situation the table below describes, an Against vote on the other's proposal is worth less to each regular than the cost of opposing it. It can at best spare the regular the loss that proposal brings them, is not sure to, and by the caption that loss is no more than the cost. *[from l:vote-unsure, l:reasons-time]* {#l:mutual-worth-less}
1. **Lemma.** In that situation, whatever the other regular chooses, each regular ranks refraining above opposing, since voting Neutral or not voting is then worth more to them than voting Against. *[from l:mutual-worth-less, l:doubt-refrain]* {#l:mutual-refrain}
1. **Lemma.** In that situation, each regular ranks opposing while the other refrains above refraining while the other opposes, since the other's opposition costs them more than their own opposing does. *[from ax:prefer, d:does-better]* {#l:mutual-own-vs-other}

In plain terms: under SD-4's 2:1 norm, each Against vote raises by two the Favor votes a proposal needs, while Neutral votes and not voting leave the count unchanged, and SD-4 names only Against voters for questions about reasons. So for a regular, voting Against a colleague's proposal costs more than voting Neutral, and the regular does better voting Against only when the vote is worth more to them than that added cost. Two regulars with pending proposals each do better not opposing the other's proposal when an Against vote on it is worth less to them than its added cost.
