## 11. The settled pattern

1. **Lemma.** Whatever the others choose, a regular does no better than to vote Favor on colleagues' proposals they support, and on one they doubt, Against does best only when worth more than its added cost. Otherwise Neutral, not voting, or even Favor can do best, since Favor risks nothing with the colleague, while Against costs risk to colleagues' support and time for reasons, and changes the result only one or two votes above the line. *[from p:against-costs-more, p:doubt-outside-count, l:vote-count, p:objector-coordination, p:prefer-vs-stop, p:mutual-support, p:repeat-objection, p:cutoff-effect, ax:support, ax:prefer, m:moves, d:does-better, d:settled-pattern]* {#l:settled-regular}
1. **Lemma.** Whatever the others choose, a design-bound author does no better than to seek direction polls as early as they can pass, propose first the smallest part that fixes the features its gain depends on, and seek the IS over a TS unless the room requires one. 1. **Lemma.** Whatever the others choose, a result-seeking author does no better than to state a trade-off and grant a change whenever the better design they can bring is worth more to it than the votes or work they cost. It does no better than to seek an early poll or an early first part only where sooner adoption outweighs the higher cost this puts on later improvements, and to back a rival that would serve users better by more than its share of the reversal's cost. *[from p:early-polls, ind:reversal-grows, p:challenger-bar, p:parts, p:disclosure, p:least-cost-response, p:change-list, d:result-seeking, m:moves-author, d:settled-pattern]* {#l:settled-result-seeking}
1. **Lemma.** Between two options, an author who values both a good result for users and particular features chooses as the author type would whose value differs more between them. *[from ax:prefer, d:result-seeking, d:design-bound, d:does-better]* {#l:weighs-both}

1. **Lemma.** Whatever the others choose, a small proposal's author does no better than to keep it presented, in person, remotely where offered, or by another qualified and prepared presenter, while it is worth more to them than costs they bear alone. 1. **Lemma.** Whatever the others choose, a user does no better than to take the move, if any, with the largest positive net gain: taking part, TS feedback, a paper a participant presents, or a request to a national body. 1. **Theorem.** Under SD-4 and the quoted clauses of ISO/IEC Directives Part 1, in the edition replacing the JTC 1 Supplement, and given the axioms, these choices form a settled pattern. In it, no player improves their position by changing only their own choice. A design-bound author seeks direction polls as early as they can pass, proposes first the smallest part fixing the features it needs, and seeks the IS over a TS. It leaves out trade-offs no reviewer would raise and answers objections with the cheapest change that keeps the 2:1 norm met. A result-seeking author states trade-offs and makes changes whenever the better design they bring is worth their cost to it. It backs a rival that serves users better by more than its share of the reversal's cost. An author who values both chooses, at each two-way choice, as the type whose value differs more. A small proposal's author keeps it presented while it is worth costs borne alone. A regular votes Favor on colleagues' proposals they support, and Against one they doubt only when that is worth more than its added cost. Otherwise the regular takes whichever of Neutral, not voting, or Favor does best, and Favor risks nothing with the colleague. A sponsor pays for participants where the decisions it values are made, study groups included. Chairs and the convenor follow SD-4's procedure unless their stake outweighs what departing can cost them. A national body votes Yes with comments unless blocking one feature is worth as much to it as the rest of the document. A user takes part only when their own stake outweighs a cost borne alone, and otherwise uses a cheaper move or none.

   In this pattern a design's content can be fixed when it is least reviewed. Its first favorable poll can make it the incumbent, and every later change or rival then pays a reversal cost that grows whether or not the design is wrong. Objections count only in the forms and times that attending teaches. In a long deadlock, the group whose sponsors pay its costs can hold longest, whichever design is better. A proposal that costs more to study than most participants' stakes in it can advance with no change at all. An objector who arrives after adoption meets these obstacles together. Other things equal, a proposal advances more readily when its authors keep presenting it or know the procedures, or when a sponsor pays for participants who hold its position. The same holds when the chair expects fewer problems from it, a poll has placed it among the work aimed at the next IS, or it is already the incumbent. No user votes, and the needs that reach a vote are those participants and their sponsors know, which need not match those of users at large. The participants present decide content, and a sponsor can add voters without limit. A change to a design costs the sponsors who have built on it, while its gain is spread across all users. An author gains at adoption, while a hidden flaw costs users later. Technical merit enters through what participants value, including a good result for users, and through the discussion, papers, and implementation experience that inform their votes. SD-4 assigns no one to rule on it, and the consensus test never asks whether an objection is right. No SD-4 step checks whether separately adopted designs work together, review whose gain is spread across all users goes undone, and the record need not show whether objections were answered. The rules ask no author to state a trade-off. Leaving one out costs a design-bound author nothing the rules impose, while stating one can cost a result-seeking author votes. A first part that fixes a structure is reviewed before anyone can study what the later parts need of it, and every rival must then replace it. The rules also contain checks on each of these effects, and each result about large or small proposals holds for any proposal with the attributes it rests on. *[from ax:support, l:settled-regular, l:settled-design-bound, l:settled-result-seeking, l:weighs-both, p:same-moves, l:settled-small-author, l:settled-sg-chair, l:settled-design-chair, l:settled-convenor, l:settled-nb, l:settled-sponsor, l:settled-user, p:checks, p:who-decides, p:incumbent-start, p:early-polls, ind:reversal-grows, p:challenger-bar, p:fluency, p:light-review, p:late-arrival, p:roadmap-poll, p:chair-confidence, p:attrition, p:users-out, p:who-takes-part, p:sponsor-votes, p:sponsor-features, l:consensus-test, p:reward-timing, p:merit-gap, l:implementation-informs, p:integration, p:review-shortfall, p:record-indistinct, p:disclosure, p:first-part, p:size-attributes, m:players, f:supplements-replaced, f:no-merit-ruling, f:present-required, ax:learning, ax:prefer, d:settled-pattern]* {#thm:settled}

In plain terms: when each player makes the choices the rules reward, given what they value, no player improves their position by changing only their own choice, and in the settled pattern those choices form, a design's content can be fixed when it is least reviewed. The rules ask no author to state a trade-off, so leaving one out costs a design-bound author nothing the rules impose, while stating one can cost a result-seeking author votes. The rules also contain checks on each of these effects.
