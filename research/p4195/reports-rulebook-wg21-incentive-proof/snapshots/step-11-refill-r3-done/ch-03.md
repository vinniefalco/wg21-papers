## 3. What a vote costs

1. **Lemma.** Under the 2:1 norm each Against vote raises by two the Favor votes a proposal needs, while Neutral votes and abstentions leave the count unchanged. One Against vote changes the result only at one or two votes above the line, and at least a third of the Favor-plus-Against votes cast Against keeps the norm unmet. *[proved by l:against-double, l:neutral-outside, l:room-size, l:one-third-against]* {#l:vote-count}
   1. **Lemma.** Under the 2:1 norm, each Against vote raises by two the number of Favor votes a proposal needs. *[from f:two-to-one, d:positions]* {#l:against-double}
   1. **Lemma.** Neutral votes and abstentions do not enter the 2:1 comparison, though SD-4 says a Neutral majority of those voting can be concerning. *[from f:two-to-one, f:not-vote, d:positions]* {#l:neutral-outside}
   1. **Lemma.** One more Against vote changes whether the 2:1 norm is met only when the Favor votes exceed twice the other Against votes by one or two. *[proved by inf:met-without-vote, inf:met-with-vote]* *[from f:two-to-one, d:positions]* {#l:room-size}
      1. Without that vote, the 2:1 norm is met exactly when the Favor votes exceed twice the other Against votes by one or more. *[from f:two-to-one, d:positions]* {#inf:met-without-vote}
      1. With that vote, the norm is met exactly when the Favor votes exceed twice the other Against votes by three or more, two more than before. *[from l:against-double]* {#inf:met-with-vote}
   1. **Lemma.** Any group casting at least one third of the Favor-plus-Against votes as Against prevents the 2:1 norm from being met. *[proved by inf:two-thirds-favor, inf:third-caps-favor]* *[from f:two-to-one, d:positions]* {#l:one-third-against}
      1. The 2:1 norm needs more than twice as many Favor votes as Against votes, that is, Favor votes making up more than two thirds of the Favor-plus-Against votes. *[from f:two-to-one, d:positions]* {#inf:two-thirds-favor}
      1. A group casting at least a third of the Favor-plus-Against votes as Against leaves the Favor votes at most two thirds of them. *[from d:positions]* {#inf:third-caps-favor}
1. **Proposition.** For a regular, an Against vote on a colleague's proposal costs more than a Neutral vote or none. It risks the colleague's later support no less, and only Against voters can be asked for reasons, which take time to prepare. *[from ax:support, ax:repeat, ax:prefer, ax:review-cost, f:against-reasons, f:no-reasons-other, d:regular, d:standing]* {#p:against-costs-more}
1. **Proposition.** A regular's doubt about a colleague's proposal enters the 2:1 count only if it is worth the extra cost of an Against vote. That vote changes the result only when the count sits one or two votes above the line, though it can also win changes to the design. *[proved by l:vote-below, l:vote-above]* *[from l:vote-count, p:against-costs-more, ax:prefer, f:against-reasons, f:direction-implement, d:stake, d:does-better]* {#p:doubt-outside-count}
   1. **Lemma.** When an Against vote is worth less to the regular than its extra cost, the regular ranks a Neutral vote or none above it. *[from p:against-costs-more, ax:prefer, d:does-better]* {#l:vote-below}
   1. **Lemma.** When an Against vote is worth more to the regular than its extra cost, the regular ranks it above a Neutral vote or none. *[from p:against-costs-more, ax:prefer, d:does-better]* {#l:vote-above}

*Table: One regular's vote on a colleague's proposal that the regular doubts. Columns: what an Against vote is worth to the regular, compared with its added cost. Each cell: the regular's rank of the vote. 2 is best within each column.* {#t:vote-choice}

| | Worth below cost | Worth above cost |
|---|---|---|
| **Neutral or abstain** | **2** | 1 |
| **Against** | 1 | **2** |

1. **Proposition.** With many Favor votes cast, one objector's Against vote changes the result only if enough others also vote Against, so its worth to each objector depends on the others' votes. The same objectors can then form either of two settled patterns: just enough of them vote Against to block, or none does. *[proved by l:objector-effect, l:objectors-none, l:objectors-block]* *[from l:vote-count, p:doubt-outside-count, p:against-costs-more, d:stake, d:settled-pattern]* {#p:objector-coordination}
   1. **Lemma.** With many Favor votes cast, an objector's Against vote leaves the result unchanged when no other objector votes Against, or when enough others already do to block. It changes the result when it is one of just enough Against votes to block, since without it the 2:1 norm would be met. *[from l:vote-count]* {#l:objector-effect}
   1. **Lemma.** None of the objectors voting Against is a settled pattern when an Against vote that leaves the result unchanged is worth less to each of them than its extra cost. *[from l:objector-effect, l:vote-below, d:settled-pattern]* {#l:objectors-none}
   1. **Lemma.** In that case, just enough objectors voting Against to block is also a settled pattern when each of them values their vote above its extra cost. *[from l:objector-effect, l:vote-above, l:vote-below, d:settled-pattern]* {#l:objectors-block}
1. **Proposition.** A participant can argue for another design and still vote Neutral, which leaves the count unchanged and draws no request for reasons. *[from f:negative-welcome, l:vote-count, f:against-reasons, f:no-reasons-other]* {#p:prefer-vs-stop}
1. **Proposition.** Two regulars with pending proposals each risk the other's support for their own by opposing the other's proposal. When each regular's doubt is worth less than that risk and the time for reasons, neither opposes, and both do better than if both opposed. *[proved by l:mutual-refrain, l:mutual-own-vs-other]* *[from ax:support, ax:prefer, p:against-costs-more, p:doubt-outside-count, l:vote-count, d:does-better]* {#p:mutual-support}
   1. **Lemma.** In the case the table below describes, each regular ranks refraining above opposing, whatever the other does. That holds because an Against vote on the other's proposal is then worth less to them than its extra cost. *[from p:doubt-outside-count, l:vote-below, ax:support]* {#l:mutual-refrain}
   1. **Lemma.** In that case, each regular ranks opposing while the other refrains above refraining while the other opposes, since the other's opposition costs them more than their own opposing does. *[from ax:prefer, d:does-better]* {#l:mutual-own-vs-other}

*Table: Two regulars, each with a proposal pending, neither of which lowers what the other values by more than the cost of opposing it, and for each of whom the other's opposition costs more than their own opposing does. Refrains means votes Favor, Neutral, or not at all. First number: A's rank. Second number: B's rank. 4 is best. † marks a Pareto-optimal cell.* {#t:mutual-support}

| | B refrains | B opposes |
|---|---|---|
| **A refrains** | **4, 4** † | 2, 3 |
| **A opposes** | 3, 2 | 1, 1 |

*[from l:mutual-refrain, l:mutual-own-vs-other]*

In plain terms: under the 2:1 norm, each Against vote raises by two the Favor votes a proposal needs, Neutral votes and abstentions leave the count unchanged, and at least a third of the Favor-plus-Against votes cast Against keeps the norm unmet. For a regular, an Against vote on a colleague's proposal costs more than a Neutral vote or none, so a doubt enters the count only if it is worth that extra cost. Two regulars with pending proposals each risk the other's support for their own by opposing the other's proposal, and when each one's doubt is worth less than that risk and the time for reasons, neither opposes and both do better than if both opposed.
