1. **Proposition.** Within WG21's rooms and plenary, the rules let a proposal advance while objections to it remain unresolved, since advancing needs a consensus call and not the persuasion of every objector. *[proved by l:address-not-resolve, p:advance-unanswered]* {#p:persuasion-not-required}

## 6. Proposal size

1. **Proposition.** A participant does better attending in person only while their own value from attending exceeds the costs that fall on them, which are lower when a sponsor pays, though ISO asks that remote participation be offered where possible and bars participation fees. *[from ax:cost, ax:who-pays, ax:prefer, f:iso-cost, f:remote, f:no-fee, f:in-person-expected, d:does-better]* {#p:money-vs-presence}

## 11. The settled pattern

1. **Lemma.** Whatever the other players choose, a regular does no better than to vote Favor on a colleague's proposal they support and, on any other, to vote Against when an Against vote is worth more to them than its added cost and otherwise to vote Neutral or not at all. *[from p:against-costs-more, p:stake-threshold, p:doubt-outside-count, p:prefer-vs-stop, l:room-size, p:mutual-support, p:repeat-objection, p:cutoff-effect, m:moves, d:settled-pattern]* {#l:settled-regular}
1. **Lemma.** Whatever the other players choose, the authors of a large proposal do no better than to seek favorable direction polls early unless later review is expected to cost them more than becoming the incumbent sooner gains them, to present the work in parts unless the parts are worth little apart, to seek the IS over a TS unless the TS's experience is worth its delay to them or the design room requires a TS, and to answer objections with the least costly change that keeps the 2:1 norm met, unless the room has directed a change or they value the users' gain from a larger change above its cost. *[from p:early-polls, p:incumbent-start, p:challenger-bar, ind:adopted-parts, p:parts, p:large-position, p:prefer-is, p:least-cost-response, p:persuasion-not-required, p:change-list, l:author-knows-more, p:reward-timing, m:moves, d:settled-pattern]* {#l:settled-large-author}
1. **Lemma.** Whatever the other players choose, the author of a small proposal does no better than to keep the proposal presented, in person, remotely where offered, or by another qualified and prepared presenter, while pursuing it is worth more to them than the costs that fall on them, and to stop pursuing it when it is not. *[from p:small-position, p:money-vs-presence, p:newcomer, p:least-cost-response, m:moves, d:settled-pattern]* {#l:settled-small-author}
1. **Lemma.** Whatever the other players choose, a study group chair does no better than to poll the competing designs, side by side where there are several, and to send on as the group's initial proposal a design that meets the 2:1 norm, and no design when none does, unless the chair's stake in the contest is worth more to them than what departing from the norm can cost them. *[from p:sg-decides, p:deadlock, p:design-changes, p:attrition, p:sg-unified, p:concern-presence, p:chair-threshold, m:moves, d:settled-pattern]* {#l:settled-sg-chair}
1. **Lemma.** Whatever the other players choose, a design room chair does no better than to declare consensus when, after discussion of the Against voters' concerns, Favor votes are more than twice the Against votes and Neutral votes are not a majority of those voting, and to re-poll or decline when Favor votes are not more than twice the Against votes, unless the chair's stake in the proposal is worth more to them than what departing from SD-4's norm can cost them. *[from p:chair-default, p:repoll, p:chair-costs, p:chair-threshold, p:chair-no-reasons, l:chair-role, p:private-channel, l:poll-wording, p:one-meeting-cycle, m:moves, d:settled-pattern]* {#l:settled-design-chair}
1. **Lemma.** Whatever the other players choose, the convenor does no better than to follow SD-4's plenary procedure on what the subgroups forward and to declare consensus except where significant opposition holds national body positions or rests on objections escalated in time, unless the convenor's stake in the proposal is worth more to them than what departing from that procedure can cost them. *[from p:plenary-ratifies, p:nb-route, p:wording-rooms, l:appointments, p:chair-threshold, m:moves, d:settled-pattern]* {#l:settled-convenor}
1. **Lemma.** Whatever the other players choose, a national body does no better than to vote Yes with comments on a document whose other content it values above blocking any one feature in it, to vote No otherwise, and to raise its objections early, and ISO encourages it to abstain when it lacks national consensus or expert input. *[from p:nb-comment, l:nb-no-scope, l:nb-comment-limits, p:nb-early, l:ballot-rules, p:bundling, p:nb-members, m:moves, d:settled-pattern]* {#l:settled-nb}
1. **Lemma.** Whatever the other players choose, a sponsor does no better than to pay for participation where the decisions it values are made, as long as the difference its paid participants make to those decisions is worth more to it than the cost. *[from p:sponsor-votes, p:money-vs-presence, p:large-position, p:nb-members, m:moves, d:settled-pattern]* {#l:settled-sponsor}
1. **Lemma.** Whatever the other players choose, a user does no better than to become a participant when the user's stakes in the decisions they could take part in together exceed the costs of participating, and otherwise to use a cheaper move, such as feedback on a TS or white paper, a paper presented by a participant, or a request to their national body, when what it can bring them exceeds its cost. *[from p:who-votes, m:moves, p:money-vs-presence, p:reward-timing, d:settled-pattern]* {#l:settled-user}
1. **Proposition.** The rules also contain provisions that bear on these effects. Every view must be heard, negative comments are welcome, and SD-4's conduct rules ask for respect and good faith and bar collusion; a group casting at least one third of the Favor-plus-Against votes as Against prevents the 2:1 norm from being met; alternatives can be explored and polled side by side, SD-4 says a good paper shows them, followup and rebuttal papers can be considered, and an author who declines a directed change should defend that decision; competing TSs are possible, a TS or white paper should list the controversial issues to address before it merges, and implementation experience informs voters. Every expert is appointed by a national body or liaison organization, SC22 may cap how many each appoints, ISO recommends that experts keep their national body informed of the opinions in the working group and asks national bodies to take account of all interests concerned at the national level, the direction group may give an opinion on any proposal that SD-4 asks design room participants to weigh, each topic on the agenda needs an on-time paper, and SD-4 asks those with a concern to tell the chairs and authors early. Plenary solicits serious design concerns, national bodies ballot each draft sent for ballot and get a written answer to every comment on an enquiry draft, minutes can be challenged, appeals are open, a white paper has no prescribed schedule, a planned schedule keeps an unfinished feature from delaying ready ones, ISO says consensus requires resolving substantial objections and ends the committee stage when all technical issues have been resolved, and SD-4 works in addition to the Directives. *[from f:views-heard, f:majority-listen, f:respect-opinions, f:negative-welcome, f:good-faith, f:conduct-report, f:no-collusion, l:one-third-against, f:explore-alternatives, f:multiple-polls, f:show-alternatives, f:followup-papers, f:direction-implement, f:competing-ts, f:ts-exit-criteria, f:pre-merge, l:implementation-informs, f:experts-appointed, f:expert-cap, f:expert-contact, f:iso-early, f:direction-opinion, f:agenda-notice, f:report-early, f:design-concerns-solicited, l:ballot-rules, f:comment-report, f:respond-comments, l:record-checks, p:appeal-continues, f:white-paper-process, p:schedule-protects, f:resolve-substantial, f:committee-stage, f:sd4-scope]* {#p:checks}
1. **Theorem.** Under SD-4 and the clauses of ISO/IEC Directives Part 1 that chapter 1 quotes, in the edition that replaces the JTC 1 Supplement, and given the axioms, the following choices form a settled pattern, in which no player improves their position by changing only their own choice. Regulars vote Favor on colleagues' proposals they support, and on others vote Against when an Against vote is worth more to them than its added cost and otherwise vote Neutral or not at all. The authors of large proposals seek favorable direction polls early unless later review is expected to cost them more than the gain, present their work in parts, seek the IS over a TS unless the TS's experience is worth its delay to them or the design room requires a TS, and answer objections with the least costly change that keeps the 2:1 norm met, unless the room has directed a change or they value the users' gain from a larger change above its cost; the authors of small proposals keep them presented only while pursuing them is worth more to them than the costs that fall on them. Sponsors pay for participation where the decisions they value are made, as long as the difference their paid participants make is worth the cost. Study group chairs send on a design that meets the 2:1 norm and otherwise none; design room chairs declare consensus when, after discussion, Favor votes are more than twice the Against votes and Neutral votes are not a majority, and otherwise re-poll or decline; the convenor follows SD-4's plenary procedure; and each chair and the convenor departs from SD-4's procedure only when their stake in the proposal is worth more to them than what the departure can cost them. National bodies vote Yes with comments on documents whose other content they value above blocking any one feature, vote No otherwise, and raise objections early; and users become participants when their stakes in the decisions they could take part in together exceed the cost, and otherwise use cheaper moves when those are worth their cost.

   In this pattern, a proposal's progress depends in part on the participants who attend for it, on their stakes in it, and on the polls it has already won; a contest in which no design meets the 2:1 norm lasts, under that norm, until voters change position, a design changes, or the voters present change, unless a chair makes a call below the norm or the design room sends the designs to competing TSs; and the record need not show whether a proposal's objections were answered. The rules also contain provisions that bear on each of these effects. Technical merit enters through what participants value, including a good result for users, and through the discussion, papers, and implementation experience that inform their votes, while no one is assigned to rule on merit itself; and each result about large or small proposals holds for any proposal with the attributes it rests on. *[from l:settled-regular, l:settled-large-author, l:settled-small-author, l:settled-sg-chair, l:settled-design-chair, l:settled-convenor, l:settled-nb, l:settled-sponsor, l:settled-user, p:checks, p:deadlock, p:record-indistinct, p:reasons-unrecorded, p:definition-without-procedure, l:consensus-test, ax:prefer, l:implementation-informs, l:author-knows-more, f:no-merit-ruling, p:size-attributes, m:players, f:supplements-replaced, d:settled-pattern]* {#thm:settled}

## 12. Corollaries

1. **Corollary.** If the settled pattern holds, the record should show the following. SD-4 keeps meeting records password-protected but lets poll questions and numeric results be quoted, and each prediction names its evidence, what would count against it, an innocent explanation, and the data that would tell the two apart. *[proved by c:pred-sponsor-against, c:pred-abstention-length, c:pred-parts, c:pred-removals, c:pred-small-fade, c:pred-nb-positions, c:pred-written-answers]* *[from f:protected]* {#c:predictions}
   1. **Corollary.** Where one sponsor pays both a proposal's authors and many of the participants present, and its interests include the proposal's adoption, Against votes are a smaller share of the Favor-plus-Against votes than on other proposals; poll results and stated affiliations, taken as the observable measure of sponsor, are the evidence, and an Against share no lower on such proposals would count against it. An innocent explanation is that participants who work together share technical views, and comparing their votes on such proposals with their votes on proposals on the same topic from authors with other affiliations would tell the two apart. *[from p:sponsor-votes, ax:prefer, p:stake-threshold, thm:settled]* {#c:pred-sponsor-against}
   1. **Corollary.** Among proposals in which participants have similar stakes, the share of participants present who cast no vote rises with the length of the paper polled; poll results, attendance counts, and paper lengths are the evidence, and no such rise would count against it. An innocent explanation is that longer papers cover narrower topics that fewer participants know, and comparing papers on topics of similar breadth but different length would tell the two apart. *[from p:review-share, f:not-vote, thm:settled]* {#c:pred-abstention-length}
   1. **Corollary.** Large proposals appear as sequences of papers, each building on parts already adopted; the mailings are the evidence, and large designs adopted in one paper would count against it. An innocent explanation is that large designs are easier to review in parts, and comparing whether a design's parts are published together and reviewed in sequence or each published only after the one before is adopted would tell the two apart. *[from ind:adopted-parts, p:parts, p:large-position, thm:settled]* {#c:pred-parts}
   1. **Corollary.** Removals after adoption are rare and arrive with national body comments or with national bodies whose No votes could fail a ballot; working drafts, ballot comments, and poll results are the evidence, and frequent removals by narrow margins would count against it. An innocent explanation is that adopted designs rarely need removal, and comparing removals with the defect reports filed against adopted features would tell the two apart. *[from p:burden-flip, p:nb-comment, f:iso-ballot, f:no-means-no, thm:settled]* {#c:pred-removals}
   1. **Corollary.** Small proposals stop advancing without a negative poll more often than large proposals do; the mailings and poll results are the evidence, and no such difference would count against it. An innocent explanation is that small proposals are more often ideas their authors drop on reflection, and comparing stops by authors who pay their own costs with stops by sponsored authors of papers of similar length would tell the two apart. *[from l:settled-small-author, p:small-position, p:large-position, thm:settled]* {#c:pred-small-fade}
   1. **Corollary.** National bodies state positions on few proposals; national body comments and plenary records of national positions are the evidence, and positions on most proposals would count against it. An innocent explanation is that national bodies agree with most proposals, and comparing how often they state positions on proposals with and without Against votes in the subgroups would tell the two apart. *[from p:nb-route, f:escalate-nb, ax:hours, l:ballot-rules, thm:settled]* {#c:pred-nb-positions}
   1. **Corollary.** Papers that advance over Against votes often leave unstated the objections they faced, other than the design alternatives and declined directions that SD-4 asks papers to address; the papers and poll results are the evidence, and papers that routinely state and answer their objections would count against it. An innocent explanation is that the objections were answered in discussion, and comparing such papers with the answers subgroup chairs give at plenary when asked would tell the two apart. *[from p:record-indistinct, ax:review-cost, f:show-alternatives, f:direction-implement, thm:settled]* {#c:pred-written-answers}
1. **Corollary.** Each rule change below would remove the support for a named item, and the model predicts the stated effect. *[proved by c:lever-anonymous, c:lever-reasons-all, c:lever-sponsor-limit, c:lever-written-answer, c:lever-deadlock-rule, c:lever-ts-path, c:lever-sustained-procedure]* {#c:levers}
