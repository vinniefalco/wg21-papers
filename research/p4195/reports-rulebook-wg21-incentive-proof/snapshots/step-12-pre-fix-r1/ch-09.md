## 9. Users, quality, and disclosure

1. **Proposition.** No user votes, and taking part costs the participant or their sponsor, while a better design's gain is spread across all users and seldom turns on one user's effort. Users' interests reach decisions only through what participants themselves value, papers a participant presents, TS feedback, and national bodies, which form positions only where that is worth their members' time. *[proved by l:traced-voters, l:nb-position-time]* *[from p:who-votes, m:moves-user, ax:users-affected, ax:prefer, f:iso-early]* {#p:users-out}
   1. **Lemma.** Apart from guests, whose choices the proof takes as given, participants cast the votes in WG21's polls and national bodies cast those in the ballots on its drafts. *[from p:who-votes, f:plenary-consent, p:who-decides, m:players, d:participant, f:registered]* {#l:traced-voters}
   1. **Lemma.** A national body forms a position through its members' discussion, as when its chair caucuses with them at a meeting, so each position takes time they could give to papers. *[from f:escalate-nb, ax:hours]* {#l:nb-position-time}
1. **Proposition.** Fees are barred and everyone in a subgroup's room has one vote, yet only those who bear the cost of taking part, or whose sponsors do, vote in WG21's polls. ISO can remove inactive national body experts, and participants propose for needs they and their sponsors know, which, since users differ from them in what matters, need not match users'. *[from f:no-fee, f:remote, f:iso-cost, f:in-person-expected, f:registered, f:inactive-experts, p:who-votes, ax:cost, ax:who-pays, ax:prefer, ax:users-affected, f:present-required]* {#p:who-takes-part}
1. **Proposition.** Authors get the adoption they value at the poll, while a flaw that implementation and use have not yet exposed costs users only later, and can fall on any user of the feature. *[from ax:prefer, ax:unknown, ax:users-affected, d:author, d:user]* {#p:reward-timing}
1. **Lemma.** Implementation and use reduce uncertainty about how well a design works, though even then a room cannot fully judge it at a poll. SD-4 describes a TS or white paper as a way to gain that experience, noting that its users typically should not rely on it in production code. *[from ax:unknown, f:ts-experience, f:ts-feedback]* {#l:implementation-informs}
1. **Proposition.** Winning a consensus call and producing the design that serves users best coincide only as far as voters can judge that at the poll and no other value they hold outweighs it. SD-4 assigns no one to judge it for them. *[proved by l:merit-in-vote, l:call-from-votes]* *[from ax:prefer, ax:unknown, p:who-decides, l:consensus-test, l:implementation-informs, f:no-merit-ruling]* {#p:merit-gap}
   1. **Lemma.** A vote reflects which design serves users best only as far as the voter can judge that at the poll and weighs a good result for users above other values. *[from ax:prefer, ax:unknown, l:implementation-informs]* {#l:merit-in-vote}
   1. **Lemma.** Chairs call consensus on the votes of the participants present, counted under the 2:1 norm, and the consensus test asks nothing about whether an objection is right. *[proved by inf:calls-on-votes, inf:test-not-right]* *[from p:who-decides, l:consensus-test]* {#l:call-from-votes}
      1. The participants present decide a design's content, as chairs call consensus on their votes counted under the 2:1 norm. *[from p:who-decides]* {#inf:calls-on-votes}
      1. Under SD-4's definition, none of the three conditions under which an objection prevents consensus asks whether the objection is right. *[from l:consensus-test]* {#inf:test-not-right}
1. **Proposition.** Close study costs the reviewer, while the gain of a better design is spread across all its users. So review worth more to users together than it costs, but less to any one participant, is review no participant does better doing. *[from ax:review-cost, ax:users-affected, ax:prefer, p:review-share, d:stake, d:does-better]* {#p:review-shortfall}
1. **Proposition.** Papers approved separately at one meeting first come together when the project editor merges their wording, which the editor's report records and a pre-ballot editing committee verifies. No SD-4 step checks whether the designs work together, while each author gains from their own design's adoption and a conflict costs users and later rework. *[proved by l:separate-approval, l:editor-merge, l:conflict-costs]* *[from f:approval-polls, f:wording-baseline, f:editors, f:no-integration-check, ax:prefer, ax:unknown, ax:users-affected, ax:rework]* {#p:integration}
   1. **Lemma.** Plenary approves each wording paper in its own poll, and during the meeting each paper's wording is written against the drafts adopted at its start. *[from f:approval-polls, f:wording-baseline]* {#l:separate-approval}
   1. **Lemma.** After the meeting the project editor applies all approved changes and reports on merging them, and before a ballot an editing committee verifies they were applied correctly. *[from f:editors]* {#l:editor-merge}
   1. **Lemma.** Each author gains from their own design's adoption. A conflict between designs may surface only once they are implemented and used, and then it costs users and forces rework of what was built on them. *[from ax:prefer, ax:unknown, ax:users-affected, ax:rework]* {#l:conflict-costs}
1. **Proposition.** Stating a drawback in the paper before an early poll can cost Favor votes when incumbency is decided, while a better-informed vote's gain is spread across the design's users. SD-4 says a good paper should show the alternatives considered, but it asks no paper to state its design's drawbacks, and a paper is considered without them. *[proved by l:author-knows-more]* *[from ax:attention, p:early-polls, ax:users-affected, ax:prefer, f:show-alternatives, f:no-drawbacks-asked, f:late-paper, f:present-required]* {#p:disclosure}
   1. **Lemma.** SD-4 relies on the presenter, an expert in the paper's domain, to educate a room of general C++ experts. It also counts a feature's designers among the best experts on any concern about it. *[from f:presenter, f:concern-authors]* {#l:author-knows-more}
1. **Proposition.** No rule requires a written answer to an objection beyond three: an author's defense of declining a directed change, ISO's response to enquiry ballot comments, and a TS ballot's Disposition of Comments. For the author, a written answer stating the objection makes it easier to raise again, but also shows later what would not be new, the test SD-4 applies to repeated objections. *[proved by l:paper-answers, l:iso-comment-answers, l:answer-in-writing, l:answer-shows-new]* *[from p:record-indistinct, f:no-paper-objections, f:direction-implement, f:respond-comments, f:comment-report, ax:turnover, p:repeat-objection, f:ballot-new-info, f:ts-disposition, f:iso-no-answer, d:rules]* {#p:written-answer}
   1. **Lemma.** SD-4 asks no proposal paper to answer an objection, apart from the defense and new information an author who declines a directed change should bring. Its request that a good paper show the alternatives considered names no objection that must be among them. *[from f:no-paper-objections, f:direction-implement, f:show-alternatives]* {#l:paper-answers}
   1. **Lemma.** ISO requires responses to all comments on an enquiry ballot, with written observations on each, while it only notes comments on a final draft for future consideration. *[from f:respond-comments, f:comment-report, f:comments-noted]* {#l:iso-comment-answers}
   1. **Lemma.** A written answer to an objection states the objection it answers, and a clear written objection is easier to raise again. *[from ax:turnover]* {#l:answer-in-writing}
   1. **Lemma.** A written answer shows later readers what a repeated objection must add to bring new information. It stays available after those present have left, and records what the objection and its answer contained. *[from ax:turnover, f:ballot-new-info]* {#l:answer-shows-new}

In plain terms: no user votes, and users' interests reach decisions only through what participants value, papers a participant presents, TS feedback, and national bodies, while a better design's gain is spread across all users. Winning a consensus call and producing the design that serves users best coincide only as far as voters can judge that at the poll and weigh it above other values, and SD-4 assigns no one to judge it for them. SD-4 asks no paper to state its design's drawbacks, and no SD-4 step checks whether designs approved in separate papers work together.
