## 7. Study groups and deadlocks

1. **Proposition.** On a topic with a study group, rival designs can be polled there first, among those who chose that room over the rooms meeting with it. The design it sends on is where the design room starts, and the design room, which owns selecting designs, can still poll a rival that has a paper. *[proved by l:sg-purpose, l:sg-attendance, l:sg-start]* *[from f:vehicle, f:multiple-polls]* {#p:sg-decides}
   1. **Lemma.** SD-4 has the convenor form a study group on a design room chair's recommendation when a topic has broad interest, no unified initial proposal, and a strong chair candidate. It gives the study group the role of producing an initial proposal that can then enter the design room. *[from f:sg-formation, f:sg-initial-proposal]* {#l:sg-purpose}
   1. **Lemma.** A participant can attend only one of the rooms meeting at the same time, and votes and speaks only there. So a study group's voters are those for whom its topic outweighs the rooms meeting with it. *[from ax:parallel-rooms, ax:prefer, d:stake, f:one-vote]* {#l:sg-attendance}
   1. **Lemma.** The design room starts from the study group's initial proposal, which a rival without a paper does not block while it is an active proposal making progress. *[from l:sg-purpose, l:sg-attendance, f:no-wait-alternatives, f:sg-initial-proposal]* {#l:sg-start}
1. **Proposition.** Paying participants to attend a study group puts a sponsor's voters where the design the design room starts from is chosen. Where the other voters cast fewer Favor-plus-Against votes, fewer paid participants make up a third of the total, enough to block, or over two thirds, enough to meet the norm. *[proved by l:third-needs-half, l:two-thirds-needs-double, l:two-thirds-favor-meets]* *[from p:sg-decides, p:sponsor-votes, l:vote-count, d:follows-norm]* {#p:sg-sponsor}
   1. **Lemma.** A group casts at least a third of the Favor-plus-Against votes exactly when it casts at least half as many as all the other voters together. *[from d:positions]* {#l:third-needs-half}
   1. **Lemma.** A group casts more than two thirds of the Favor-plus-Against votes exactly when it casts more than twice as many as all the other voters together. *[from d:positions]* {#l:two-thirds-needs-double}
   1. **Lemma.** A proposal meets the 2:1 norm whenever more than two thirds of its Favor-plus-Against votes are Favor, since its Favor votes then number more than twice its Against votes. *[from f:two-to-one, d:positions]* {#l:two-thirds-favor-meets}
1. **Proposition.** When each of two groups casts at least a third of the Favor-plus-Against votes against the other's design, neither design meets the 2:1 norm. The deadlock lasts until voters change position, a design changes, the voters present change, a chair calls consensus below the norm, or the design room sends both to competing TSs. *[proved by l:count-depends, l:leave-on-call]* *[from l:vote-count, f:two-to-one, f:design-forward, f:vehicle, f:competing-ts]* {#p:deadlock}
   1. **Lemma.** The count in a poll depends only on who is present, what the poll asks, and how each person present votes on it, if at all. *[from f:one-vote, d:positions]* {#l:count-depends}
   1. **Lemma.** A design leaves a design room only when the room's chair calls consensus on the design and its ship vehicle. *[from f:design-forward, f:vehicle, f:plenary-owns]* {#l:leave-on-call}
1. **Proposition.** In a long deadlock, a group whose sponsors keep paying its costs bears less per meeting than a group paying its own way. In the case the table below describes, the first does better holding and the second yielding, whichever design is better. *[proved by l:hold-yield-holder-gains, l:hold-yield-both-hold, l:hold-yield-whole-over-half, l:hold-yield-half-over-deadlock, l:hold-yield-half-over-none, l:hold-yield-cheap-holds, l:hold-yield-costly-yields]* *[from ax:who-pays, ax:cost, p:deadlock]* {#p:attrition}
   1. **Lemma.** When one deadlocked group yields, the other holds, and no one else votes Against the holder's design, the deadlock ends with that design advancing. The holder gains its whole stake, the yielder none of its own, and neither bears the cost of holding through a deadlock. *[proved by inf:yielder-stops, inf:holder-stake, inf:no-holding-cost]* *[from p:deadlock, l:vote-count, d:stake]* {#l:hold-yield-holder-gains}
      1. A group that yields stops voting Against the other's design, so when no one else votes Against it, the holders' Favor votes meet the 2:1 norm. *[from p:deadlock, l:vote-count]* {#inf:yielder-stops}
      1. The design that advances brings the holder the difference it makes to what the holder values, its whole stake, and brings the yielder none of what its own design would. *[from d:stake, inf:yielder-stops]* {#inf:holder-stake}
      1. The deadlock then ends at the next poll, so neither group bears the cost of holding through further meetings. *[from inf:yielder-stops, ax:cost]* {#inf:no-holding-cost}
   1. **Lemma.** When both groups hold and the deadlock ends with either design equally likely, each can expect half its stake on average, less its cost of holding through it. A group's participants whose costs sponsors pay bear less of that cost per meeting. *[from p:deadlock, ax:cost, ax:who-pays, d:stake]* {#l:hold-yield-both-hold}
   1. **Lemma.** Each group does better holding while the other yields, gaining its whole stake at no cost of holding, than when both yield to a compromise worth half its stake. *[proved by inf:whole-vs-half, inf:whole-rank]* *[from l:hold-yield-holder-gains, ax:prefer, d:does-better]* {#l:hold-yield-whole-over-half}
      1. Holding while the other yields brings a group its whole stake at no cost of holding, while both yielding brings a compromise worth half its stake. *[from l:hold-yield-holder-gains]* {#inf:whole-vs-half}
      1. A whole stake is worth more than half of it, so each group ranks holding while the other yields above both yielding. *[from inf:whole-vs-half, ax:prefer, d:does-better]* {#inf:whole-rank}
   1. **Lemma.** Each group does better when both yield to a compromise worth half its stake than when both hold. That holds because a deadlock both hold brings half its stake on average, less a cost of holding. *[proved by inf:both-hold-net, inf:half-beats-deadlock]* *[from l:hold-yield-both-hold, ax:cost, ax:prefer, d:does-better]* {#l:hold-yield-half-over-deadlock}
      1. When both hold, a group can expect half its stake on average, less its cost of holding through the deadlock, a cost above zero since attending costs money and time. *[from l:hold-yield-both-hold, ax:cost]* {#inf:both-hold-net}
      1. Half its stake with no cost of holding is worth more than half its stake less such a cost, so each group ranks both yielding above both holding. *[from inf:both-hold-net, ax:prefer, d:does-better]* {#inf:half-beats-deadlock}
   1. **Lemma.** Each group does better when both yield to a compromise worth half its stake than when it yields while the other holds, which brings none of its stake. *[proved by inf:half-vs-none, inf:half-rank]* *[from l:hold-yield-holder-gains, ax:prefer, d:does-better]* {#l:hold-yield-half-over-none}
      1. Yielding while the other holds brings a group none of its stake, and both yielding brings it a compromise worth half its stake, with no cost of holding either way. *[from l:hold-yield-holder-gains]* {#inf:half-vs-none}
      1. Half its stake is worth more than none, so each group ranks both yielding above yielding while the other holds. *[from inf:half-vs-none, ax:prefer, d:does-better]* {#inf:half-rank}
   1. **Lemma.** A group whose cost of holding is less than half its stake does better holding through a deadlock both hold than yielding while the other holds. That holds because half its stake on average, less that cost, leaves it better off than none of its stake. *[proved by inf:cheap-net, inf:cheap-rank]* *[from l:hold-yield-both-hold, l:hold-yield-holder-gains, ax:prefer, d:does-better]* {#l:hold-yield-cheap-holds}
      1. Holding through a deadlock both hold brings half its stake on average less its cost of holding, and yielding while the other holds brings none of its stake. *[from l:hold-yield-both-hold, l:hold-yield-holder-gains]* {#inf:cheap-net}
      1. With that cost less than half its stake, the first is worth more than nothing, so the group ranks holding above yielding. *[from inf:cheap-net, ax:prefer, d:does-better]* {#inf:cheap-rank}
   1. **Lemma.** A group whose cost of holding is more than half its stake does better yielding while the other holds than holding through a deadlock both hold. That holds because half its stake on average, less that cost, leaves it worse off than none of its stake. *[proved by inf:costly-rank]* *[from l:hold-yield-both-hold, l:hold-yield-holder-gains, ax:prefer, d:does-better]* {#l:hold-yield-costly-yields}
      1. With that cost more than half its stake, half its stake on average less that cost is worth less than nothing, so the group ranks yielding above holding. *[from inf:cheap-net, ax:prefer, d:does-better]* {#inf:costly-rank}

*Table: Two groups in a deadlock, with equal stakes in their own designs. Group A's sponsors pay its participants' costs and value its holding above what they pay, while group B's participants pay their own. Holding through the deadlock costs group A less than half its stake and group B more than half. A deadlock both hold ends with either design equally likely, and both yielding means a compromise worth half its stake to each. First number: group A's rank. Second number: group B's rank. 4 is best. † marks a Pareto-optimal cell.* {#t:hold-or-yield}

|---|---|---|

In plain terms: on a topic with a study group, the design room starts from the design the study group sends on, and where the other voters there cast fewer Favor-plus-Against votes, fewer paid participants are enough to block or to meet the norm. When each of two groups casts at least a third of the Favor-plus-Against votes against the other's design, neither design meets the 2:1 norm, and the deadlock lasts until positions, designs, or the voters present change, a chair calls consensus below the norm, or the design room sends both to competing TSs. In a long deadlock, a group whose sponsors pay its costs bears less per meeting than a group paying its own way, and in the case the table describes, the first holds and the second yields, whichever design is better.
