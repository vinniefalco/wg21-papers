## 4. What the record keeps and how objections count

1. **Proposition.** A proposal can advance in WG21's rooms and plenary with objections unresolved, since it needs a consensus call, which the 2:1 norm normally supports, not every objector's persuasion. *[proved by l:address-not-resolve, p:advance-unanswered]* {#p:persuasion-not-required}
   1. **Lemma.** Under ISO, the obligation to address a sustained opposition is no obligation to resolve it, and a sustained opposition is not a veto. Still, ISO's general principles say consensus requires the resolution of substantial objections. *[from f:not-veto, f:resolve-substantial]* {#l:address-not-resolve}
   1. **Proposition.** A proposal can advance while discussed objections to it remain unresolved, because advancing requires a consensus call, which the 2:1 norm normally supports, and consensus need not imply unanimity. *[from l:address-not-resolve, f:design-forward, f:wording-forward, f:plenary-owns, f:two-to-one, f:consensus-def]* {#p:advance-unanswered}
1. **Proposition.** The record SD-4 and the searched ISO clauses require of a WG21 poll need not show whether an objection was answered or outvoted, or why consensus was called over it. *[proved by l:record-content, l:record-checks, l:iso-register-level]* *[from f:iso-no-reason-record]* {#p:record-indistinct}
   1. **Lemma.** SD-4 requires minutes and lets poll questions and numeric results be quoted publicly, but requires no record of how an objection was answered or why a consensus call was made. Plenary may ask a subgroup chair to summarize the reason for a decision. *[from f:minutes, f:protected, f:no-reason-record, f:plenary-reason]* {#l:record-content}
   1. **Lemma.** ISO lets participants comment on or object to minutes before they are confirmed, and has the chair of SC22 see that points of view at SC22's meetings are summarized. It bars participants from commenting publicly on others' contributions. *[from f:minutes-objections, f:views-summarized, f:no-public-comment]* {#l:record-checks}
   1. **Lemma.** The chair of SC22 judges consensus at SC22's committee stage, and ISO 2.5.7 recommends steps for a sustained opposition, including registering it, to the leadership. It does not say whether a convenor applies them inside a working group, and under either reading SD-4 adds no step for assessing or registering an opposition in subgroup polls. *[proved by f:iso-no-convenor]* *[from f:chair-judges, f:pmember-consensus, f:committee-stage, f:iso-register, f:leadership-judges, f:sustained-def, f:no-sustained-procedure, d:national-body]* {#l:iso-register-level}
      1. Clause 2.5.7 of ISO/IEC Directives Part 1 does not mention a convenor (ISO/IEC Directives Part 1, 2.5.7; searched: convenor, convener). {#f:iso-no-convenor}
1. **Proposition.** SD-4 adopts ISO's definition of consensus, which turns on sustained opposition, but names no step for judging whether an opposition is sustained or for registering one, and no criteria for an important part of the concerned interests. Whoever ISO's leadership is for a working group, SD-4's own steps are the 2:1 norm after discussion, reasons from Against voters on request, escalation, and plenary's 3-way poll and question about national positions. *[proved by f:consensus-source]* *[from f:wg-consensus, f:consensus-def, f:sustained-def, f:two-to-one, f:against-reasons, f:escalate-deadline, f:plenary-consent, f:plenary-nb, f:iso-register, f:no-sustained-procedure]* {#p:definition-without-procedure}
   1. **Fact.** "Consensus is defined as follows in the ISO/IEC Guide 2:2004, as quoted in the ISO/IEC Directives:" (SD-4, "Consensus") {#f:consensus-source}
1. **Lemma.** Under SD-4's definition, an objection prevents consensus only if it concerns a substantial issue, is maintained, and comes from an important part of the concerned interests. The leadership judges that part case by case, none of the three conditions asks whether the objection is right, and every view must still be heard. *[proved by l:leadership-assesses]* *[from f:consensus-def, f:sustained-def, f:majority-listen, f:views-heard]* {#l:consensus-test}
   1. **Lemma.** ISO has the committee leadership determine the concerned interests case by case and assess whether an opposition has been sustained by an important part of them. *[from f:concerned-interests, f:iso-register, f:leadership-judges]* {#l:leadership-assesses}
1. **Proposition.** When an objection's Against votes are too few to stop the 2:1 norm, the cheapest response keeping it met at that poll is no change or a partial change. Finding out whether those objectors are right leaves that count unchanged, and SD-4 expects any change the room directs to be made or defended with new information. *[from ax:prefer, ax:redesign-cost, ax:review-cost, l:vote-count, f:direction-implement]* {#p:least-cost-response}
1. **Proposition.** SD-4 asks an Against voter to be ready to name the changes that would win them over, or to say that none would. Concerns stated as changes give authors items to grant one at a time, while opposition in any form offers none and weighs in the poll only as a vote. *[from f:against-reasons, m:moves-author]* {#p:change-list}
1. **Proposition.** Of minutes, non-public email lists, meeting records, ballot comments, and papers, only a paper puts an objection's argument outside the password-protected materials without anyone else's help. Writing one takes time. *[from p:record-indistinct, f:protected, f:minutes-objections, f:comments-national, f:late-info, f:escalate-paper, m:moves-regular, ax:review-cost]* {#p:dissent-needs-paper}
1. **Proposition.** A proposal's case lasts for decades in its required paper, while an unwritten reason survives only with those who heard it and, once they all leave, only by rediscovery. Outside the protected records, an objection's argument outlasts its hearers only in writing, such as the objector's paper or an author's paper showing the alternative or defending a declined change. *[from f:no-paper, ax:turnover, p:dissent-needs-paper, p:record-indistinct, f:show-alternatives, f:direction-implement]* {#p:reasons-unrecorded}
1. **Proposition.** SD-4 describes an objector who presses on after a consensus call, without new information or allowed escalation, as not accepting a group decision, whether or not anyone answered it. It says the same of a national body comment repeating a decided objection, unless the comment brings new information or the body warned all along that it would vote No. *[from f:minority-accept, f:accept-decisions, f:escalation-allowed, f:timely, f:ballot-decided, f:ballot-new-info, f:no-surprise]* {#p:repeat-objection}
1. **Proposition.** SD-4 gives an objection weight at plenary only if its holder escalated it by 5pm the evening before, on the email lists and within their national body. There, by default, it is not a national position, and after the deadline even new information goes to the email lists or a ballot comment, with a discussion paper. *[from f:plenary-weight, f:escalate-deadline, f:escalate-nb, f:late-info, f:escalate-paper]* {#p:late-objection}
1. **Proposition.** No design is considered on merit alone: a proposal needs an on-time paper and a presenter, a rival needs a paper to exist, and objections need fixed forms and times. Participants learn these rules partly by attending, some only that way, and other things equal, whoever knows them has the better chance of adoption, whatever their design skill. *[from f:late-paper, f:present-required, f:no-wait-alternatives, f:one-vote, p:late-objection, f:ballot-new-info, ax:learning, m:moves]* {#p:fluency}
1. **Proposition.** Other things equal, a newcomer, lacking what earlier meetings teach, spends more effort getting a paper considered than a returning participant, though SD-4 expects everyone to know its procedures. Where WG21 runs study groups that incubate early proposals, such as EWGI and LEWGI, a newcomer's early proposal can start there. *[from ax:learning, d:newcomer, f:familiar, f:sg-initial-proposal, d:rooms]* {#p:newcomer}

In plain terms: a proposal can advance with objections unresolved, and the record SD-4 and the searched ISO clauses require of a poll need not show whether an objection was answered or why consensus was called over it. When objectors are too few to stop the 2:1 norm, the cheapest response that keeps it met at that poll is no change or a partial one, and outside the protected records an objection's argument outlasts its hearers only in writing. Objections count only in fixed forms and at fixed times, and other things equal, whoever knows these rules, some of which only attending teaches, has the better chance of adoption.
