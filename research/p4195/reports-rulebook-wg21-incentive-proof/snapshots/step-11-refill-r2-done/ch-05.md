## 5. Momentum: incumbents and the cost of reversal

1. **Induction.** Each decision built on a design adds work that reversing the design would redo or discard, and more is built after each favorable poll. So the cost of reversing a design grows as it advances, whether or not the design is wrong. *[from ax:rework, ax:prefer, d:reversal, f:design-forward, f:wording-forward, f:plenary-owns]* {#ind:reversal-grows}
1. **Proposition.** A design passing a direction poll becomes the incumbent, and a rival without a paper cannot block it, though the chair may delay it one meeting for one to arrive. A rival with a paper can be polled alongside it, the room can direct changes, and by default a TS merge proceeds with it unless an alternative is actively progressing. *[from f:no-paper, f:no-wait-alternatives, f:explore-alternatives, f:direction-implement, f:multiple-polls, f:ts-default, d:incumbent, d:polls]* {#p:incumbent-start}
1. **Proposition.** A design can become the incumbent at its first direction poll, when it has had less review than at any later meeting. From then on, every rival and every later change to it bears a reversal cost that grows as more is built on it. *[proved by l:first-meeting-poll, l:rival-is-reversal]* *[from p:incumbent-start, ind:reversal-grows]* {#p:early-polls}
   1. **Lemma.** A design room chair can take a direction poll at the meeting where a proposal is first presented, when it has had less committee review than at any later meeting. *[from m:moves-chair, ax:first-review, d:polls, d:rooms]* {#l:first-meeting-poll}
   1. **Lemma.** Adopting a rival in an incumbent's place, or changing the incumbent, replaces in whole or part a design its design room approved by a direction poll, which is a reversal. *[from d:incumbent, d:polls, d:reversal]* {#l:rival-is-reversal}
1. **Proposition.** A rival wins a voter from an incumbent only with an advantage worth more to that voter than their share of the reversal's cost. That hurdle grows with each decision built on the incumbent, which faces no such hurdle itself. *[proved by l:reversal-against-rival]* *[from ind:reversal-grows, ax:prefer, d:does-better]* {#p:challenger-bar}
   1. **Lemma.** Adopting a rival over the incumbent is a reversal, whose cost in redone or discarded work can fall on what a voter values, while keeping the incumbent reverses nothing. *[from l:rival-is-reversal, ax:rework, ax:prefer, d:reversal]* {#l:reversal-against-rival}
1. **Lemma.** A direction poll asks voters only to agree that work should continue, less than a forwarding poll asks, so it can pass where a forwarding poll would fail. Yet passing it makes the design the incumbent. *[proved by l:direction-asks-less]* *[from d:polls, f:two-to-one, d:incumbent, p:early-polls]* {#l:poll-wording}
   1. **Lemma.** A participant who would vote Favor on sending a design to the next room prefers that work on it continue rather than stop. One who doubts the design as it stands can prefer that too, since the room can direct changes. *[from d:polls, f:design-forward, f:direction-implement, ax:prefer]* {#l:direction-asks-less}
1. **Proposition.** If a poll places a proposal among the work the committee aims to include in the next IS, the proposal joins a category SD-4 has chairs take up first. A meeting's hours are fixed, so the time it then gets comes out of the time left for papers outside those categories. *[from f:prioritization, ax:hours]* {#p:roadmap-poll}
1. **Proposition.** Close study takes longer the bigger a design is, and a bigger design has more places where a defect can go unnoticed. For a reviewer whose stake does not grow with size, close study therefore stops being worth its time sooner on a big design than on a small one. *[proved by l:study-worth-stake]* *[from ax:review-cost, ax:unknown]* {#p:review-share}
   1. **Lemma.** A participant weighing their time against a poll's outcome does better studying the proposal only when the study's time is worth less to them than their stake in that poll. *[from ax:attention, ax:review-cost, ax:prefer, d:stake, d:does-better]* {#l:study-worth-stake}
1. **Proposition.** Participants who do not study a proposal typically abstain, so one costing more to study than most stakes in it is voted on mainly by those with large stakes. Unless those of them who object cast at least a third of the Favor-plus-Against votes, it can advance with no change at all. *[proved by l:low-stake-abstains, l:under-third-meets-norm]* *[from f:not-vote, p:least-cost-response]* {#p:light-review}
   1. **Lemma.** A participant for whom studying a proposal takes time worth more than their stake in its poll does better not studying it, and so typically does not vote on it. *[from l:study-worth-stake, ax:prefer, f:not-vote]* {#l:low-stake-abstains}
   1. **Lemma.** When those voting Against cast less than a third of the Favor-plus-Against votes, the Favor votes are more than twice the Against votes, so the 2:1 norm is met. *[from f:two-to-one, d:positions]* {#l:under-third-meets-norm}
1. **Proposition.** A design normally needs the 2:1 norm to advance, and once adopted, a paper or ballot comment to remove it normally needs the same, switching the default to keeping it. After a successful final ballot, design changes are expected to have near-unanimous consent, ISO bars technical changes at approval, and SD-4 calls published features much harder to update incompatibly. *[proved by l:removal-needs-norm]* *[from f:two-to-one, f:near-unanimous, f:late-changes, f:is-hard-to-change]* {#p:burden-flip}
   1. **Lemma.** Removing an adopted design is a design change, so a paper or a ballot comment must propose it. Plenary adopts the removal only as a change the subgroups recommend, normally under the 2:1 norm. *[from d:reversal, f:no-paper, f:comment-content, f:plenary-owns, f:plenary-no-design, f:two-to-one]* {#l:removal-needs-norm}
1. **Proposition.** An objector who arrives after a design has been adopted faces three obstacles at once. Removal needs a paper meeting the 2:1 norm, reversal costs more for every decision built on the design, and the record keeps its case but unwritten objections only in memory. *[from p:burden-flip, ind:reversal-grows, p:challenger-bar, p:reasons-unrecorded, p:late-objection]* {#p:late-arrival}

In plain terms: a design can become the incumbent when it has had less review than at any later meeting, and from then on a rival wins a voter only with an advantage worth more to them than their share of a reversal cost that grows with each decision built on the design. A proposal that costs more to study than most stakes in it is voted on mainly by those with large stakes, and it can advance with no change at all unless those of them who object cast at least a third of the Favor-plus-Against votes. Once a design is adopted, removing it normally needs a paper or ballot comment meeting the same 2:1 norm, so an objector who arrives late faces that norm, a reversal cost that has grown, and a record that keeps the design's case but unwritten objections only in memory.
