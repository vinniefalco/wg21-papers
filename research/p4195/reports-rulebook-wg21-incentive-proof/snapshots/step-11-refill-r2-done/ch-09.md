## 9. Users, quality, and disclosure

1. **Proposition.** No user votes, and taking part costs the participant or their sponsor, while a better design's gain is spread across all users and seldom turns on one user's effort. Users' interests reach decisions only through what participants themselves value, papers a participant presents, TS feedback, and national bodies, which form positions only where that is worth their members' time. *[proved by l:users-through-deciders, l:nb-position-time]* *[from p:who-votes, m:moves-user, ax:users-affected, ax:prefer, f:iso-early, f:escalate-nb, ax:hours]* {#p:users-out}
   1. **Lemma.** Users' interests reach WG21's decisions only through participants and national bodies, since participants' votes and consensus calls decide content, national bodies ballot on whole documents, and no user votes. *[from p:who-decides, p:who-votes]* {#l:users-through-deciders}
   1. **Lemma.** Forming a national position takes time from the national body's members. Under SD-4, a position is by default not a national position unless the national body has discussed it and determined that it is. *[from f:escalate-nb]* {#l:nb-position-time}
1. **Proposition.** Fees are barred and everyone in a subgroup's room has one vote, yet only those who bear the cost of taking part, or whose sponsors do, vote in WG21's polls. ISO can remove inactive national body experts, and participants propose for needs they and their sponsors know, which, since users differ from them in what matters, need not match users'. *[from f:no-fee, f:remote, f:iso-cost, f:in-person-expected, f:registered, f:inactive-experts, p:who-votes, ax:cost, ax:who-pays, ax:prefer, ax:users-affected, f:present-required]* {#p:who-takes-part}
1. **Proposition.** An author gains at adoption, while a flaw that implementation and use have not yet exposed costs users only after adoption, and can fall on any user of the feature. *[from ax:prefer, ax:unknown, ax:users-affected, d:author, d:user]* {#p:reward-timing}
1. **Lemma.** Implementation and use reduce uncertainty about how well a design works, though even then a room cannot fully judge it at a poll. SD-4 describes a TS or white paper as a way to gain that experience, noting that its users typically should not rely on it in production code. *[from ax:unknown, f:ts-experience, f:ts-feedback]* {#l:implementation-informs}
1. **Proposition.** Winning a consensus call and producing the design that serves users best coincide only as far as voters can judge that at the poll and weigh it above other values. SD-4 assigns no one to judge it for them. *[proved by l:merit-in-vote]* *[from ax:prefer, ax:unknown, p:who-decides, l:consensus-test, l:implementation-informs, f:no-merit-ruling]* {#p:merit-gap}
   1. **Lemma.** A vote reflects which design serves users best only as far as the voter can judge that at the poll and weighs a good result for users above other values. *[from ax:prefer, ax:unknown, l:implementation-informs]* {#l:merit-in-vote}
1. **Proposition.** Close study costs the reviewer, while the gain of a better design is spread across all its users. So review worth more to users together than it costs, but less to any one participant, is review no participant does better doing. *[from ax:review-cost, ax:users-affected, ax:prefer, p:review-share, d:stake, d:does-better]* {#p:review-shortfall}
1. **Proposition.** No step in SD-4 checks whether separately adopted designs work together, and each author gains from their own design's adoption while a conflict costs users and later rework. The nearest steps ask a good paper to fit the language's principles, have new wording written against the adopted drafts, and let a TS merge early for integration testing. *[proved by l:fit-steps]* *[from f:no-integration-check, f:principle-based, f:wording-baseline, f:early-merge, ax:prefer, ax:unknown, ax:users-affected, ax:rework]* {#p:integration}
   1. **Lemma.** Several SD-4 steps concern how a design fits the rest of C++: a good paper should show how it fits the language's principles, and new wording is written against the adopted drafts. Each project editor also applies all approved changes and reports comments that arise in merging them, and a TS can merge early for integration testing. *[from f:principle-based, f:wording-baseline, f:editors, f:early-merge]* {#l:fit-steps weakened}
1. **Lemma.** SD-4 relies on the presenter, an expert in the paper's domain, to educate a room of general C++ experts. It also counts a feature's designers among the best experts on any concern about it. *[from f:presenter, f:concern-authors]* {#l:author-knows-more}
1. **Proposition.** Stating a drawback in the paper before an early poll can cost Favor votes when incumbency is decided, while a better-informed vote's gain is spread across the design's users. SD-4 says a good paper should show the alternatives considered, but it asks no paper to state its design's drawbacks, and a paper is considered without them. *[from ax:attention, p:early-polls, l:author-knows-more, ax:users-affected, ax:prefer, f:show-alternatives, f:no-drawbacks-asked, f:late-paper, f:present-required]* {#p:disclosure}
1. **Proposition.** No rule requires a written answer to an objection, apart from an author's defense of declining a directed change and ISO's response to each enquiry ballot comment. For the author, a written answer makes the objection easier to raise again, but also shows later what would not be new, the test SD-4 applies to repeated objections. *[proved by l:paper-answers, l:iso-comment-answers, l:answer-in-writing, l:answer-shows-new]* *[from p:record-indistinct, f:no-paper-objections, f:direction-implement, f:respond-comments, f:comment-report, ax:turnover, p:repeat-objection, f:ballot-new-info]* {#p:written-answer}
   1. **Lemma.** SD-4 asks no proposal paper to answer an objection, apart from the defense and new information an author who declines a directed change should bring. Its request that a good paper show the alternatives considered names no objection that must be among them. *[from f:no-paper-objections, f:direction-implement, f:show-alternatives]* {#l:paper-answers}
   1. **Lemma.** ISO requires responses to all comments on an enquiry ballot, with written observations on each, while it only notes comments on a final draft for future consideration. *[from f:respond-comments, f:comment-report, f:comments-noted]* {#l:iso-comment-answers}
   1. **Lemma.** A written answer to an objection states the objection it answers, and a clear written objection is easier to raise again. *[from ax:turnover]* {#l:answer-in-writing}
   1. **Lemma.** A written answer shows later readers what a repeated objection must add to bring new information. It stays available after those present have left, and records what the objection and its answer contained. *[from ax:turnover, f:ballot-new-info]* {#l:answer-shows-new}

In plain terms: no user votes, so users' interests reach decisions only through what participants themselves value, papers a participant presents, TS feedback, and national bodies. The design that wins a consensus call is the one that serves users best only as far as voters can judge that at the poll and weigh it above other values. SD-4 asks no paper to state its design's drawbacks, and no rule requires a written answer to an objection apart from an author's defense of declining a directed change and ISO's response to each enquiry ballot comment.
