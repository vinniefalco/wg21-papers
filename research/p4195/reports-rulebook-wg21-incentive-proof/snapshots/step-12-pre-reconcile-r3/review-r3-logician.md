# Logician review, round 3

p:repeat-objection | overreach | f:minority-accept describes as not accepting a group decision a heard minority that says "we still object so you can't proceed" and refuses to accept the consensus, not every objector who keeps objecting. An objector who goes on disagreeing while accepting the decision falls outside the quoted text. | Narrow the subject to "an objector who, once heard, keeps insisting after a consensus call that the work cannot proceed, without new information or allowed escalation".

p:chair-default | overreach | Its children l:call-above and l:call-below, and the t:chair-call caption, establish the ranking only for a design room chair, yet p:chair-default concludes it for any chair, and inf:sg-call-default relies on it for study group chairs. | Write "a chair" for "a design room chair" in l:call-above and the t:chair-call caption, since their bases f:two-to-one and d:follows-norm cover every subgroup poll.

l:iso-comment-answers | overreach | "and no other written answer to an objection" claims more than f:iso-no-answer, which covers only the clauses it lists. f:ts-disposition says comments on a TS ballot "must be formally responded to with a Disposition of Comments paper", which the sibling l:ts-comment-answers relies on. | Narrow it to "and no other clause the proof quotes requires a written answer to an objection".

inf:oppose-no-rank | hidden premise | It says only the No counts against approval, but its basis inf:no-counts-against and l:ballot-rules count a No only when it carries technical comments, and a No without them ties with abstaining. Nothing in the table's case says this body's No carries technical comments. | State once in the t:nb-ballot caption that a No in either column carries comments of a technical nature, as f:no-conditional-yes requires on a final draft.

inf:no-gains-nothing | hidden premise | Ranking abstaining strictly above No needs this body's No to carry technical comments, since inf:no-counts-against counts only such a No, and the case never says its comments are technical. | The same single caption statement in t:nb-ballot fixes this, so no separate change is needed.

inf:bound-ts-reach | overreach | "which is all that author's gain depends on" claims more than d:design-bound, which says the author's gain depends on keeping particular features fixed but, unlike d:result-seeking, never says it depends on nothing else. A sponsor whose interests also gain from early reach to users is not excluded. | Replace the clause with "the value d:design-bound names", so that d:does-better, which l:bound-vehicle already cites, holds the author's other values equal.

inf:early-sooner | hidden premise | No cited item says a direction poll speeds the polls after it: d:polls defines the poll and f:design-forward describes forwarding, but neither makes a direction poll a step before forwarding. | Add a cited premise that an early poll brings the author the room's direction sooner, from f:negative-welcome (polls give "meaningful and actionable feedback") and f:direction-implement (directed changes come "typically for subsequent revisions of the proposal to be considered at the next meeting").

thm:settled | overreach | "A sponsor pays for participants where the decisions it values are made" drops the condition l:settled-sponsor proves, "while what they bring it is worth their cost", and is false without it for a sponsor whose participants are not worth what they cost. | Add "while what they bring it is worth their cost" to that sentence.

l:thm-pattern | hidden premise | It has each choice the Theorem leaves open "made the way that does best for its player given the others' choices" and concludes a settled pattern, but no cited item shows that such mutually best choices exist together. The open choices of different players can depend on one another, so their joint existence is an assumption. | Either add, as a stated assumption cited here, that the open choices admit a set of mutual best responses, or narrow the Theorem to "every settled pattern includes these choices", which the whatever-the-others-choose lemmas already prove.

inf:thm-review-undone | overreach | "whether to study a paper is a choice each participant makes the way that does best for them" covers non-regular participants and guests, whose choices m:players takes as given, so "that review goes undone" goes beyond what l:thm-pattern settles. | Narrow the subject to the players, "a choice each player makes the way that does best for them, so no player does that review", and match the Theorem's sentence to it.

inf:thm-no-request | hidden premise | "SD-4 never uses the word trade-off" is not supplied by f:no-drawbacks-asked, which says only that the searched terms, trade-off among them, occur in limited contexts, not that the word is absent from SD-4. | Add that absence to f:no-drawbacks-asked's search result and cite it, or else cut the clause, which l:thm-disclosure does not use.

inf:sa-tell-apart | hidden premise | "which movers and stayers polled together share" assumes movers learn after the move only what stayers learn, but no cited item rules out technical learning from the new sponsor's colleagues, which would also turn movers' votes only after the move. | State as a condition of the test that a move brings the mover no technical learning the stayers lack, or weaken "favor the model" to "fit the model and also views learned at the new sponsor".

inf:al-rise | overreach | It predicts fewer votes as revisions grow longer but drops the condition its basis inf:al-study keeps, that participants' stakes are unchanged, though stakes can rise as a proposal moves from direction polls to forwarding polls. | State once in c:pred-abstention-length that the comparison is among polls of the same form, with participants' stakes in the proposal held fixed.

inf:pa-ease | overreach | ax:review-cost says added length adds review time, so stating what later parts need costs reviewers something, and "costs reviewers little" supports at most "little reason" to omit it, not "no reason". | Write "gives little reason to omit it" here and "gives an author little reason" in inf:pa-tell-apart.

inf:sf-tell-apart | hidden premise | "Reflection made before submission" limits the other explanation to reflection before submission, which inf:sf-reflection does not, and a sponsor that stops paying after reassessing the proposal would raise stops after the loss under reflection too. | Narrow the test to sponsor losses unrelated to the proposal, such as a sponsor ending all its WG21 participation, and drop the limit to reflection before submission.

inf:nb-few | overreach | Its basis gives only that a national body forms a position where that is worth its members' time and that a ballot vote needs none, which does not make positions few rather than many. So positions on most proposals would not count against what the cited items predict. | Predict instead fewer positions where forming one costs more, which inf:nb-tell-apart already tests, and carry the change into c:pred-nb-positions and inf:nl-fewer, which rely on "few".

inf:nb-tell-apart | hidden premise | "agreement predicts no such difference" assumes bodies whose experts attend in person agree with proposals as often as others, but f:in-person-expected, which it cites, says bodies send experts in person "if they want to affect the direction of the standard". Under the agreement explanation those bodies could disagree more often and so state more positions. | Narrow the comparison to one body's positions across meetings with and without its experts present in person, which holds the body itself fixed.

inf:co-shortfall | overreach | "Close study worth less to any one participant than its cost goes undone" claims more than p:review-shortfall, which says only that no participant does better doing it, and participants whose choices m:players takes as given may still do it. | Write "is study no participant does better doing", which still supports "can lack study".

inf:ts-delay | overreach | "the cost left to an author of accepting one would be the risk" treats the two costs p:prefer-is names as the only ones, but f:ts-disposition also lists a TS's formal ballot, Disposition of Comments, and ISO editing. | Write "the cost p:prefer-is names that would remain is the risk", which still supports the falling cost c:lever-ts-path states.
