## 6. Sponsors, proposal size, and parts

1. **Proposition.** Each participant a sponsor pays to attend has a vote in the room and values its interests, and no rule limits how many it pays. SC22 may cap only the experts each national body or liaison appoints, and at SC22 itself each national body counts once. *[from p:who-votes, f:no-sponsor-cap, f:one-vote, ax:who-pays, f:experts-appointed, f:registered, f:expert-cap, f:pmember-consensus, ax:prefer, d:rules]* {#p:sponsor-votes}
1. **Proposition.** Changing the features a sponsor's interests depend on costs that sponsor rework of what it built on them, lost fit with its requirements, and more review, all still to come. The gain of a better design is spread across all the feature's users, and the same 2:1 norm decides every proposal, however many users will rely on it. *[from ax:prefer, ax:rework, ax:review-cost, ax:users-affected, f:two-to-one, d:sponsor]* {#p:sponsor-features}
1. **Induction.** Each part adopted adds to what a rival to the whole design must replace: the part itself and the work built on it, later parts that depend on it included. *[proved by l:parts-build, l:rival-replaces-parts]* *[from m:moves-author, ind:reversal-grows, d:reversal, ax:rework]* {#ind:adopted-parts}
   1. **Lemma.** An author can propose a design in papers that each propose part of it, and a later part that depends on adopted parts builds on them. *[from m:moves-author, ax:rework]* {#l:parts-build}
   1. **Lemma.** Adopting a rival to the whole design in its place replaces every part already adopted, a reversal of each, which redoes or discards the work built on it. *[from d:reversal, ind:reversal-grows]* {#l:rival-replaces-parts}
1. **Proposition.** Presenting a design in parts lets the first parts be adopted before the whole is reviewed, and each adopted part adds to what a rival to the whole must replace. Its costs include a paper, a presentation, and a set of polls for each part. *[proved by l:part-advances-alone, l:part-adds-hurdle, l:part-cost]* *[from m:moves-author, f:design-forward, f:wording-forward, f:plenary-owns, ind:adopted-parts, p:challenger-bar, ax:review-cost, f:present-required]* {#p:parts}
   1. **Lemma.** Each part's paper advances on its own consensus calls in the design and wording rooms and at plenary, so an early part can be adopted before later parts are presented. *[from m:moves-author, f:design-forward, f:wording-forward, f:plenary-owns]* {#l:part-advances-alone}
   1. **Lemma.** Each adopted part is one more part a rival to the whole must replace, raising the advantage the rival needs to win a voter. *[proved by inf:part-replace, inf:part-voter-share]* *[from ind:adopted-parts, p:challenger-bar]* {#l:part-adds-hurdle}
      1. A rival to the whole design must replace every part already adopted, so each adopted part adds work its reversal must redo or discard. *[from ind:adopted-parts, d:reversal]* {#inf:part-replace}
      1. A rival wins a voter only with an advantage worth more than that voter's share of the reversal's cost, so a costlier reversal raises the advantage it needs. *[from p:challenger-bar, inf:part-replace, ax:rework, ax:hours]* {#inf:part-voter-share}
   1. **Lemma.** Each part needs its own paper, which takes time to write, and its own presenter at each meeting where it is considered. It also needs its own polls in the design and wording rooms and at plenary. *[from ax:review-cost, f:present-required, f:design-forward, f:wording-forward, f:plenary-owns]* {#l:part-cost}
1. **Proposition.** A first part fixing a design's structure can be adopted before reviewers can study in detail what later parts need, and every rival to that structure must then replace it. SD-4 asks no paper to state those needs, so a design-bound author does best proposing first the smallest part that fixes the structure and can pass. *[proved by l:first-part-fix, l:first-part-foresight, l:first-part-oppose, l:first-part-smallest]* *[from ax:first-review, f:no-parts-step, ind:adopted-parts, l:vote-count]* {#p:first-part}
   1. **Lemma.** A design-bound author does better when its first part fixes the features its gain depends on, as the structure later parts build on, than when a later part fixes them. The first part is adopted sooner, and every later part that depends on it adds to the cost of the reversal a rival would need. *[proved by inf:fix-sooner, inf:fix-hurdle, inf:fix-rank]* *[from d:design-bound, p:parts, ind:adopted-parts, ind:reversal-grows, p:challenger-bar, ax:prefer, d:does-better]* {#l:first-part-fix}
      1. Parts advance one paper at a time, so the first part can be adopted before any later part, fixing its features sooner. *[from p:parts]* {#inf:fix-sooner}
      1. Every later part that depends on the first builds on it, so each adds to the cost of the reversal that changing the first part's features would need. *[from ind:adopted-parts, ind:reversal-grows, p:challenger-bar]* {#inf:fix-hurdle}
      1. A design-bound author's gain depends on those features staying fixed, so it does better when they are fixed sooner and changing them needs a costlier reversal. *[from inf:fix-sooner, inf:fix-hurdle, d:design-bound, ax:prefer, d:does-better]* {#inf:fix-rank}
   1. **Lemma.** At the first part's polls, only those who foresee the rest can base their vote on what the later parts will need of it. Others cannot study that in detail until those parts are presented, and SD-4 asks no paper to state it. *[from ax:first-review, ax:attention, f:no-parts-step]* {#l:first-part-foresight}
   1. **Lemma.** Against votes on the first part can keep it below the 2:1 line, while removing it once adopted needs a paper or ballot comment meeting the norm. So an opponent who foresees the rest does better voting Against the first part than waiting to seek its removal after adoption. *[proved by inf:oppose-early-count, inf:oppose-late-burden, inf:oppose-early-rank]* *[from l:vote-count, p:burden-flip, ax:prefer, d:does-better, d:follows-norm]* {#l:first-part-oppose}
      1. Against votes making up at least a third of the Favor-plus-Against votes on the first part keep it from meeting the 2:1 norm. *[from l:vote-count]* {#inf:oppose-early-count}
      1. Once the first part is adopted, removing it needs a paper or ballot comment that itself meets the 2:1 norm. *[from p:burden-flip, d:follows-norm]* {#inf:oppose-late-burden}
      1. Blocking the first part takes a third of the Favor-plus-Against votes, while removing it later takes over two thirds, so the opponent does better voting Against early. *[from inf:oppose-early-count, inf:oppose-late-burden, l:vote-count, ax:prefer, d:does-better]* {#inf:oppose-early-rank}
   1. **Lemma.** Of two first parts that fix the same features and meet the 2:1 norm against opponents who foresee the rest, the smaller is ready and reviewed sooner. So a design-bound author whose gain depends on those features does better with the smaller, which can fix them sooner. *[from ax:review-cost, ax:hours, d:design-bound, ax:prefer, d:does-better, ind:adopted-parts]* {#l:first-part-smallest}
1. **Proposition.** A large proposal has more sponsored authors in the room than a small one, and more expected meetings where a poll can make it the incumbent or end its incumbency. *[from d:large, d:small, d:incumbent, p:sponsor-votes]* {#p:large-position}
1. **Proposition.** A small proposal's author bears every meeting's cost alone, and outside SD-4's priority categories the proposal gets agenda time only after the priority work. Where a study group incubates proposals on its topic, a small proposal can start there. *[from d:small, ax:cost, f:prioritization, f:agenda-papers, f:sg-initial-proposal, d:rooms]* {#p:small-position}
1. **Proposition.** Each result here about large or small proposals rests on an attribute their definitions name: who pays the authors' costs, how many authors, review's expected span, or paper length. So it holds for any proposal with that attribute, whatever its size. *[from d:small, d:large, p:large-position, p:small-position]* {#p:size-attributes}

In plain terms: each participant a sponsor pays to attend has a vote in the room and values its interests, and no rule limits how many it pays. A design-bound author's best first paper is the smallest part that fixes the design's structure and still meets the 2:1 norm against opponents who foresee the rest. A small proposal's author bears every meeting's cost alone, and each result here about large or small proposals holds for any proposal with the attribute it rests on.
