## 5. Incumbents and the cost of reversal

1. **Induction.** Each decision built on a design adds work that reversing the design would redo or discard, and more is built after each favorable poll. So the cost of reversing a design grows as it advances, whether or not the design is wrong. *[from ax:rework, ax:prefer, d:reversal, f:design-forward, f:wording-forward, f:plenary-owns]* {#ind:reversal-grows}
1. **Proposition.** A design passing a design room's direction poll becomes the incumbent, a paperless rival cannot block it, and the chair may delay it one meeting for a paper to arrive. A rival with a paper can be polled alongside it, the room can direct changes, and by default a TS merge proceeds with it unless an alternative is actively progressing. *[proved by l:incumbent-by-poll, l:paperless-rival, l:rival-with-paper, l:ts-merge-default]* *[from f:no-paper, f:no-wait-alternatives, f:explore-alternatives, f:direction-implement, f:multiple-polls, f:ts-default, d:incumbent, d:polls]* {#p:incumbent-start}
   1. **Lemma.** A design that passes a direction poll in a design room is the incumbent on its topic until it fails a later direction poll there. *[from d:incumbent, d:polls]* {#l:incumbent-by-poll}
   1. **Lemma.** A rival without a paper does not block an active proposal making progress, though the design room chair may delay the proposal one meeting for the rival's paper. *[from f:no-paper, f:no-wait-alternatives]* {#l:paperless-rival}
   1. **Lemma.** A rival with a paper can be polled alongside the incumbent, which SD-4 encourages, and the room can direct changes, which the author should make or defend declining. *[from f:multiple-polls, f:explore-alternatives, f:direction-implement]* {#l:rival-with-paper}
   1. **Lemma.** When a TS is ready to merge into the IS and no alternative is actively progressing, SD-4's default is to move forward with the proposal it has. *[from f:ts-default]* {#l:ts-merge-default}
1. **Proposition.** A design can become the incumbent at its first direction poll, when it has had less review than at any later meeting. From then on, a rival or a later change must redo or discard work built on it, at a cost that grows as more is built. *[proved by l:first-meeting-poll, l:rival-reversal]* *[from ax:first-review, ax:unknown, d:incumbent, d:polls, p:incumbent-start, ind:reversal-grows, d:reversal, ax:rework]* {#p:early-polls}
   1. **Lemma.** When a proposal is first presented to the committee in a design room, the chair may take a direction poll on it at that meeting. *[from m:moves-chair, f:chair-polls, d:polls, d:rooms]* {#l:first-meeting-poll}
   1. **Lemma.** Adopting a rival in an incumbent's place replaces what a design room approved in a direction poll, so it is a reversal. *[from d:incumbent, d:polls, d:reversal]* {#l:rival-reversal}
1. **Proposition.** A rival wins a voter from an incumbent only with an advantage worth more to that voter than their share of the reversal's cost. The advantage a rival needs grows with each decision built on the incumbent, while keeping the incumbent needs none. *[proved by l:only-rival-reverses, l:rival-net]* *[from ax:prefer, ind:reversal-grows, p:incumbent-start, d:incumbent, d:reversal, d:does-better, ax:rework]* {#p:challenger-bar}
   1. **Lemma.** Keeping an incumbent removes or replaces nothing, so of the two choices only adopting a rival in its place is a reversal. *[from l:rival-reversal, d:reversal, d:incumbent]* {#l:only-rival-reverses}
   1. **Lemma.** A participant does better with a rival than with the incumbent only when the rival's advantage to them outweighs their share of the reversal's cost, which keeping the incumbent avoids. *[from l:only-rival-reverses, ax:rework, ax:hours, ax:prefer, d:does-better]* {#l:rival-net}
1. **Lemma.** A direction poll asks voters only to agree that work should continue, less than a forwarding poll asks, so it can pass where a forwarding poll would fail. Passing it in a design room makes the design the incumbent. *[proved by l:direction-asks-less, l:design-room-incumbent]* *[from d:polls, d:incumbent, f:design-forward, ax:prefer, p:early-polls]* {#l:poll-wording}
   1. **Lemma.** A participant who would vote Favor on sending a design to the next room prefers that work on it continue rather than stop. One who doubts the design as it stands can prefer that too, since the room can direct changes. *[from d:polls, f:design-forward, f:direction-implement, ax:prefer]* {#l:direction-asks-less}
   1. **Lemma.** Passing a direction poll in a design room makes a design the incumbent, while passing one in a study group does not. *[from d:incumbent, d:rooms]* {#l:design-room-incumbent}
1. **Proposition.** If a poll places a proposal among the work the committee aims to include in the next edition, the proposal joins a category SD-4 has chairs take up first. A meeting's hours are fixed, so the time it then gets comes out of the time left for papers outside those categories. *[from f:prioritization, ax:hours]* {#p:roadmap-poll}
1. **Proposition.** Close study takes longer the bigger a design is, and a bigger design has more places where a defect can go unnoticed. For a reviewer whose stake does not grow with size, close study therefore stops being worth its time sooner on a big design than on a small one. *[proved by l:study-worth]* *[from ax:review-cost, ax:attention, ax:unknown, ax:prefer, d:stake, d:does-better]* {#p:review-share}
   1. **Lemma.** Studying a proposal affects its poll only through what the participant learns, which can change their vote or what they say in discussion. So what studying brings them through that poll is worth no more than their stake in it. *[from ax:attention, f:negative-welcome, d:stake]* {#l:study-worth}
1. **Proposition.** Since those who skip a paper and its discussion typically abstain, those with large stakes cast most votes on a proposal costing more to read than most stakes in it. Unless those of them who object cast at least a third of the Favor-plus-Against votes, it can advance with no change at all. *[proved by l:skip-study, l:under-third]* *[from f:not-vote, p:review-share, ax:review-cost, ax:prefer, l:vote-count, p:least-cost-response]* {#p:light-review}
   1. **Lemma.** A participant whose stake in a proposal is worth less than the time reading its paper and following its discussion takes does better doing neither. *[from l:study-worth, f:not-vote, ax:review-cost, ax:prefer, d:does-better]* {#l:skip-study}
   1. **Lemma.** When fewer than a third of the Favor-plus-Against votes are Against, Favor votes are more than twice the Against votes, which meets the 2:1 norm. *[from f:two-to-one, d:positions]* {#l:under-third}
1. **Proposition.** A design needs the 2:1 norm to advance, and once adopted, a paper or ballot comment to remove it needs the same, switching the default to keeping it. After a successful final ballot, design changes are expected to have near-unanimous consent, ISO bars technical changes at approval, and SD-4 calls published features much harder to update incompatibly. *[proved by l:removal-routes, l:removal-polls]* *[from f:two-to-one, f:no-paper, f:comment-content, f:near-unanimous, f:late-changes, f:is-hard-to-change, d:reversal, d:follows-norm]* {#p:burden-flip}
   1. **Lemma.** Removing an adopted design is a design change, so a proposal to do it needs a paper, and a national body can also request it in a ballot comment. *[from f:no-paper, f:comment-content, m:moves-nb]* {#l:removal-routes}
   1. **Lemma.** Design rooms own selecting designs and plenary does not change them, so a removal, by paper or comment, needs the 2:1 norm in a design room. *[from f:vehicle, f:plenary-no-design, f:two-to-one, l:removal-routes, d:follows-norm]* {#l:removal-polls}
1. **Proposition.** Three things hold at once for an objector who arrives after a design has been adopted. Removal needs a paper or ballot comment meeting the 2:1 norm, reversal costs more as more is built, and the record keeps its case but unwritten objections only in memory. *[proved by l:late-removal, l:late-reversal, l:late-record]* *[from p:burden-flip, ind:reversal-grows, p:challenger-bar, p:reasons-unrecorded, p:late-objection]* {#p:late-arrival}
   1. **Lemma.** Once a design is adopted, removing it needs a paper or ballot comment meeting the 2:1 norm, which switches the default to keeping it. *[from p:burden-flip, d:follows-norm]* {#l:late-removal}
   1. **Lemma.** Removing or replacing an adopted design is a reversal, whose cost grows with every decision built on it. A rival wins a voter only with an advantage worth more than their share of that cost. *[from ind:reversal-grows, p:challenger-bar]* {#l:late-reversal}
   1. **Lemma.** The design's case lasts in its paper, while objections no one wrote down survive only with those who heard them. *[from p:reasons-unrecorded]* {#l:late-record}

In plain terms: a design can become the incumbent at its first direction poll, when least reviewed; rivals or later changes must redo or discard work built on it, at a growing cost. A proposal costing more to read than most stakes in it can advance unchanged unless its objectors cast at least a third of the Favor-plus-Against votes. After adoption, a paper or ballot comment to remove a design needs the same 2:1 norm it needed to advance, switching the default to keeping it.
