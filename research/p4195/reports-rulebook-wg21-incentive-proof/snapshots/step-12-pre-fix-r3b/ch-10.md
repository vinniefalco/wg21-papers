## 10. The schedule and national bodies

1. **Proposition.** A proposal that misses an edition's feature cutoff waits at least for the next edition, planned three years later. It can ship sooner as a white paper, which has no prescribed schedule, and the planned schedule keeps an unfinished feature from delaying the features that are ready. *[proved by l:cutoff]* *[from f:white-paper-process, f:plenary-owns, ax:schedule, d:edition]* {#p:miss-cost}
   1. **Lemma.** Once an edition's draft goes to ballot, SD-4 treats a ballot comment that requests adding a feature as out of scope, and WG21 plans the next edition three years later. *[from f:ballot-no-new-features, d:edition, ax:schedule]* {#l:cutoff}
1. **Proposition.** At the feature cutoff, a one-meeting delay for a paperless rival sends a proposal to the next planned edition. So does an Against vote that keeps its count from meeting the 2:1 norm. *[proved by l:forward-by-cutoff, l:norm-forwarding]* *[from p:miss-cost, f:no-wait-alternatives, l:vote-count, f:two-to-one, f:plenary-owns, d:edition, d:follows-norm]* {#p:cutoff-effect}
   1. **Lemma.** Plenary adopts only changes the design and wording rooms recommend, so a proposal is approved for an edition only if those rooms forward it by the edition's feature cutoff. *[from f:plenary-owns, f:design-forward, f:wording-forward, d:edition]* {#l:forward-by-cutoff}
   1. **Lemma.** A chair who follows the 2:1 norm forwards a proposal only on a count above 2:1 after discussion. *[from d:follows-norm, d:positions, f:design-forward, f:wording-forward, d:rooms]* {#l:norm-forwarding}
1. **Proposition.** A TS delays IS adoption by about one to two years, and its experience can bring incompatible changes first: a gain to result-seeking authors, a risk to design-bound ones. It reaches implementers and users early, can publish while concerns remain if their holders agree, and the design room can require it or the white paper ISO and SC22 recommend. *[proved by l:ts-result-seeking, l:ts-design-bound, l:ts-early]* *[from f:ts-lag, f:ts-experience, f:ts-feedback, f:is-hard-to-change, f:vehicle, f:white-paper, f:white-paper-process, l:implementation-informs, d:result-seeking, d:design-bound, ax:prefer, f:ts-unblock]* {#p:prefer-is}
   1. **Lemma.** An incompatible change that fixes a shortcoming found in implementing and using a TS makes the adopted design serve its users better, which raises a result-seeking author's gain. *[from f:ts-experience, f:ts-feedback, l:implementation-informs, d:result-seeking, ax:prefer]* {#l:ts-result-seeking}
   1. **Lemma.** An incompatible change made after implementing and using a TS can alter the features a design-bound author's gain depends on keeping fixed. *[from f:ts-experience, f:ts-feedback, d:design-bound]* {#l:ts-design-bound}
   1. **Lemma.** A TS unblocks early implementers and users and can be published while strong technical concerns remain, if those holding them are willing to publish without the changes they suggest. *[from f:ts-unblock]* {#l:ts-early}
1. **Lemma.** Every national body may vote on enquiry and final ballots, which pass with two thirds of P-members' counted votes positive and at most a quarter of all counted votes negative. Abstentions and negative votes without technical comments go uncounted, ISO encourages abstaining without national consensus or expert input, sustained opposition is inapplicable, and a failed final draft returns to SC22. *[from f:all-nbs-vote, f:enquiry-count, f:iso-ballot, f:no-conditional-yes, f:nb-abstain, f:not-ballots, f:failed-fdis, d:national-body]* {#l:ballot-rules}
1. **Proposition.** A national body's No opposes every feature in the document at once, including ones its own experts support, while WG21 considers its comments whatever its vote. In the table's cases, a body valuing the rest of a document does better voting Yes with comments, and one wanting the work in no form does better voting No. *[proved by l:rank-rest-yes, l:rank-rest-abstain, l:rank-oppose-no, l:rank-oppose-abstain]* *[from p:who-decides, f:experts-personal, f:no-means-no, f:respond-comments, f:comments-noted, l:ballot-rules, ax:prefer, d:does-better]* {#p:nb-comment}
   1. **Lemma.** A body that values the rest of a document, as the table below defines it, ranks Yes with comments above abstaining. Only the Yes counts toward the approval it prefers, and its comments fare the same either way. *[proved by inf:yes-counts, inf:comments-either-way]* *[from l:ballot-rules, f:no-means-no, ax:prefer]* {#l:rank-rest-yes}
      1. Abstentions are not counted, so of the two only Yes with comments adds to the positive P-member votes approval needs. *[from l:ballot-rules]* {#inf:yes-counts}
      1. Such a body expects its comments to fare the same under any vote and cannot rule out that its vote decides the result, so it ranks Yes above abstaining. *[from f:no-means-no, inf:yes-counts, ax:prefer]* {#inf:comments-either-way}
   1. **Lemma.** Such a body ranks abstaining above No, since only the No counts against the approval it prefers, and in this case its comments gain nothing from a No. *[proved by inf:no-counts-against, inf:no-gains-nothing]* *[from l:ballot-rules, f:no-means-no, ax:prefer]* {#l:rank-rest-abstain}
      1. A No with technical comments counts against approval, while an abstention is not counted at all. *[from l:ballot-rules]* {#inf:no-counts-against}
      1. Such a body expects its comments to fare the same under any vote and cannot rule out that its vote decides the result, so it ranks abstaining above No. *[from f:no-means-no, inf:no-counts-against, ax:prefer]* {#inf:no-gains-nothing}
   1. **Lemma.** A body that wants the work in no form, as the table below defines it, ranks No above abstaining. Only the No counts against approval, which is what SD-4 says No is for. *[proved by inf:oppose-no-use, inf:oppose-no-rank]* *[from l:ballot-rules, f:ballot-no, ax:prefer]* {#l:rank-oppose-no}
      1. SD-4 says a No vote should express that the national body does not want the work to progress at all in any form. *[from f:ballot-no]* {#inf:oppose-no-use}
      1. Such a body cannot rule out that its vote decides the result, and of No and abstaining, only the No counts against approval, so it ranks No first. *[from inf:no-counts-against, ax:prefer]* {#inf:oppose-no-rank}
   1. **Lemma.** Such a body ranks abstaining above Yes with comments, since only the Yes counts toward approval of work it wants in no form. *[proved by inf:oppose-abstain-rank]* *[from l:ballot-rules, f:ballot-no, ax:prefer]* {#l:rank-oppose-abstain}

1. **Proposition.** A national body whose comments fare the same under any vote does better voting No over one feature only if it values removing it above the rest on schedule. SD-4 expects such a body to have told the committee all along, and ISO asks every national body to make its standpoint clear early. *[from p:nb-comment, f:ballot-no, f:no-surprise, f:timely, f:iso-early, ax:prefer, d:does-better, f:no-means-no]* {#p:credible-threat}
1. **Proposition.** A national body forms its position by its panel's own rules, which SD-4 does not state, so whether a sponsor's paid participants add weight there depends on those rules. In the United States, INCITS gives each organization one voting membership, with a Defense Department exception, no vote to consultants a voting member funds, and binds delegates to official positions. *[proved by l:incits-votes, l:incits-positions]* *[from f:late-info, f:escalate-nb, ax:who-pays]* {#p:nb-members}
   1. **Lemma.** INCITS gives each organization one voting membership, the Defense Department up to four on technical committees and groups, and on its Executive Board counts a controlled entity with its controller. It admits a consultant as a voting member only if no voting member funds it, and lets standards developing organizations and consortia be represented by other voting members' employees. *[from f:incits-one-vote, f:incits-organization, f:incits-limits, f:incits-control]* {#l:incits-votes}
   1. **Lemma.** INCITS's Executive Board approves the United States position that goes to ANSI, and every United States delegate must support an official position, which the TAG may set before a meeting. At an international plenary, the delegation may determine the position on a new issue by caucus consensus. *[from f:incits-position, f:incits-delegates, f:incits-caucus]* {#l:incits-positions}

In plain terms: at the feature cutoff, a one-meeting delay for a paperless rival, or a count at or below 2:1, sends a proposal to the next planned edition. A national body's No opposes every feature at once, so in the table's case only a body valuing one feature's removal above the rest on schedule does better voting No. Whether a sponsor's paid participants add weight to a national body's position depends on its panel's own rules.
