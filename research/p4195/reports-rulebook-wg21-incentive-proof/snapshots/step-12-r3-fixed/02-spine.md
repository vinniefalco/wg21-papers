## 2. Who decides, and with what moves

1. **Proposition.** The participants present decide a design's content: chairs call consensus on their votes, counted under the 2:1 norm, in the design rooms and the study groups that send them proposals. SD-4 assigns no one to rule on a design's quality, wording rooms check only that wording expresses the design, and national bodies vote on each balloted draft as a whole. *[from f:one-vote, f:two-to-one, f:design-forward, f:plenary-owns, f:plenary-no-design, f:sg-initial-proposal, f:vehicle, f:no-merit-ruling, f:spec-subgroups, f:wording-forward, f:wording-refer, f:iso-ballot, f:ballot-no, f:no-conditional-yes, d:rooms, d:follows-norm]* {#p:who-decides}
1. **Proposition.** Each person in a subgroup's room has one vote, guests a convenor invites included, while no user votes in any WG21 poll or in the ballots on its drafts. *[from f:one-vote, f:guests, f:all-nbs-vote, f:iso-ballot, d:participant, d:user, d:national-body, f:plenary-polls]* {#p:who-votes}
1. **Proposition.** The players are result-seeking authors, design-bound authors, regulars, study group chairs, design room chairs, the convenor, national bodies, sponsors, and users. The proof takes as given SD-4's text, the convenor's identity, appointments, study groups, and guest invitations, and the choices of wording room chairs, guests, non-regular participants, and the direction group. *[from p:who-decides, p:who-votes, d:settled-pattern, d:author, d:result-seeking, d:design-bound, d:regular, d:rooms, d:national-body, d:sponsor, d:user]* {#m:players}
1. **Proposition.** Each player has these moves under the rules. *[proved by m:moves-author, m:moves-regular, m:moves-chair, m:moves-convenor, m:moves-nb, m:moves-sponsor, m:moves-user]* {#m:moves}

## 3. What a vote costs

1. **Lemma.** Under the 2:1 norm each Against vote raises by two the Favor votes a proposal needs, while Neutral votes and abstentions leave the count unchanged. One Against vote changes the result only at one or two votes above the 2:1 threshold, and at least a third of Favor-plus-Against votes cast Against keeps the norm unmet. *[proved by l:against-double, l:neutral-outside, l:room-size, l:one-third-against]* {#l:vote-count}
   1. **Lemma.** Under the 2:1 norm, each Against vote raises by two the number of Favor votes a proposal needs. *[from f:two-to-one, d:positions]* {#l:against-double}
   1. **Lemma.** The 2:1 comparison counts only Favor and Against votes, and SD-4 says a Neutral majority of those voting can be concerning. *[from f:two-to-one, f:not-vote, d:positions]* {#l:neutral-outside}
   1. **Lemma.** One more Against vote changes whether the 2:1 norm is met only when the Favor votes exceed twice the other Against votes by one or two. *[from f:two-to-one, d:positions]* {#l:room-size}
   1. **Lemma.** Any group casting at least one third of the Favor-plus-Against votes as Against prevents the 2:1 norm from being met. *[from f:two-to-one, d:positions]* {#l:one-third-against}
1. **Proposition.** For a regular, an Against vote on a colleague's proposal costs more than a Neutral vote or none. It risks the colleague's later support at least as much, and SD-4 tells only Against voters to be ready to give reasons, which take time to prepare. *[from ax:support, ax:repeat, ax:prefer, ax:review-cost, f:against-reasons, f:no-reasons-other, d:regular, d:standing]* {#p:against-costs-more}
1. **Proposition.** On a colleague's proposal they doubt, a regular does better voting Against only when the change that vote can make outweighs the standing and time it costs. Counted alone, an Against vote changes the result only at one or two votes above the 2:1 threshold, but discussing its concerns can change other votes or the design. *[from l:vote-count, p:against-costs-more, ax:prefer, f:against-reasons, f:direction-implement, d:stake, d:does-better, f:two-to-one, ax:attention]* {#p:doubt-outside-count}
1. **Proposition.** With many Favor votes cast, one objector's Against vote changes the result only if enough others also vote Against, so its worth to each objector depends on the others' votes. The same objectors can then form either of two settled patterns: just enough of them vote Against to block, or none does. *[from l:vote-count, p:doubt-outside-count, p:against-costs-more, d:stake, d:settled-pattern]* {#p:objector-coordination}
1. **Proposition.** A participant can argue for another design yet vote Neutral, which leaves the count unchanged and, unlike Against, is a vote SD-4 asks no one to be ready to explain. *[from f:negative-welcome, l:vote-count, f:against-reasons, f:no-reasons-other]* {#p:prefer-vs-stop}
1. **Proposition.** Two regulars with pending proposals each risk the other's support for their own by opposing the other's proposal. If each one's doubt is worth less than that risk and the time for reasons, each does better refraining whatever the other does, and both do better than both opposing. *[proved by l:mutual-refrain, l:mutual-own-vs-other]* *[from ax:support, ax:prefer, p:against-costs-more, p:doubt-outside-count, l:vote-count, d:does-better]* {#p:mutual-support}
   1. **Lemma.** In the case the table below describes, opposing the other's proposal costs each regular more in standing and time than it can change. So each ranks refraining above opposing, whatever the other does. *[from p:doubt-outside-count, ax:support]* {#l:mutual-refrain}
   1. **Lemma.** In that case, each regular ranks opposing while the other refrains above refraining while the other opposes, since the other's opposition costs them more than their own opposing does. *[from ax:prefer, d:does-better]* {#l:mutual-own-vs-other}

## 4. What the record keeps and how objections count

1. **Proposition.** A proposal can advance through WG21 with objections unresolved, since it needs consensus calls, under the 2:1 norm in subgroups and by the convenor at plenary, not every objector's persuasion. *[proved by l:address-not-resolve, p:advance-unanswered]* {#p:persuasion-not-required}
   1. **Lemma.** Under ISO, the obligation to address a sustained opposition is no obligation to resolve it, and a sustained opposition is not a veto. Still, ISO's general principles say consensus requires the resolution of substantial objections. *[from f:not-veto, f:resolve-substantial]* {#l:address-not-resolve}
   1. **Proposition.** A proposal can advance with discussed objections unresolved, because it advances on consensus calls under the 2:1 norm in subgroups and by the convenor at plenary. Consensus need not imply unanimity. *[from l:address-not-resolve, f:design-forward, f:wording-forward, f:plenary-owns, f:two-to-one, f:consensus-def]* {#p:advance-unanswered}
1. **Proposition.** The record SD-4 and the searched ISO clauses require of a WG21 poll need not show whether an objection was answered or outvoted, or why consensus was called over it. *[proved by l:record-content, l:record-checks, l:iso-register-level]* *[from f:iso-no-reason-record, f:iso-no-answer]* {#p:record-indistinct}
   1. **Lemma.** SD-4 requires minutes and lets poll questions and numeric results be quoted publicly, but requires no record of how an objection was answered or why a consensus call was made. Plenary may ask a subgroup chair to summarize the reason for a decision. *[from f:minutes, f:protected, f:no-reason-record, f:plenary-reason]* {#l:record-content}
   1. **Lemma.** ISO lets participants comment on or object to minutes before they are confirmed, and has the chair of SC22 see that points of view at SC22's meetings are summarized. It bars participants from commenting publicly on others' contributions. *[from f:minutes-objections, f:views-summarized, f:no-public-comment]* {#l:record-checks}
   1. **Lemma.** The chair of SC22 judges consensus at SC22's committee stage, and ISO 2.5.7 recommends steps for a sustained opposition, including registering it, to the leadership. It does not say whether a convenor applies them inside a working group, and under either reading SD-4 adds no step for assessing or registering an opposition in subgroup polls. *[from f:chair-judges, f:pmember-consensus, f:committee-stage, f:iso-register, f:leadership-judges, f:sustained-def, f:no-sustained-procedure, d:national-body]* {#l:iso-register-level}
1. **Proposition.** SD-4 adopts ISO's definition of consensus, marked by no sustained opposition, but names no step for judging or registering one, or criteria for an important part of the concerned interests. SD-4's steps for an objection include the 2:1 norm, Against voters' reasons on request, escalation, and plenary's 3-way poll and national-position question. *[from f:wg-consensus, f:consensus-def, f:sustained-def, f:two-to-one, f:against-reasons, f:escalate-deadline, f:plenary-consent, f:plenary-nb, f:iso-register, f:no-sustained-procedure]* {#p:definition-without-procedure}
1. **Lemma.** Under SD-4's definition, an objection prevents consensus as a sustained opposition only if it concerns a substantial issue, is maintained, and comes from an important part of the concerned interests. The leadership judges that part case by case, none of the three conditions asks whether the objection is right, and the process must seek to reconcile conflicting arguments. *[from f:consensus-def, f:sustained-def, f:concerned-interests, f:majority-listen, f:views-heard]* {#l:consensus-test}
1. **Proposition.** When an objection's Against votes are too few to keep the 2:1 norm unmet, the cheapest response keeping it met at that poll is no change or a partial change. The count measures votes, not whether the objectors are right, and SD-4 expects any change the room directs to be made or defended with new information. *[from ax:prefer, ax:redesign-cost, ax:review-cost, l:vote-count, f:direction-implement]* {#p:least-cost-response}
1. **Proposition.** SD-4 asks an Against voter to be ready to name the changes that would change their position, or to say that none would. Concerns stated as changes give authors items to grant one at a time, while opposition in any form offers none and counts in the poll only as a vote. *[from f:against-reasons, m:moves-author]* {#p:change-list}
1. **Proposition.** Of minutes, non-public email lists, records of subgroup discussion, ballot comments, and papers, only a paper puts an objection's argument outside the password-protected materials without anyone else's help. Writing one takes time. *[from p:record-indistinct, f:protected, f:minutes-objections, f:comments-national, f:late-info, f:escalate-paper, m:moves-regular, ax:review-cost]* {#p:dissent-needs-paper}
1. **Proposition.** A proposal's case lasts for decades in its required paper, while an unwritten reason is kept only in the memory of those who heard it. SD-4 asks whoever raises a concern to see an issue filed or a paper written, and outside the protected records only writing keeps an objection's argument after its hearers leave. *[from f:no-paper, ax:turnover, p:dissent-needs-paper, p:record-indistinct, f:show-alternatives, f:direction-implement, f:report-followup]* {#p:reasons-unrecorded}
1. **Proposition.** In SD-4's terms, a heard objector insisting after a consensus call that work cannot proceed, without new information or allowed escalation, is not accepting a group decision, answered or not. SD-4 describes likewise a ballot comment repeating an objection decided with its national body present and heard, unless it brings new information or the body always warned of a No. *[from f:minority-accept, f:accept-decisions, f:escalation-allowed, f:timely, f:ballot-decided, f:ballot-new-info, f:no-surprise]* {#p:repeat-objection}
1. **Proposition.** SD-4 gives an objection weight at plenary only if its holder escalated it during the week, on the email lists by 5pm the evening before and within their national body. There, by default, it is not a national position, and after the deadline even new information goes to the email lists or a ballot comment, with a discussion paper. *[from f:plenary-weight, f:escalate-deadline, f:escalate-nb, f:late-info, f:escalate-paper]* {#p:late-objection}
1. **Proposition.** Every design needs more than merit to be considered: a proposal needs an on-time paper and a presenter, a rival a paper to exist, and objections fixed forms and times. Other things equal, whoever knows more of how the committee applies them, some of it learned only by attending, has the better chance of adoption, whatever their design skill. *[from f:late-paper, f:present-required, f:no-wait-alternatives, f:one-vote, p:late-objection, f:ballot-new-info, ax:learning, m:moves, f:late-documents]* {#p:fluency}
1. **Proposition.** Other things equal, a newcomer, lacking what earlier meetings teach, spends more effort getting a paper considered than a returning participant, and SD-4 expects everyone to know its procedures. Where a study group incubates proposals on its topic, a newcomer's early proposal can start there. *[from ax:learning, d:newcomer, f:familiar, f:sg-initial-proposal, d:rooms]* {#p:newcomer}

## 5. Incumbents and the cost of reversal

1. **Induction.** Each decision built on a design adds work that reversing the design would redo or discard, and more is built after each favorable poll. So the cost of reversing a design grows as it advances, whether or not the design is wrong. *[from ax:rework, ax:prefer, d:reversal, f:design-forward, f:wording-forward, f:plenary-owns]* {#ind:reversal-grows}
1. **Proposition.** A design passing a design room's direction poll becomes the incumbent, a paperless rival cannot block it, and the chair may delay it one meeting for a paper to arrive. A rival with a paper can be polled alongside it, the room can direct changes, and by default a TS merge proceeds with it unless an alternative is actively progressing. *[from f:no-paper, f:no-wait-alternatives, f:explore-alternatives, f:direction-implement, f:multiple-polls, f:ts-default, d:incumbent, d:polls]* {#p:incumbent-start}
1. **Proposition.** A design can become the incumbent at its first direction poll, when it has had less review than at any later meeting. From then on, a rival or a later change must redo or discard work built on it, at a cost that grows as more is built. *[from ax:first-review, ax:unknown, d:incumbent, d:polls, p:incumbent-start, ind:reversal-grows, d:reversal, ax:rework]* {#p:early-polls}
1. **Proposition.** A rival wins a voter from an incumbent only with an advantage worth more to that voter than their share of the reversal's cost. The advantage a rival needs grows with each decision built on the incumbent, while keeping the incumbent needs none. *[from ax:prefer, ind:reversal-grows, p:incumbent-start, d:incumbent, d:reversal, d:does-better, ax:rework]* {#p:challenger-bar}
1. **Lemma.** A direction poll asks voters only to agree that work should continue, less than a forwarding poll asks, so it can pass where a forwarding poll would fail. Passing it in a design room makes the design the incumbent. *[from d:polls, d:incumbent, f:design-forward, ax:prefer, p:early-polls]* {#l:poll-wording}
1. **Proposition.** If a poll places a proposal among the work the committee aims to include in the next edition, the proposal joins a category SD-4 has chairs take up first. A meeting's hours are fixed, so the time it then gets comes out of the time left for papers outside those categories. *[from f:prioritization, ax:hours]* {#p:roadmap-poll}
1. **Proposition.** Close study takes longer the bigger a design is, and a bigger design has more places where a defect can go unnoticed. For a reviewer whose stake does not grow with size, close study therefore stops being worth its time sooner on a big design than on a small one. *[from ax:review-cost, ax:attention, ax:unknown, ax:prefer, d:stake, d:does-better]* {#p:review-share}
1. **Proposition.** Since those who skip a paper and its discussion typically abstain, those with large stakes cast most votes on a proposal costing more to read than most stakes in it. Unless those of them who object cast at least a third of the Favor-plus-Against votes, it can advance with no change at all. *[from f:not-vote, p:review-share, ax:review-cost, ax:prefer, l:vote-count, p:least-cost-response]* {#p:light-review}
1. **Proposition.** A design needs the 2:1 norm to advance, and once adopted, a paper or ballot comment to remove it needs the same, switching the default to keeping it. After a successful final ballot, design changes are expected to have near-unanimous consent, ISO bars technical changes at approval, and SD-4 calls published features much harder to update incompatibly. *[from f:two-to-one, f:no-paper, f:comment-content, f:near-unanimous, f:late-changes, f:is-hard-to-change, d:reversal, d:follows-norm]* {#p:burden-flip}
1. **Proposition.** An objector who arrives after a design is adopted needs a paper or ballot comment meeting the 2:1 norm, against a reversal cost that grows as more is built. The record keeps the design's case, while unwritten objections are kept only in memory. *[from p:burden-flip, ind:reversal-grows, p:challenger-bar, p:reasons-unrecorded, p:late-objection]* {#p:late-arrival}

## 6. Sponsors, proposal size, and parts

1. **Proposition.** Each participant a sponsor pays to attend has a vote in the room and values its interests, and no rule limits how many it pays. Each is appointed by a national body or liaison that chooses whom to appoint, SC22 may cap only how many each appoints, and at SC22 each national body counts once. *[from p:who-votes, f:no-sponsor-cap, f:one-vote, ax:who-pays, f:experts-appointed, f:registered, f:expert-cap, f:pmember-consensus, ax:prefer, d:rules]* {#p:sponsor-votes}
1. **Proposition.** Changing the features a sponsor's interests depend on costs that sponsor rework of what it built on them, lost fit with its requirements, and more review, all still to come. The gain of a better design is spread across all the feature's users, and the same 2:1 norm decides every proposal, however many users will rely on it. *[from ax:prefer, ax:rework, ax:review-cost, ax:users-affected, f:two-to-one, d:sponsor]* {#p:sponsor-features}
1. **Induction.** Each part adopted adds to what a rival to the whole design must replace: the part itself and the work built on it, later parts that depend on it included. *[from m:moves-author, ind:reversal-grows, d:reversal, ax:rework]* {#ind:adopted-parts}
1. **Proposition.** Presenting a design in parts lets the first parts be adopted before the whole is reviewed, and each adopted part adds to what a rival to the whole must replace. Its costs include a paper, a presentation, and a set of polls for each part. *[from m:moves-author, f:design-forward, f:wording-forward, f:plenary-owns, ind:adopted-parts, p:challenger-bar, ax:review-cost, f:present-required]* {#p:parts}
1. **Proposition.** A first part fixing a design's structure can be adopted before reviewers can study in detail what later parts need, and every rival to that structure must then replace it. SD-4 asks no paper to state those needs, and the smallest part fixing the structure that can pass is reviewed soonest, so a design-bound author does best proposing it first. *[from ax:first-review, ax:attention, ax:prefer, d:stake, f:no-parts-step, p:parts, ind:adopted-parts, p:challenger-bar, l:vote-count, d:design-bound]* {#p:first-part}
1. **Proposition.** A large proposal has more sponsored authors in the room than a small one, and more expected meetings where a poll can make it the incumbent or end its incumbency. *[from d:large, d:small, d:incumbent, p:sponsor-votes]* {#p:large-position}
1. **Proposition.** A small proposal's author bears every meeting's cost alone, and outside SD-4's priority categories the proposal gets agenda time only after the priority work. Where a study group incubates proposals on its topic, a small proposal can start there. *[from d:small, ax:cost, f:prioritization, f:agenda-papers, f:sg-initial-proposal, d:rooms]* {#p:small-position}
1. **Proposition.** Each result here about large or small proposals rests on an attribute their definitions name: who pays the authors' costs, how many authors, review's expected span, or paper length. So it holds for any proposal with that attribute, whatever its size. *[from d:small, d:large, p:large-position, p:small-position]* {#p:size-attributes}

## 7. Study groups and deadlocks

1. **Proposition.** On a topic with a study group, rival designs can be polled there first, among those who chose that room over the rooms meeting with it. The design it sends on is where the design room starts, and the design room, which owns selecting designs, can still poll a rival that has a paper. *[proved by l:sg-purpose, l:sg-attendance, l:sg-start]* *[from f:vehicle, f:multiple-polls]* {#p:sg-decides}
   1. **Lemma.** SD-4 has the convenor form a study group on a design room chair's recommendation when a topic has broad interest, no unified initial proposal, and a strong chair candidate. It gives the study group the role of producing an initial proposal that can then enter the design room. *[from f:sg-formation, f:sg-initial-proposal]* {#l:sg-purpose}
   1. **Lemma.** A participant can attend only one of the rooms meeting at the same time, and votes and speaks only there. So when a study group meets alongside other rooms, its voters are those for whom its topic outweighs them. *[from ax:parallel-rooms, ax:prefer, d:stake, f:one-vote]* {#l:sg-attendance}
   1. **Lemma.** The design room starts from the study group's initial proposal, which a rival without a paper does not block while it is an active proposal making progress. *[from l:sg-purpose, l:sg-attendance, f:no-wait-alternatives, f:sg-initial-proposal]* {#l:sg-start}
1. **Proposition.** Paying participants to attend a study group puts voters who value the sponsor's interests where the design the design room starts from is chosen. Where the other voters cast fewer Favor-plus-Against votes, fewer paid participants make up a third of the total, enough to block, or over two thirds, enough to meet the norm. *[from p:sg-decides, p:sponsor-votes, l:vote-count, d:follows-norm]* {#p:sg-sponsor}
1. **Proposition.** When each of two groups casts at least a third of the Favor-plus-Against votes against the other's design, neither design meets the 2:1 norm. The deadlock lasts until voters change position, a design changes, the voters present change, a chair calls consensus below the norm, or the design room sends both to competing TSs. *[from l:vote-count, f:two-to-one, f:design-forward, f:vehicle, f:competing-ts]* {#p:deadlock}
1. **Proposition.** In a long deadlock, a group whose sponsors keep paying its costs bears less per meeting than a group paying its own way. In the case the table below describes, the first does better holding and the second yielding, whichever design is better. *[proved by l:hold-yield-holder-gains, l:hold-yield-both-hold, l:hold-yield-whole-over-half, l:hold-yield-half-over-deadlock, l:hold-yield-half-over-none, l:hold-yield-cheap-holds, l:hold-yield-costly-yields]* *[from ax:who-pays, ax:cost, p:deadlock]* {#p:attrition}
   1. **Lemma.** When one deadlocked group yields, the other holds, and no one else votes Against the holder's design, the deadlock ends with that design advancing. The holder gains its whole stake, the yielder none of its own, and neither bears the cost of holding through a deadlock. *[from p:deadlock, l:vote-count, d:stake]* {#l:hold-yield-holder-gains}
   1. **Lemma.** When both groups hold and the deadlock ends with either design equally likely, each can expect half its stake on average, less its cost of holding through it. A group's participants whose costs sponsors pay bear less of that cost per meeting. *[from p:deadlock, ax:cost, ax:who-pays, d:stake]* {#l:hold-yield-both-hold}
   1. **Lemma.** Each group does better holding while the other yields, gaining its whole stake at no cost of holding, than when both yield to a compromise worth half its stake. *[from l:hold-yield-holder-gains, ax:prefer, d:does-better]* {#l:hold-yield-whole-over-half}
   1. **Lemma.** Each group does better when both yield to a compromise worth half its stake than when both hold. Every later rival or change must then redo work built on it, at a cost that grows whether or not the design is wrong. Objections count only in fixed forms and at fixed times, and some of how the committee applies these rules is learned only by attending. In a long deadlock, the group whose sponsors keep paying its costs can hold longest, whichever design is better. A proposal that costs more to read than most participants' stakes in it can advance with no change at all. An objector arriving after adoption needs a paper or ballot comment meeting the 2:1 norm, faces a reversal cost that has grown, and finds unwritten objections kept only in memory. Other things equal, a proposal advances more readily when its authors keep presenting it or know the procedures, or when a sponsor pays for participants who hold its position. The same holds when the chair expects fewer problems from it, a poll has placed it among the work aimed at the next edition, or it is already the incumbent. No user votes in WG21's polls or ballots, and the needs put to a vote are those participants and sponsors know, which need not match those of users at large. The participants present decide content, and no rule limits how many voters one sponsor pays for. A change to a design costs the sponsors who have built on it, while its gain is spread across all users. Authors get adoption at the poll, while a flaw found only in implementation and use costs them later and in part, and can fall on any user. Technical merit enters through what participants value, including a good result for users, and through the discussion, papers, and implementation experience that inform their votes. SD-4 assigns no one to rule on it, and the consensus test never asks whether an objection is right. No SD-4 step checks whether separately adopted designs work together, and no player does review worth its cost to users together but not to any one participant. The record need not show whether objections were answered. The rules ask no author to state their own design's drawbacks. Leaving one out costs a design-bound author no step the rules require before adoption, while stating one can cost a result-seeking author votes. A first part fixing a structure can be adopted before reviewers can study in detail what later parts need, and every rival to that structure must then replace it. The rules also provide checks: a third of the Favor-plus-Against votes can block, and every view must be heard. Rivals with papers can be polled together, implementation experience informs votes, and national bodies vote on each balloted draft.
