## Findings

f:no-sustained-procedure | unsupported absence claim | SD-4 does use "sustained", "concerned interest(s)", and "important part" only inside the quoted consensus definition ("Consensus"), never uses "register", and gives no criteria for an important part of the concerned interests. But "address" and "resolve" from the item's own search list, plus the synonyms opposition, objection, concern, and Against, find SD-4 steps for assessing and addressing an opposition its holders maintain. In "Subgroup responsibility and polls": `A proposal normally advances if there are more than twice as many in favor of a proposal as against, after discussion of the concerns of those voting against and possibly a re-poll to see if opinions have improved.` and `Anyone voting Against, especially Strongly Against, may be asked for their reasons by the chair. They should be prepared to articulate their rationale, including either what change(s) would address their concern and change their position, or else that they are opposed to pursuing the proposal in any form and no change would change their position.` In "Closing plenary session responsibility and polls": `If there is significant opposition, the Convener will usually ask whether the Against votes are personal positions or national body positions, typically by asking the national body panel chairs who are present.` In "Escalating a serious “cannot live with” objection, or significant new information": steps for raising such an objection on the email lists and within one's national body, then `Objections that are not so escalated, but are raised or re-raised in plenary, should not be given weight in plenary.` None of these names a sustained opposition, but each reaches one when it arises (a national body's maintained Against vote in plenary is the plainest case), so "no step for assessing, registering, or addressing a sustained opposition" is not accepted on sight. The registering part holds: "All meetings must be minuted." is general, and no SD-4 step registers an opposition. | Narrow the item to the parts that hold and add the synonyms to the searched list, for example `**Fact.** SD-4 uses "sustained" and "concerned interests" only in its consensus definition, gives no criteria for an important part of the concerned interests, and has no step for registering an opposition (SD-4, whole document; searched: sustained, concerned interest, important part, register, record, minute, assess, address, resolve, opposition, objection, Against). {#f:no-sustained-procedure}`. If a later item contrasts SD-4 with ISO, compare SD-4 with the three steps 2.5.7 names (assess whether an important part of the concerned interest sustains the opposition, register it if not, try to resolve it in good faith if so) and acknowledge the SD-4 steps above instead of saying SD-4 has none. The ledger shows p:definition-without-procedure in chapter 5 resting on this item, so it needs the same narrowing. Confidence: medium - the claim holds only if "step for" means a step that names sustained opposition, and SD-4's general steps for Against votes and escalated objections reach any opposition, sustained or not.

f:escalate-deadline | misquote | SD-4, "Escalating a serious “cannot live with” objection, or significant new information": the bullet completes the lead-in `As early as possible, make the EWG and LEWG chairs aware of your concern. (SD-4, "Escalating a serious “cannot live with” objection, or significant new information") {#f:escalate-deadline}`. Both quoted passages match the extract word for word and cross no page number or copyright line. f:escalate-nb completes the same lead-in and needs no change while it stays directly after this item. Confidence: medium - the bullet's own words point to the same week's plenary, but only the lead-in limits the deadline, and the ledger's p:late-objection and p:nb-route build on this item.

- f:no-sponsor-cap (SD-4 and every extracted ISO clause). Searched: sponsor, employ, compan, organization, organisation, firm, corporat, affiliat, maximum, limit. Nearest hits: 1.12.1 lets the committee "decide upon the maximum number of Experts appointed by each P-member and liaison organization" (a permission to cap by appointing body, not by payer, and not itself a limit); 5.3.1 lets "voluntary sponsors" fund meeting facilities and bars a meeting from promoting a sponsor's products. No text limits the number of participants whose costs one sponsor pays. The item's clause list matches the extract's ISO headings exactly. Holds.

The parent holds for f:no-sustained-procedure once that item is narrowed, so it needs no change of its own.

The three-year schedule is not in the extract, but the item does not attribute it to SD-4 or the Directives, and the kit allows it as a structural fact.
- d:national-body: SC22 as the committee WG21 belongs to fits SD-4 ("our immediate parent committee SC22") and 1.12.1 ("its parent committee"). The reading rule for "the committee" fits every Directives quotation in the chapter that uses the phrase (f:convenor-term, f:expert-cap, f:pmember-consensus, f:sustained-def, f:concerned-interests, f:comment-report), and it rightly leaves out SD-4 quotations such as f:no-surprise, where "the committee" means WG21.

- ax:prefer, ax:support, ax:repeat, d:newcomer, d:player, d:does-better, d:incumbent, d:small, and d:large make no statement about what SD-4 or the Directives require, permit, or omit.
