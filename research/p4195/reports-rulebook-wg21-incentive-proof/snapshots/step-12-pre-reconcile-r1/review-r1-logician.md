# Logician review, round 1

## Chapter 2

- p:who-decides | overreach | The cited facts put each determination with a chair or the convenor (f:design-forward, f:wording-forward, f:plenary-owns) and make the 2:1 norm only what normally happens, so "the participants present decide a design's content" says more than chairs deciding with the votes as a normal guide. | Weaker wording: "Chairs decide a design's content by consensus calls that normally follow the votes of the participants present under the 2:1 norm."
- p:who-votes | overreach | "Each person in a subgroup's room casts one vote" is stronger than f:one-vote's "can cast one vote", and f:not-vote says not everyone votes. | Weaker wording: "can cast one vote".
- m:moves-chair | hidden premise | "A study group chair polls which design leaves the group as its initial proposal" states a procedure no cited item supplies; f:sg-initial-proposal says only that a study group produces an initial proposal, and f:chair-polls lets a chair take any poll. l:sg-call and inf:sg-call-norm build on it when they treat the study group's output as a consensus call under the 2:1 norm. | Weaker wording: "a study group chair can poll which design leaves the group", plus an added cited premise in l:sg-call if its consensus-call claim stays.

## Chapter 3

- p:against-costs-more | overreach | f:no-reasons-other shows only that SD-4 names no step for asking other voters for reasons, so "only Against voters can be asked for reasons" claims more than the basis; p:prefer-vs-stop repeats this as "draws no request for reasons". | Weaker wording: "only Against voters are ones SD-4 says the chair may ask for reasons", which still supports the extra cost.

## Chapter 4

- p:definition-without-procedure | overreach | "SD-4's own steps are" presents the list as complete, but no cited absence fact supports that, and SD-4 also provides a private concern to the chair (f:private-concern), time-boxing (f:timebox), and the one-meeting delay for a rival (f:no-wait-alternatives). | Weaker wording: "SD-4's own steps include".
- p:least-cost-response | invalid step | "Finding out whether those objectors are right leaves that count unchanged" does not follow from l:study-count, which says only that the comparison tallies votes, and ax:attention says what a participant learns can change their vote. | Weaker wording: "The 2:1 count registers whether those objectors are right only through the votes that finding out changes."
- l:reason-fades | invalid step | ax:turnover lets an absent person learn what was said from "those present who remember it", which includes hearers who have since stopped attending, so "once all of them have stopped attending, a later participant can voice it only by rediscovering it" does not follow; inf:writing-only takes the same step. | Weaker wording: "once no one who remembers it can be asked", or an added cited premise that those who stop attending stop passing on what they heard.
- l:rules-by-attending | overreach | ax:learning says some of how the committee works is learned only by attending, not that some of these written SD-4 rules (on-time papers, presenters, escalation deadlines) are; p:fluency and l:thm-objections ("the forms and times that attending teaches") inherit the stronger claim. | Weaker wording: "Participants learn how the committee works partly by attending, some of it only that way."
- p:newcomer | hidden premise | No cited item says WG21 runs EWGI or LEWGI, and p:small-position names them the same way. | Cut "such as EWGI and LEWGI" in both items, or add a cited Fact.

## Chapter 5

- l:rival-reversal | invalid step | A direction poll approves only continuing work on a design (d:polls), and l:direction-asks-less has doubters vote for it because the room can direct changes, so "later changing it" does not remove or replace what the poll approved. p:early-polls, inf:early-improvement-cost, and l:thm-fixed-early inherit "every later change bears a reversal cost". | Cut "or later changing it", or add a cited premise that a direction poll approves the design as it stands.
- l:rival-net | hidden premise | "their share of the reversal's cost" assumes every voter bears part of the redo-or-discard cost, but ax:rework does not say who bears it, so for a voter who built nothing the share can be zero and need not grow with what is built. p:challenger-bar's growing hurdle, inf:part-voter-share, and inf:backing-share rest on it. | Add a cited premise saying which voters bear the reversal's cost, or weaker wording: "a voter who bears part of the reversal's cost".
- p:burden-flip | hidden premise | f:two-to-one and d:positions give the 2:1 norm only as a sufficient condition ("normally advances if"), and no cited item says a proposal at or below it normally fails, so "normally needs the 2:1 norm" for advancing and for removal is unsupported. l:removal-polls, l:late-removal, inf:oppose-late-burden, and inf:oppose-early-rank ("removing it later takes over two thirds") inherit this. | Add a cited premise that a proposal at or below 2:1 normally does not advance, or weaker wording: "normally advances with the 2:1 norm".
- p:late-arrival | overreach | "Removal needs a paper meeting the 2:1 norm" drops "or ballot comment" and "normally" from l:late-removal, its own basis; inf:thm-late-arrival and l:thm-objections repeat the stronger form. | Weaker wording: "Removal normally needs a paper or ballot comment meeting the 2:1 norm."

## Chapter 6

- l:parts-build | overreach | ax:rework says that after a favorable poll authors and others build more on a design, not that every later part builds on every part already adopted; ind:adopted-parts and inf:fix-hurdle inherit "every later part builds on". | Weaker wording: "later parts can build on the parts already adopted".
- p:parts | overreach | "The cost is a paper and a presentation for each part" presents the costs in l:part-cost as the only ones, which nothing cited shows. | Weaker wording: "Its costs include a paper and a presentation for each part."
- l:first-part-oppose | invalid step | The conclusion compares voting Against the first part with "waiting to vote Against a later part", but the children compare blocking the first part with removing it after adoption, and voting Against a later part leaves the first part and its structure adopted. | Weaker wording: "than waiting to seek its removal after adoption".
- l:first-part-smallest | hidden premise | "takes less time to write and to review" and "at less cost" count only the first paper, while the rest of the design must still come as later parts, each with its own paper and presenter (l:part-cost). | Add a cited premise that the later parts cost no more when the first part is smaller, or weaker wording: "a first paper that costs less to write and review".
- p:large-position | overreach | d:large and d:small give only the expected span of review, so "has ... more meetings where a poll can make it the incumbent" is stronger than its basis. | Weaker wording: "is expected to have more meetings".
- p:large-position | hidden premise | "end that standing" uses the defined term standing, colleagues' readiness to support a participant (d:standing), for incumbency, a sense no cited item supplies. | Weaker wording: "or end its incumbency".
- p:size-attributes | overreach | "Each result about large or small proposals" covers results the item does not cite, including later ones such as l:settled-small-author, c:pred-parts, and c:pred-small-fade, while its basis is only p:large-position and p:small-position. | Weaker wording: "Each result in this chapter about large or small proposals".

## Chapter 7

- p:deadlock | overreach | "The deadlock lasts until" presents the list of exits as complete, but l:count-depends and the cited facts do not exclude others, such as a design whose authors stop presenting it (f:present-required) or a white paper. | Weaker wording: "The deadlock can end when".

## Chapter 8

- p:chair-threshold | overreach | "Every consensus call has a cost" is stronger than the basis, which covers declaring consensus over Against votes and declining, not a call with no Against votes. | Weaker wording: "Every contested consensus call has a cost."
- p:chair-threshold | hidden premise | f:chairs-appointed and f:convenor-term show that a post can end, not that departing from the 2:1 norm makes that likelier than following it, and the rankings in l:call-above, l:call-below, and l:convenor-depart need that difference. | Add a cited premise that the convenor or SC22 weighs adherence to SD-4 in appointing chairs and convenors.
- inf:call-above-norm | invalid step | f:two-to-one includes "possibly a re-poll to see if opinions have improved" in the norm itself, so re-polling on a count above 2:1 does not depart from it. | Cut "or re-polling", so that only declining departs.
- inf:call-below-norm | hidden premise | The norm as f:two-to-one and d:positions state it says what normally happens above 2:1 and gives no call at or below, so declaring consensus there departs from nothing it says, and p:deadlock itself lists "a chair calls consensus below the norm". l:call-below, l:dchair-call, l:norm-forwarding, and the second sentence of p:cutoff-effect inherit this. | Add a cited premise that under SD-4 a proposal at or below 2:1 normally does not advance.
- p:chair-confidence | overreach | f:present-required and f:invested let another presenter pass feedback to an author who revises, so a record of responding to review can come from revisions without taking part; l:review-needs-part gives SD-4's expectation, not exclusivity. | Weaker wording: "SD-4 expects that record to come from taking part."
- l:plenary-late-weight | overreach | f:plenary-weight says such objections "should not be given weight", which is SD-4's instruction, not what happens; p:plenary-ratifies and inf:nb-late-plenary repeat "get no weight". | Weaker wording: "SD-4 says should get no weight there."

## Chapter 9

- p:users-out | hidden premise | "national bodies, which form positions only where that is worth their members' time" needs a national body to count its members' time as a cost, but ax:prefer lists a national body's values as outcomes and national interests, and l:nb-position-time shows only that positions take that time. inf:nb-cost, inf:nm-more, and inf:nl-fewer inherit it. | Add a cited premise, or weaker wording: "national bodies, whose positions take their members' time".
- p:reward-timing | overreach | d:result-seeking ties a result-seeking author's gain to how well the adopted design serves users, which a flaw found later lowers, so "An author gains at adoption" does not cover all of an author's gain; inf:thm-reward-timing and l:thm-costs-gains repeat it. | Weaker wording: "An author's gain from adoption itself comes at adoption."
- l:merit-in-vote | overreach | A vote can also reflect the design that serves users best when the voter's other values point the same way, so "weighs a good result for users above other values" is stronger than ax:prefer supports. p:merit-gap, inf:thm-merit-gap, and l:thm-merit inherit it. | Weaker wording: "and no other value outweighs it".
- p:written-answer | overreach | "No rule requires a written answer" goes beyond f:no-paper-objections and f:respond-comments, which cover SD-4 and the named ISO clauses only. | Weaker wording: "Neither SD-4 nor the searched ISO clauses require a written answer".

## Chapter 11

- l:favor-no-risk | hidden premise | ax:support speaks of Against, Neutral, and not voting and says nothing about Favor, so no cited item supplies that a Favor vote gives the colleague no reason to support the voter less readily. inf:favor-cost, l:favor-supported, l:doubt-options, and thm:settled ("Favor risks nothing with the colleague") rest on it. | Add a cited premise, for example a clause in ax:support.
- l:settled-design-bound | overreach | "propose first the smallest part that fixes the features its gain depends on" drops the condition in p:first-part that the part "still meets the 2:1 norm against opponents who foresee the rest"; thm:settled, inf:pa-design-bound, and inf:fp-small repeat it. | Restore the condition.
- l:bound-omit | hidden premise | inf:omit-free shows only that leaving a trade-off out costs nothing "the rules impose", but the lemma needs it to cost nothing at all, for example no loss of standing if implementation later exposes it (ax:unknown). | Add a cited premise, or weaker wording: "does no better, counting only the costs the rules impose".
- l:bound-cheapest | overreach | When objectors who can keep the norm unmet ask only for changes to the features the author's gain depends on, the cheapest change that keeps the norm met changes those features with no room direction, so "leaves the features ... alone unless the room directs a change to them" is too strong. | Weaker wording: "unless the room directs a change to them or only such a change keeps the norm met".
- l:settled-small-author | invalid step | "When those costs, which grow with each meeting it waits ..., outweigh it" counts meetings already paid for, which stopping cannot recover, while l:small-keep-stop compares the chance of adoption with the costs still to come; inf:sf-small repeats the step. | Weaker wording: "When the cost of the meetings still ahead outweighs its chance of adoption".
- inf:sg-order-cost | hidden premise | It applies p:chair-threshold, which concerns departing from the 2:1 norm, to departing from a polling order SD-4 only encourages, and nothing cited links that to a chair's post. | Add a cited premise, or cut the post clause.
- l:convenor-weightless | hidden premise | f:plenary-weight gives unescalated objections no weight and f:plenary-consent defines unanimous consent over all positions, but no cited item says the convenor then declares consensus; inf:nm-unsettled builds on it. | Weaker wording: "SD-4's plenary procedure gives that opposition no weight in the convenor's consensus call."
- inf:user-price | invalid step | d:sponsor says a sponsor pays some or all of a participant's costs, not that every participant has one, and a user who takes part becomes a participant a sponsor could pay, so "bears the full price of attending alone" does not follow. l:user-take-part, l:settled-user, and thm:settled inherit it. | Weaker wording: "a user with no sponsor bears the full price".
- p:checks | overreach | No item shows that any listed rule acts on any effect the proof derives, and p:integration says the editing committee verifies only that changes were applied, not that designs work together; l:thm-checks-sizes and thm:settled go further with "checks on each of these effects". | Weaker wording: "The rules also contain these provisions."

- l:thm-fixed-early | overreach | The basis shows a design can become the incumbent when least reviewed, after which changes bear a growing cost, while l:rival-with-paper and f:direction-implement show the room can still direct changes, so "content can be fixed" is too strong; the chapter's closing summary repeats it. | Weaker wording: "a design can become the incumbent when it is least reviewed".
- l:thm-gaps | overreach | "review whose gain is spread across all users goes undone" drops the condition in p:review-shortfall that the review is worth less to any one participant than its cost, and every design's gain is spread across its users (ax:users-affected). | Restore the condition.
