## 9. Users, quality, and disclosure

1. **Proposition.** No user votes, and taking part costs the participant or their sponsor, while a better design's gain is spread across all users and seldom turns on one user's effort. Users' interests reach decisions only through what participants themselves value, papers a participant presents, TS feedback, and national bodies, which form positions only where that is worth their members' time. *[proved by l:user-channels, l:nb-position-time]* *[from p:who-votes, ax:users-affected]* {#p:users-out}
   1. **Lemma.** Users' interests reach decisions only through what participants themselves value, papers a participant presents, TS feedback, and national bodies. *[from p:who-votes, p:who-decides, m:moves-user, m:players, d:user, ax:prefer, f:iso-early]* {#l:user-channels}
   1. **Lemma.** A national body forms a position on a proposal only where that position is worth its members' time. *[proved by inf:nb-discussion, inf:nb-time]* *[from ax:prefer]* {#l:nb-position-time}
      1. Under SD-4, a position is normally a national position only if the national body discussed it before or during the meeting. *[from f:escalate-nb]* {#inf:nb-discussion}
      1. Time a national body's members spend discussing one proposal comes out of the time they have for other papers, whose outcomes the national body may also value. *[from ax:hours, ax:attention, ax:prefer]* {#inf:nb-time}
1. **Proposition.** The rules bar participation fees and exclude no registered expert, yet only those who bear the cost of taking part, or whose sponsors do, get a vote. They propose for needs they and their sponsors know, and since users differ from participants in which features matter, the needs reaching a vote need not match users' needs. *[from f:registered, f:no-fee, f:remote, f:iso-cost, f:in-person-expected, p:who-votes, ax:cost, ax:who-pays, ax:prefer, ax:users-affected, f:present-required]* {#p:who-takes-part}
   1. **Lemma.** The rules bar participation fees and give each person in a subgroup's room one vote, yet only those who bear the cost of taking part, or whose sponsors do, vote in WG21's polls. They propose for needs they and their sponsors know, and since users differ from participants in which features matter, the needs reaching a vote need not match users' needs. *[proved by inf:voters-bear-cost, inf:proposals-present]* *[from f:no-fee, p:who-votes, ax:users-affected]* {#l:cost-bearers-vote weakened}
      1. Only those present vote in WG21's polls, and taking part costs each of them or a sponsor who pays for them. *[from p:who-votes, ax:cost, ax:who-pays, ax:users-affected]* {#inf:voters-bear-cost}
      1. A proposal reaches a vote only if an author or another qualified and prepared presenter presents it, and a participant with a sponsor values the sponsor's interests. *[from f:present-required, ax:prefer]* {#inf:proposals-present}
1. **Proposition.** An author gains at adoption, while a flaw that implementation and use have not yet exposed costs users only after adoption, and can fall on any user of the feature. *[from ax:prefer, ax:unknown, ax:users-affected, d:author, d:user]* {#p:reward-timing}
1. **Lemma.** Implementation and use reduce uncertainty about how well a design works, though even then a room cannot fully judge it at a poll. SD-4 describes a TS or white paper as a way to gain that experience, noting that its users typically should not rely on it in production code. *[from ax:unknown, f:ts-experience, f:ts-feedback]* {#l:implementation-informs}
1. **Proposition.** Winning a consensus call and producing the design that serves users best coincide only as far as voters can judge that at the poll and weigh it above other values. SD-4 assigns no one to judge it for them. *[from ax:prefer, ax:unknown, p:who-decides, l:consensus-test, l:implementation-informs, f:no-merit-ruling]* {#p:merit-gap}
1. **Proposition.** Close study costs the reviewer, while the gain of a better design is spread across all its users. So review worth more to users together than it costs, but less to any one participant, is review no participant does better doing. *[from ax:review-cost, ax:users-affected, ax:prefer, p:review-share, d:stake, d:does-better]* {#p:review-shortfall}
1. **Proposition.** No step in SD-4 checks whether separately adopted designs work together, and each author gains from their own design's adoption while a conflict costs users and later rework. The nearest steps ask a good paper to fit the language's principles, have new wording written against the adopted drafts, and let a TS merge early for integration testing. *[from f:no-integration-check, f:principle-based, f:wording-baseline, f:early-merge, ax:prefer, ax:unknown, ax:users-affected, ax:rework]* {#p:integration}
1. **Lemma.** SD-4 relies on the presenter, an expert in the paper's domain, to educate a room of general C++ experts. It also counts a feature's designers among the best experts on any concern about it. *[from f:presenter, f:concern-authors]* {#l:author-knows-more}
1. **Proposition.** Stating a drawback in the paper before an early poll can cost Favor votes when incumbency is decided, while a better-informed vote's gain is spread across the design's users. SD-4 says a good paper should show the alternatives considered, but it asks no paper to state its design's drawbacks, and a paper is considered without them. *[proved by l:drawback-votes]* *[from p:early-polls, ax:users-affected, f:show-alternatives, f:no-drawbacks-asked, f:late-paper, f:present-required]* {#p:disclosure}
   1. **Lemma.** Stating a drawback in the paper can cost the proposal Favor votes. *[proved by inf:drawback-unseen, inf:drawback-lowers]* {#l:drawback-votes}
      1. SD-4 counts a design's authors among the best experts on concerns about it, and voters study only some papers closely, so an author can know drawbacks the room would miss. *[from l:author-knows-more, ax:attention]* {#inf:drawback-unseen}
      1. What a voter learns from a paper can change their vote, and learning of a drawback can lower what the design is worth to them. *[from ax:attention, ax:prefer]* {#inf:drawback-lowers}
1. **Proposition.** No rule requires a written answer to an objection, apart from an author's defense of declining a directed change and ISO's response to each enquiry ballot comment. For the author, a written answer makes the objection easier to raise again, but also shows later what would not be new, the test SD-4 applies to repeated objections. *[proved by l:answer-keeps-objection, l:answer-shows-considered]* *[from p:record-indistinct, f:no-paper-objections, f:direction-implement, f:respond-comments, f:comment-report, p:repeat-objection, f:ballot-new-info]* {#p:written-answer}
   1. **Lemma.** A written answer puts the objection it answers in writing, where later participants can find it and raise it again more easily. *[from ax:turnover]* {#l:answer-keeps-objection}
   1. **Lemma.** A written answer also shows later participants that the objection was already raised and answered. *[from ax:turnover]* {#l:answer-shows-considered}

In plain terms: no user votes, and users' interests reach decisions only through what participants themselves value, papers a participant presents, TS feedback, and national bodies. Winning a consensus call and producing the design that serves users best coincide only as far as voters can judge that at the poll and weigh it above other values. Stating a drawback in the paper before an early poll can cost Favor votes, and SD-4 asks no paper to state its design's drawbacks.
