## 5. Momentum: incumbents and the cost of reversal

1. **Induction.** Each decision built on a design adds work that reversing the design would redo or discard, and more is built after each favorable poll. So the cost of reversing a design grows as it advances, whether or not the design is wrong. *[from ax:rework, ax:prefer, d:reversal, f:design-forward, f:wording-forward, f:plenary-owns]* {#ind:reversal-grows}
1. **Proposition.** A design passing a direction poll becomes the incumbent, and a rival without a paper cannot block it, though the chair may delay it one meeting for one to arrive. A rival with a paper can be polled alongside it, the room can direct changes, and by default a TS merge proceeds with it unless an alternative is actively progressing. *[from f:no-paper, f:no-wait-alternatives, f:explore-alternatives, f:direction-implement, f:multiple-polls, f:ts-default, d:incumbent, d:polls]* {#p:incumbent-start}
1. **Proposition.** A design can become the incumbent at its first direction poll, when it has had less review than at any later meeting. From then on, every rival and every later change to it bears a reversal cost that grows as more is built on it. *[proved by l:first-presentation-poll, l:replace-reverses]* *[from ax:first-review, ax:unknown, d:incumbent, d:polls, p:incumbent-start, ind:reversal-grows, d:reversal]* {#p:early-polls}
   1. **Lemma.** A design room chair may take a direction poll at the meeting where a design is first presented, when it has had less committee review than at any later meeting. *[from f:chair-polls, d:polls, ax:first-review]* {#l:first-presentation-poll}
   1. **Lemma.** A rival adopted in the incumbent's place, or a change to part of it, replaces something a design room approved in its direction poll, so each is a reversal. The work built on what it replaces must then be redone or discarded. *[from d:reversal, d:incumbent, d:polls, ax:rework]* {#l:replace-reverses}
1. **Proposition.** A rival wins a voter from an incumbent only with an advantage worth more to that voter than their share of the reversal's cost. That hurdle grows with each decision built on the incumbent, which faces no such hurdle itself. *[proved by l:rival-weighs-cost, l:rival-cost-grows]* *[from ax:prefer, ind:reversal-grows, p:incumbent-start, d:incumbent, d:reversal, d:does-better]* {#p:challenger-bar}
   1. **Lemma.** Adopting the rival brings a voter its advantage but also puts on them their share of the cost of redoing or discarding the work built on the incumbent. Keeping the incumbent replaces nothing, so it puts no such cost on them. *[from l:replace-reverses, d:reversal]* {#l:rival-weighs-cost}
   1. **Lemma.** Each decision built on the incumbent adds work that adopting the rival would redo or discard, which raises the reversal's cost and lowers no voter's share of it. *[from ind:reversal-grows, l:replace-reverses]* {#l:rival-cost-grows}
1. **Lemma.** A direction poll asks voters only to agree that work should continue, less than a forwarding poll asks, so it can pass where a forwarding poll would fail. Yet passing it makes the design the incumbent. *[from d:polls, d:incumbent, f:design-forward, ax:prefer, p:early-polls]* {#l:poll-wording}
1. **Proposition.** If a poll places a proposal among the work the committee aims to include in the next IS, the proposal joins a category SD-4 has chairs take up first. A meeting's hours are fixed, so the time it then gets comes out of the time left for papers outside those categories. *[from f:prioritization, ax:hours]* {#p:roadmap-poll}
1. **Proposition.** A longer, more complex design costs more to study and has more places where a defect can go unnoticed. Yet a reviewer whose stake does not grow with its size does better studying it less closely than a smaller design with the same stake. *[proved by l:study-gain-cap, l:study-limit]* *[from ax:review-cost, ax:attention, ax:unknown, ax:prefer, d:stake, d:does-better]* {#p:review-share}
   1. **Lemma.** A reviewer's study of a design affects its poll only through what they vote or say, so what it brings them through the poll is worth at most their stake. *[from ax:attention, d:stake]* {#l:study-gain-cap}
   1. **Lemma.** Studying a larger design as closely as a smaller one takes a reviewer more time. With the same stake in both, the reviewer does better studying the larger one less closely whenever studying it that closely takes time worth more to them than the stake. *[from ax:review-cost, l:study-gain-cap, ax:prefer, d:does-better]* {#l:study-limit weakened}
1. **Proposition.** Participants who do not study a proposal typically abstain, so one costing more to study than most stakes in it is voted on mainly by those with large stakes. Unless those of them who object cast at least a third of the Favor-plus-Against votes, it can advance with no change at all. *[proved by l:skip-costly-study, l:under-third-meets]* *[from f:not-vote, p:review-share, ax:review-cost, ax:prefer, l:vote-count, p:least-cost-response]* {#p:light-review}
   1. **Lemma.** A participant whose stake in a poll is worth less than the time studying its proposal takes does better leaving it unstudied, since study brings them at most that stake. *[from l:study-gain-cap, ax:review-cost, ax:prefer, d:does-better]* {#l:skip-costly-study}
   1. **Lemma.** When Against votes are fewer than a third of the Favor-plus-Against votes, Favor votes exceed twice the Against votes, which meets the 2:1 norm with the proposal unchanged. *[from f:two-to-one, d:positions]* {#l:under-third-meets}
1. **Proposition.** A design normally needs the 2:1 norm to advance, and once adopted, a paper or ballot comment to remove it normally needs the same, switching the default to keeping it. After a successful final ballot, design changes are expected to have near-unanimous consent, ISO bars technical changes at approval, and SD-4 calls published features much harder to update incompatibly. *[from f:two-to-one, f:no-paper, f:comment-content, f:near-unanimous, f:late-changes, f:is-hard-to-change, d:reversal]* {#p:burden-flip}
1. **Proposition.** An objector who arrives after a design has been adopted faces three obstacles at once. Removal needs a paper meeting the 2:1 norm, reversal costs more for every decision built on the design, and the record keeps its case but unwritten objections only in memory. *[from p:burden-flip, ind:reversal-grows, p:challenger-bar, p:reasons-unrecorded, p:late-objection]* {#p:late-arrival}

In plain terms: a design can become the incumbent at the meeting where it is first presented and least reviewed, and from then on a rival wins a voter only with an advantage worth more than that voter's share of a cost of reversal that grows with each decision built on the design, whether or not the design is wrong. A proposal that costs more to study than most stakes in it is voted on mainly by those with large stakes, and it can advance with no change unless its objectors cast at least a third of the Favor-plus-Against votes. Once a design is adopted, removing it normally needs the same 2:1 norm that adopting it did, and an objector who arrives later also faces the growing cost of reversal and a record that keeps the design's case but unwritten objections only in memory.
