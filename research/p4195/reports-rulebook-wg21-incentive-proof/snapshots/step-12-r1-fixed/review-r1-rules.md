# Rules check: full draft, round 1

Checked every Fact, and every sentence that describes a rule, in the foundation and chapters 2 to 12 against the rule extract (SD-4 in full, the extracted clauses of ISO/IEC Directives Part 1, and the extracted INCITS sections). Scope: all, so every item in the twelve draft files, including each chapter's "In plain terms" paragraph. The drafts have no abstract or appendix. For text without a key, KEY gives the chapter number plus "In plain terms".

Result: 20 findings - 19 unsupported rule claims and 1 unsupported absence claim. No misquote or wrong-section findings.

Coverage:

- Quoting Facts: 166 labeled Facts (165 in the foundation, f:consensus-source in chapter 4) plus the unlabeled level-3 fact f:iso-no-convenor. All 201 quoted passages match the extract word for word under the section or clause their Fact cites (checked by script after setting curly quote marks and apostrophes to straight and collapsing whitespace). Citations to a sub-clause inside a multi-clause extract section (1.7, 2.5.6, 3.1.2, 3.1.3, and 6.1.1 to 6.1.3) were checked by position and hold.
- INCITS 7.17.1.4: the extract runs 7.17.1.3 and 7.17.1.4 together with no boundary. The INCITS Organization, Policies and Procedures source text (eb-2026-00285, page 69) puts the caucus sentence under 7.17.1.4, so f:incits-caucus cites the right subclause. Splitting the extract heading at that boundary would let later rounds confirm it from the extract alone.
- Framing words in Facts, such as "for WG21, the chair of SC22", "The first exception", "Of the pre-merge issues", and "all but the first from a slide it displays", match what the quoted text says.
- Section-header Facts (f:sd4-purpose through f:incits, and f:absences) name the sections and clauses their children come from.
- Absence Facts: 13, searched as listed under "Absence searches". 12 hold; f:no-merit-ruling does not.
- Other rule-describing sentences in axioms, definitions, lemmas, propositions, inferences, corollaries, the Theorem, and the "In plain terms" paragraphs were each matched to supporting extract text. 19 lack it and are listed below in draft order. Several share a root: f:no-merit-ruling (p:who-decides, ch2 In plain terms), the reasons step (p:against-costs-more, c:lever-reasons-all), the 2:1 norm read as a necessary condition (inf:call-below-norm, l:norm-forwarding), the protected records (l:records-protected, c:predictions), and "trade-off" (thm:settled, l:thm-disclosure, ch11 In plain terms).
- Close calls that pass: p:disclosure, inf:omit-free, inf:thm-omit-free, and the chapter 9 recap say SD-4 "asks no paper" to state its design's drawbacks. In context these mean the author's proposal paper, which f:no-drawbacks-asked covers, though SD-4 does ask a TS or white paper to list `the list of must-address controversial issues` and to help users `estimate the degree of risk`. inf:wa-predict's "address only" means only among objections. l:convenor-weightless follows from f:plenary-weight and the consensus definition.

## Findings

f:no-merit-ruling | unsupported absence claim | The ruling half holds: no SD-4 text gives anyone a binding ruling on which side of a technical dispute is correct, and chairs and the convenor determine only consensus. The quality half does not. SD-4, "Subgroup responsibility and polls", gives the direction group a charter that `includes setting forth a group opinion on:` a list that includes `Providing an opinion on any specific proposal: This includes whether the proposal should be pursued or not, and if pursued any changes that should be considered. Design group participants are strongly encouraged to give weight to an opinion that the design group feel strongly enough about to suggest.` The same section says `The design subgroups (EWG and LEWG) own selecting and recommending feature designs and ship vehicles (TS or IS).` and lets a chair act `based on such requests or just on their own judgment` on a proposal that, for example, `is not well thought out`. The searched stem "judge" misses "judgment", which SD-4 uses three times, and "opinion" was not searched. | Narrow the item to rulings and widen the search: `**Fact.** SD-4 assigns no one the role of ruling which side of a technical dispute is correct or how good a design is (SD-4, whole document; searched: rule, ruling, decide, arbiter, correct, authority, quality, judg, evaluat, assess, merit, opinion, select, pursu). {#f:no-merit-ruling}`. Where an item needs the direction group's advice, cite f:direction-opinion. p:merit-gap ("to judge it for them"), l:thm-merit, inf:thm-merit-no-judge, and the Theorem ("to rule on it") already use forms that hold. Confidence: medium - turns on whether an advisory opinion counts as judging a design's quality.

Confidence: medium - same reading question as f:no-merit-ruling.

Confidence: high - the extract names ballots on questions and on appointments.

ch2 In plain terms | unsupported rule claim | First sentence: "SD-4 assigns no one to judge a design's quality", the same claim as p:who-decides, against the same direction group charter text. | Write "SD-4 assigns no one to rule on a design's quality". p:against-costs-more | unsupported rule claim | "only Against voters can be asked for reasons" states a bar SD-4 does not contain. SD-4, "Subgroup responsibility and polls": `Anyone voting Against, especially Strongly Against, may be asked for their reasons by the chair. They should be prepared to articulate their rationale, including either what change(s) would address their concern and change their position, or else that they are opposed to pursuing the proposal in any form and no change would change their position.` The text permits asking Against voters and tells them to be ready. f:no-reasons-other finds no step for other voters, which is an absence, not a prohibition. | Write "and SD-4 names only Against voters as voters a chair may ask for reasons and tells them to be prepared, which takes time". The cost comparison still follows. Confidence: high - "can be asked" turns an absence into a prohibition.

p:persuasion-not-required | unsupported rule claim | The item says a proposal advances "in WG21's rooms and plenary" on "a consensus call, which the 2:1 norm normally supports". SD-4 states the norm only for subgroup polls: `Subgroup polls, especially in design subgroups, should favor progress.` For plenary it gives `The default is to ask if there is objection to unanimous consent.`, a 3-way poll if anyone objects, and consensus `as determined by the Convener`, with no ratio ("Closing plenary session responsibility and polls"). | Write "since in the rooms it needs a consensus call, which the 2:1 norm normally supports, and at plenary the convenor's consensus call, not every objector's persuasion". Confidence: high - the extract states no ratio for plenary.

p:definition-without-procedure | unsupported rule claim | "SD-4's own steps are the 2:1 norm after discussion, reasons from Against voters on request, escalation, and plenary's 3-way poll and question about national positions" reads as the full list, but SD-4 has further steps for an objection. "Proposals and papers": `the EWG or LEWG subgroup chair may elect to delay the progress of the active proposal by one meeting to give the objector(s) opportunity to bring an on-time proposal paper`. "Closing plenary session responsibility and polls": design subgroup chairs present approved designs `including to solicit all present to express any serious design concerns that may not have been discovered in subgroups so that these can be known and addressed before extensive wording effort is invested.` "Subgroup responsibility and polls": a concern shared `privately and in advance with the subgroup chair`, after which the chair may time-box the proposal and poll whether to go on. | Write "SD-4's own steps include ..." or add the one-meeting delay for a rival's paper and plenary's call for serious design concerns to the list, citing f:no-wait-alternatives and f:design-concerns-solicited. Confidence: medium - holds as written only if the list was not meant to be complete.

inf:call-above-norm | unsupported rule claim | SD-4, "Subgroup responsibility and polls": `A proposal normally advances if there are more than twice as many in favor of a proposal as against, after discussion of the concerns of those voting against and possibly a re-poll to see if opinions have improved.` The norm itself names a re-poll as a possible step, and `The subgroup chair may take any polls they choose.` Nothing in the extract makes a re-poll on a count above 2:1 a departure from the norm; only declining departs from "normally advances". | Write "declining departs from it, while a re-poll only delays the advance it gives", and check whether l:call-above, t:chair-call, and l:dchair-call need re-polling and declining ranked apart. ## Absence searches

Searches were case-insensitive over the extract, using word stems where shown. "SD-4" means every SD-4 section in the extract.

- f:no-reason-record (SD-4). Searched: record, minute, reason, answer, tally, objection, why, rationale, articulat, explain, justif, summar, wiki, register, log, note, defend, ask. Holds. The only reasons steps are the request to Against voters and plenary's spoken question to a subgroup chair about `the reason for the decision of the subgroup`. "All meetings must be minuted." sets no content, and the Straw Polls wiki page lists polls and papers, not reasons.
- f:no-reasons-other (SD-4). Searched: reason, rationale, articulat, explain, justif, why, ask, asked, defend. Holds. Only Against voters are named. Plenary asks a subgroup chair, not a voter, and "explain how and why" is a step for whoever raises a concern.
- f:iso-no-reason-record (ISO 1.8.2, 2.5.7, 5.5). Searched: reason, rationale, justif, why, basis, ground, explain, because, record, regist, summar, document, minute, written. Holds. Registering an opposition, summarizing points of view, and documenting outcomes ask for no record of why consensus was judged to exist; "on the basis of the consensus" describes the decision, not a record.
- f:no-sustained-procedure (SD-4). Searched: sustain, concerned interest, important part, stakeholder, interest, assess, address, resolv, opposition, oppos, maintain, persist, register, record, minute, objection, Against. Holds. "Sustained", "concerned interests", and "important part" occur only in the quoted definition, and SD-4 never uses "register". The plenary question on significant opposition and the escalation steps reach an opposition but do not name a sustained opposition as such.
- f:no-sponsor-cap (SD-4 and the listed ISO clauses; the extract's only other ISO clause is 0.1). Searched: sponsor, employ, compan, organization, fund, pay, paid, maximum, limit, number of. Holds. "Sponsor" occurs only for meeting sponsors in 5.3.1, and the only cap is 1.12.1's maximum number of Experts appointed by each P-member and liaison organization.
- f:no-merit-ruling (SD-4). Searched: rule, ruling, decide, decision, arbit, correct, authority, quality, judg, evaluat, merit, opinion, determin, select, pursu, recommend, verdict, adjudic. Fails in part (see finding): the direction group's opinion on any specific proposal, the design subgroups' ownership of selecting designs, and a chair's "own judgment" on expediting a proposal. The item's stem "judge" misses "judgment".
- f:no-concern-record (SD-4). Searched: privately, private, concern, record, minute, written, in writing, wiki, note. Holds. The private-concern step sets no record.
- f:no-paper-objections (SD-4). Searched: objection, concern, answer, address, rebut, respon, rationale, revision, feedback, oppos, disagree, critic, controvers, issue, defend. Holds for proposal papers. Revisions "based on feedback", rebuttal papers that "can of course be considered", and the TS Disposition of Comments paper do not ask a proposal paper to state the objections against it. The Disposition of Comments paper matters for p:written-answer.
- f:no-drawbacks-asked (SD-4). Searched: trade, cost, drawback, downside, disadvantage, limitation, weakness, shortcoming, risk, flaw, defect, constraint, problem. Holds. "Cost" occurs only in "travel costs", "shortcomings" and "risk" concern a TS or white paper and its users, and "problematic" concerns today's code. "Trade" never occurs, which also confirms inf:thm-no-request's "SD-4 never uses the word trade-off".
- f:no-interest-statement (SD-4). Searched: disclos, declar, affiliat, interest, employ, sponsor, fund, compan, behalf, pay, paid, conflict of, represent, organization. Holds. "Interest" means interest in a topic or proposal, and "paying" means paying attention.
- f:no-integration-check (SD-4). Searched: interact, integrat, coheren, consisten, together, harmon, conflict, fit, merg, combin, compatib, verif, check. Holds. "Integration testing" is a reason to merge a TS early, the editor merges approved changes and the editing committee verifies they were applied correctly, and "fits with the rest of the language's principles" concerns principles, not other adopted designs.
- f:no-parts-step (SD-4). Searched: part, parts, later, future, rule out, preclud, forecl, extension, extend, subsequent, follow-on, builds on, depend, increment, phase, piecemeal, series, sequence, together. Holds.
- f:iso-no-convenor (ISO 2.5.7). Searched: conven, working group, WG, leader. Holds. 2.5.7 names the Chair, the Secretary/Committee Manager, the Project Leader, and "the leadership", and mentions working groups only as places where sustained oppositions are expressed.
