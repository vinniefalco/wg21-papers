## 1. Starting points

### Axioms

1. **Axiom.** Attending a WG21 meeting in person costs money and time.
2. **Axiom.** A sponsor can pay a participant's travel and salary, but cannot attend, speak, or vote in the participant's place.
3. **Axiom.** A participant who attends most meetings expects to meet many of the same colleagues at future meetings.
4. **Axiom.** Given a choice, and other things equal, each participant, sponsor, national body, and user prefers the option that brings it more of what it values, sooner, at less cost to it, and weighs a gain in one value against a loss in another on one scale of its own. For this proof, a participant values adoption of the proposals they support, the interests of their sponsor if they have one, their standing, keeping any chair or convenor post they hold, their time and money, and a good result for users; a sponsor values its interests; a national body values the outcomes its national position supports and, where it has none yet, the interests concerned at its national level; and a user values the features they use or would use, how well those features work, and their own time and money.
5. **Axiom.** A participant who votes Against a colleague's proposal cannot rule out that the colleague will support the participant's own proposals less readily; a Neutral vote, or not voting, gives the colleague no more reason for this than an Against vote does.
6. **Axiom.** No participant can study every paper closely, so each chooses which papers to study.
7. **Axiom.** Writing or reviewing a paper takes time, and, other things equal, a longer or more complex paper takes more; preparing to give reasons for a vote also takes time.
8. **Axiom.** Before a design has been implemented and used, nobody can be certain how well it will work in practice.
9. **Axiom.** When a proposal is first presented, it has received less committee review than at any later meeting.
10. **Axiom.** If a decision is reversed, the work built on it must be redone or discarded.
11. **Axiom.** Revising a design throughout costs its authors more work than a change confined to one part of it.
12. **Axiom.** A participant learns how the committee works partly by attending its meetings.
13. **Axiom.** Over the years, some participants stop attending and others join.
14. **Axiom.** A meeting has a fixed number of hours, so time given to one paper is not available to another.
15. **Axiom.** WG21 plans a new edition of the C++ standard every three years, and ISO publishes each edition once national bodies approve it.
16. **Axiom.** When several subgroups meet at the same time, a participant can attend only one of them.
17. **Axiom.** A design in the standard applies to every user of the feature it specifies.

### Definitions

18. **Definition.** A participant is a registered expert attending a WG21 meeting. Guests a convenor invites are not participants.
19. **Definition.** A regular is a participant who expects to attend future meetings with many of the same colleagues.
20. **Definition.** A newcomer is a participant attending for the first time; a newcomer who expects to attend future meetings with many of the same colleagues is also a regular.
21. **Definition.** An author is a participant who presents a proposal paper as one of its authors.
22. **Definition.** A sponsor is an organization that pays some or all of a participant's costs of taking part.
23. **Definition.** A user is a person who writes C++ and does not participate.
24. **Definition.** A design room is EWG or LEWG, a study group is any WG21 study group, a wording room is CWG or LWG, and a subgroup is any of these. Plenary is the closing plenary session of a WG21 meeting, a chair is the chair of a subgroup, the convenor is the WG21 convenor, and a consensus call is a chair's or the convenor's determination of consensus.
25. **Definition.** A national body is an ISO member body that is a P-member of SC22, the committee WG21 belongs to. In quotations from the ISO/IEC Directives, "the committee" means SC22, "the Chair" means the chair of SC22, and "National Body" means any ISO member body, which on enquiry and final ballots includes bodies that are not P-members of SC22.
26. **Definition.** Favor, Neutral, and Against are the poll positions as SD-4 capitalizes them, with Strongly Favor and Weakly Favor counting as Favor and likewise for Against. The 2:1 norm is SD-4's statement on subgroup polls that a proposal normally advances when, after discussion of the concerns of those voting against, more than twice as many are in favor as against, even if many vote Neutral, though a Neutral majority of those voting can be concerning.
27. **Definition.** A participant's standing is the readiness of colleagues to support the participant's future proposals.
28. **Definition.** A player is a person or body whose choices the proof considers.
29. **Definition.** A player's stake in a poll is the difference the poll's outcome makes to what the player values.
30. **Definition.** A statement that a player does better with one option than another means that, with every value the statement does not name held equal, the option is worth more to the player than the other, by the player's own weighing of the values the statement names.
31. **Definition.** A direction poll is a poll on whether to continue work on a design.
32. **Definition.** A forwarding poll is a poll to send a proposal to the next room.
33. **Definition.** An incumbent on a topic is a design that has passed a direction poll in a design room and has not failed a later direction poll there.
34. **Definition.** An edition is one published version of the C++ standard.
35. **Definition.** The feature cutoff is the last meeting at which a new feature can be approved for an edition.
36. **Definition.** A reversal is the removal or replacement of a design or decision that WG21 or one of its subgroups has approved.
37. **Definition.** A settled pattern is a set of choices, one per player, in which no player improves their own position by changing only their own choice; game theory calls this a Nash equilibrium.
38. **Definition.** A small proposal is a proposal by one author who pays their own costs and expects its design to be reviewed within one meeting.
39. **Definition.** A large proposal is a proposal whose two or more authors have their costs paid by one or more sponsors and expect its review to span more than one edition. Its paper is longer and more complex than the paper of any small proposal.

## 11. The settled pattern

135. **Lemma.** Whatever the other players choose, a regular does no better than to vote Favor on a colleague's proposal they support and, on any other, to vote Against when an Against vote is worth more to them than its added cost and otherwise to vote Neutral or not at all. *[from 67, 68, 69, 71, 70, 73, 84, 122, 63, 37]*
136. **Lemma.** Whatever the other players choose, the authors of a large proposal do no better than to seek favorable direction polls early unless later review is expected to cost them more than becoming the incumbent sooner gains them, to present the work in parts unless the parts are worth little apart, to seek the IS over a TS unless the TS's experience is worth its delay to them or the design room requires a TS, and to answer objections with the least costly change that keeps the 2:1 norm met, unless the room has directed a change or they value the users' gain from a larger change above its cost. *[from 90, 87, 89, 96, 97, 98, 123, 80, 79, 81, 94, 95, 63, 37]*
137. **Lemma.** Whatever the other players choose, the author of a small proposal does no better than to keep the proposal presented, in person, remotely where offered, or by another qualified and prepared presenter, while pursuing it is worth more to them than the costs that fall on them, and to stop pursuing it when it is not. *[from 100, 86, 101, 80, 63, 37]*
138. **Lemma.** Whatever the other players choose, a study group chair does no better than to poll the competing designs, side by side where there are several, and to send on as the group's initial proposal a design that meets the 2:1 norm, and no design when none does, unless the chair's stake in the contest is worth more to them than what departing from the norm can cost them. *[from 104, 105, 106, 107, 108, 109, 115, 63, 37]*
139. **Lemma.** Whatever the other players choose, a design room chair does no better than to declare consensus when, after discussion of the Against voters' concerns, Favor votes are more than twice the Against votes and Neutral votes are not a majority of those voting, and to re-poll or decline when Favor votes are not more than twice the Against votes, unless the chair's stake in the proposal is worth more to them than what departing from SD-4's norm can cost them. *[from 113, 114, 112, 115, 116, 111, 117, 91, 121, 63, 37]*
140. **Lemma.** Whatever the other players choose, the convenor does no better than to follow SD-4's plenary procedure on what the subgroups forward and to declare consensus except where significant opposition holds national body positions or rests on objections escalated in time, unless the convenor's stake in the proposal is worth more to them than what departing from that procedure can cost them. *[from 118, 131, 119, 110, 115, 63, 37]*
141. **Lemma.** Whatever the other players choose, a national body does no better than to vote Yes with comments on a document whose other content it values above blocking any one feature in it, to vote No otherwise, and to raise its objections early, and ISO encourages it to abstain when it lacks national consensus or expert input. *[from 129, 128, 130, 133, 127, 124, 132, 63, 37]*
142. **Lemma.** Whatever the other players choose, a sponsor does no better than to pay for participation where the decisions it values are made, as long as the difference its paid participants make to those decisions is worth more to it than the cost. *[from 92, 86, 98, 132, 63, 37]*
143. **Lemma.** Whatever the other players choose, a user does no better than to become a participant when the user's stakes in the decisions they could take part in together exceed the costs of participating, and otherwise to use a cheaper move, such as feedback on a TS or white paper, a paper presented by a participant, or a request to their national body, when what it can bring them exceeds its cost. *[from 61, 63, 86, 95, 37]*
144. **Proposition.** The rules also contain provisions that bear on these effects. Every view must be heard, negative comments are welcome, and SD-4's conduct rules ask for respect and good faith and bar collusion; a group casting at least one third of the Favor-plus-Against votes as Against prevents the 2:1 norm from being met; alternatives can be explored and polled side by side, SD-4 says a good paper shows them, followup and rebuttal papers can be considered, and an author who declines a directed change should defend that decision; competing TSs are possible, a TS or white paper should list the controversial issues to address before it merges, and implementation experience informs voters. 145. **Theorem.** Under SD-4 and the clauses of ISO/IEC Directives Part 1 that chapter 1 quotes, in the edition that replaces the JTC 1 Supplement, and given the axioms, the following choices form a settled pattern, in which no player improves their position by changing only their own choice. Regulars vote Favor on colleagues' proposals they support, and on others vote Against when an Against vote is worth more to them than its added cost and otherwise vote Neutral or not at all. The authors of large proposals seek favorable direction polls early unless later review is expected to cost them more than the gain, present their work in parts, seek the IS over a TS unless the TS's experience is worth its delay to them or the design room requires a TS, and answer objections with the least costly change that keeps the 2:1 norm met, unless the room has directed a change or they value the users' gain from a larger change above its cost; the authors of small proposals keep them presented only while pursuing them is worth more to them than the costs that fall on them. Sponsors pay for participation where the decisions they value are made, as long as the difference their paid participants make is worth the cost. Study group chairs send on a design that meets the 2:1 norm and otherwise none; design room chairs declare consensus when, after discussion, Favor votes are more than twice the Against votes and Neutral votes are not a majority, and otherwise re-poll or decline; the convenor follows SD-4's plenary procedure; and each chair and the convenor departs from SD-4's procedure only when their stake in the proposal is worth more to them than what the departure can cost them. National bodies vote Yes with comments on documents whose other content they value above blocking any one feature, vote No otherwise, and raise objections early; and users become participants when their stakes in the decisions they could take part in together exceed the cost, and otherwise use cheaper moves when those are worth their cost.

     In this pattern, a proposal's progress depends in part on the participants who attend for it, on their stakes in it, and on the polls it has already won; a contest in which no design meets the 2:1 norm lasts, under that norm, until voters change position, a design changes, or the voters present change, unless a chair makes a call below the norm or the design room sends the designs to competing TSs; and the record need not show whether a proposal's objections were answered. The rules also contain provisions that bear on each of these effects. Technical merit enters through what participants value, including a good result for users, and through the discussion, papers, and implementation experience that inform their votes, while no one is assigned to rule on merit itself; and each result about large or small proposals holds for any proposal with the attributes it rests on. *[from 135, 136, 137, 138, 139, 140, 141, 142, 143, 144, 105, 76, 83, 77, 78, 4, 99, 94, 58f, 102, 62, 51a, 37]*

## 12. Corollaries

146. **Corollary.** If the settled pattern holds, the record should show the following. SD-4 keeps meeting records password-protected but lets poll questions and numeric results be quoted, and each prediction names its evidence, what would count against it, an innocent explanation, and the data that would tell the two apart. *[proved by 146a, 146b, 146c, 146d, 146e, 146f, 146g]* *[from 50a]*
     1. **Corollary.** Where one sponsor pays both a proposal's authors and many of the participants present, and its interests include the proposal's adoption, Against votes are a smaller share of the Favor-plus-Against votes than on other proposals; poll results and stated affiliations, taken as the observable measure of sponsor, are the evidence, and an Against share no lower on such proposals would count against it. An innocent explanation is that participants who work together share technical views, and comparing their votes on such proposals with their votes on proposals on the same topic from authors with other affiliations would tell the two apart. *[from 92, 4, 68, 145]*
     1. **Corollary.** Among proposals in which participants have similar stakes, the share of participants present who cast no vote rises with the length of the paper polled; poll results, attendance counts, and paper lengths are the evidence, and no such rise would count against it. An innocent explanation is that longer papers cover narrower topics that fewer participants know, and comparing papers on topics of similar breadth but different length would tell the two apart. *[from 93, 44g, 145]*
     1. **Corollary.** Large proposals appear as sequences of papers, each building on parts already adopted; the mailings are the evidence, and large designs adopted in one paper would count against it. An innocent explanation is that large designs are easier to review in parts, and comparing whether a design's parts are published together and reviewed in sequence or each published only after the one before is adopted would tell the two apart. *[from 96, 97, 98, 145]*
     1. **Corollary.** Removals after adoption are rare and arrive with national body comments or with national bodies whose No votes could fail a ballot; working drafts, ballot comments, and poll results are the evidence, and frequent removals by narrow margins would count against it. An innocent explanation is that adopted designs rarely need removal, and comparing removals with the defect reports filed against adopted features would tell the two apart. *[from 125, 129, 54g, 47c, 145]*
     1. **Corollary.** Small proposals stop advancing without a negative poll more often than large proposals do; the mailings and poll results are the evidence, and no such difference would count against it. An innocent explanation is that small proposals are more often ideas their authors drop on reflection, and comparing stops by authors who pay their own costs with stops by sponsored authors of papers of similar length would tell the two apart. *[from 137, 100, 98, 145]*
     1. **Corollary.** National bodies state positions on few proposals; national body comments and plenary records of national positions are the evidence, and positions on most proposals would count against it.      1. **Corollary.** Papers that advance over Against votes often leave unstated the objections they faced, other than the design alternatives and declined directions that SD-4 asks papers to address; the papers and poll results are the evidence, and papers that routinely state and answer their objections would count against it.
