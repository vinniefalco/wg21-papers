## 5. Momentum: incumbents and the cost of reversal

1. **Induction.** Each decision built on a design adds work that reversing the design would redo or discard, and more is built after each favorable poll. So the cost of reversing a design grows as it advances, whether or not the design is wrong. *[from ax:rework, ax:prefer, d:reversal, f:design-forward, f:wording-forward, f:plenary-owns]* {#ind:reversal-grows}
1. **Proposition.** A design passing a design room's direction poll becomes the incumbent, a paperless rival cannot block it, and the chair may delay it one meeting for a paper to arrive. A rival with a paper can be polled alongside it, the room can direct changes, and by default a TS merge proceeds with it unless an alternative is actively progressing. *[from f:no-paper, f:no-wait-alternatives, f:explore-alternatives, f:direction-implement, f:multiple-polls, f:ts-default, d:incumbent, d:polls]* {#p:incumbent-start}
1. **Proposition.** A design can become the incumbent at its first direction poll, when it has had less review than at any later meeting. From then on, every rival and every later change to it bears a reversal cost that grows as more is built on it. *[from ax:first-review, ax:unknown, d:incumbent, d:polls, p:incumbent-start, ind:reversal-grows, d:reversal]* {#p:early-polls}
1. **Proposition.** A rival wins a voter from an incumbent only with an advantage worth more to that voter than their share of the reversal's cost. That hurdle grows with each decision built on the incumbent, which faces no such hurdle itself. *[from ax:prefer, ind:reversal-grows, p:incumbent-start, d:incumbent, d:reversal, d:does-better]* {#p:challenger-bar}
1. **Lemma.** A direction poll asks voters only to agree that work should continue, less than a forwarding poll asks, so it can pass where a forwarding poll would fail. Yet passing it in a design room makes the design the incumbent. *[from d:polls, d:incumbent, f:design-forward, ax:prefer, p:early-polls]* {#l:poll-wording}
1. **Proposition.** If a poll places a proposal among the work the committee aims to include in the next IS, the proposal joins a category SD-4 has chairs take up first. A meeting's hours are fixed, so the time it then gets comes out of the time left for papers outside those categories. *[from f:prioritization, ax:hours]* {#p:roadmap-poll}
1. **Proposition.** Close study takes longer the bigger a design is, and a bigger design has more places where a defect can go unnoticed. For a reviewer whose stake does not grow with size, close study therefore stops being worth its time sooner on a big design than on a small one. *[from ax:review-cost, ax:attention, ax:unknown, ax:prefer, d:stake, d:does-better]* {#p:review-share}
1. **Proposition.** Participants who do not study a proposal typically abstain, so one costing more to study than most stakes in it is voted on mainly by those with large stakes. Unless those of them who object cast at least a third of the Favor-plus-Against votes, it can advance with no change at all. *[from f:not-vote, p:review-share, ax:review-cost, ax:prefer, l:vote-count, p:least-cost-response]* {#p:light-review}
1. **Proposition.** A design normally needs the 2:1 norm to advance, and once adopted, a paper or ballot comment to remove it normally needs the same, switching the default to keeping it. After a successful final ballot, design changes are expected to have near-unanimous consent, ISO bars technical changes at approval, and SD-4 calls published features much harder to update incompatibly. *[from f:two-to-one, f:no-paper, f:comment-content, f:near-unanimous, f:late-changes, f:is-hard-to-change, d:reversal]* {#p:burden-flip}
1. **Proposition.** An objector who arrives after a design has been adopted faces three obstacles at once. Removal needs a paper meeting the 2:1 norm, reversal costs more for every decision built on the design, and the record keeps its case but unwritten objections only in memory. *[from p:burden-flip, ind:reversal-grows, p:challenger-bar, p:reasons-unrecorded, p:late-objection]* {#p:late-arrival}

## 6. Sponsors, proposal size, and parts

1. **Proposition.** A sponsor adds a voter to a room for each participant it pays to attend, and neither SD-4 nor the searched ISO clauses limit how many it pays. SC22 may cap only the experts each national body or liaison appoints, and at SC22 itself each national body counts once. *[from p:who-votes, f:no-sponsor-cap, f:one-vote, ax:who-pays, f:experts-appointed, f:registered, f:expert-cap, f:pmember-consensus]* {#p:sponsor-votes}
1. **Proposition.** Changing the features a sponsor's interests depend on costs that sponsor rework of what it built on them, lost fit with its requirements, and more review, all still to come. The gain of a better design is spread across all the feature's users, and the same 2:1 norm decides every proposal, however many users will rely on it. *[from ax:prefer, ax:rework, ax:review-cost, ax:users-affected, f:two-to-one, d:sponsor]* {#p:sponsor-features}
1. **Induction.** Each part of a design adopted in parts becomes work every later part builds on, so a rival to the whole design must replace every part already adopted. *[from m:moves-author, ind:reversal-grows, d:reversal]* {#ind:adopted-parts}
1. **Proposition.** Presenting a design in parts lets the first parts be adopted before the whole is reviewed, and each adopted part adds to what a rival to the whole must replace. The cost is a paper and a presentation for each part. *[from m:moves-author, f:design-forward, f:wording-forward, f:plenary-owns, ind:adopted-parts, p:challenger-bar, ax:review-cost, f:present-required]* {#p:parts}
1. **Proposition.** Reviewers cannot study what later parts need of a first part that fixes a design's structure, SD-4 asks no paper to say, and once adopted, every rival must replace it. So a design-bound author's best first paper is the smallest part that fixes that structure and still meets the 2:1 norm against opponents who foresee the rest. *[from ax:first-review, ax:attention, ax:prefer, d:stake, f:no-parts-step, p:parts, ind:adopted-parts, p:challenger-bar, l:vote-count, d:design-bound]* {#p:first-part}
1. **Proposition.** A large proposal has more sponsored authors in the room than a small one, and more meetings where a poll can make it the incumbent or end that standing. Each step it advances brings it nearer to work that can make plenary progress, which SD-4 has chairs take up first. *[from d:large, d:small, d:incumbent, p:sponsor-votes, f:prioritization, f:design-forward, f:wording-forward, f:plenary-owns]* {#p:large-position}
1. **Proposition.** A small proposal's author bears every meeting's cost alone, and outside SD-4's priority categories the proposal gets agenda time only after the priority work. Where WG21 runs study groups that incubate early proposals, such as EWGI and LEWGI, SD-4's statement that study groups own incubating applies to them. *[from d:small, ax:cost, f:prioritization, f:agenda-papers, f:sg-initial-proposal, d:rooms]* {#p:small-position}
1. **Proposition.** Each result about large or small proposals rests on an attribute their definitions name: who pays the authors' costs, the number of authors, review's expected span, or the paper's length. So it holds for any proposal with that attribute, whatever its size. *[from d:small, d:large, p:large-position, p:small-position]* {#p:size-attributes}

## 7. Study groups and deadlocks

1. **Proposition.** On a topic with a study group, rival designs are first polled there, among those who chose that room over the rooms meeting with it. The design it sends on is where the design room starts, and the design room, which owns selecting designs, can still poll a rival that has a paper. *[proved by l:sg-purpose, l:sg-attendance, l:sg-start]* *[from f:vehicle, f:multiple-polls]* {#p:sg-decides}
   1. **Lemma.** SD-4 has the convenor form a study group on a design room chair's recommendation when a topic has broad interest, no unified initial proposal, and a strong chair candidate. It gives the study group the role of producing an initial proposal that can then enter the design room. *[from f:sg-formation, f:sg-initial-proposal]* {#l:sg-purpose}
   1. **Lemma.** A participant can attend only one of the rooms meeting at the same time, and votes and speaks only there. So a study group's voters are those for whom its topic outweighs the rooms meeting with it. *[from ax:parallel-rooms, ax:prefer, d:stake, f:one-vote]* {#l:sg-attendance}
   1. **Lemma.** The design room starts from the study group's initial proposal, which a rival without a paper does not block while it is an active proposal making progress. *[from l:sg-purpose, l:sg-attendance, f:no-wait-alternatives, f:sg-initial-proposal]* {#l:sg-start}
1. **Proposition.** Paying participants to attend a study group puts a sponsor's voters where the design the design room starts from is chosen. Where the other voters cast fewer Favor-plus-Against votes, fewer paid participants make up a third of the total, enough to block, or over two thirds, enough to meet the norm. *[from p:sg-decides, p:sponsor-votes, l:vote-count]* {#p:sg-sponsor}
1. **Proposition.** When each of two groups casts at least a third of the Favor-plus-Against votes against the other's design, neither design meets the 2:1 norm. The deadlock lasts until voters change position, a design changes, the voters present change, a chair calls consensus below the norm, or the design room sends both to competing TSs. *[from l:vote-count, f:two-to-one, f:design-forward, f:vehicle, f:competing-ts]* {#p:deadlock}
1. **Proposition.** In a long deadlock, a group whose sponsors pay its costs bears less per meeting than a group paying its own way. In the case the table below describes, the first holds and the second yields, whichever design is better. *[proved by l:hold-yield-holder-gains, l:hold-yield-both-hold, l:hold-yield-whole-over-half, l:hold-yield-half-over-deadlock, l:hold-yield-half-over-none, l:hold-yield-cheap-holds, l:hold-yield-costly-yields]* *[from ax:who-pays, ax:cost, p:deadlock]* {#p:attrition}
   1. **Lemma.** When one deadlocked group yields, the other holds, and no one else votes Against the holder's design, the deadlock ends with that design advancing. The holder gains its whole stake, the yielder none of its own, and neither bears the cost of holding through a deadlock. *[from p:deadlock, l:vote-count, d:stake]* {#l:hold-yield-holder-gains}
   1. **Lemma.** When both groups hold and the deadlock ends with either design equally likely, each can expect half its stake on average, less its cost of holding through it. Members whose costs sponsors pay bear less of that cost per meeting. *[from p:deadlock, ax:cost, ax:who-pays, d:stake]* {#l:hold-yield-both-hold}
   1. **Lemma.** Each group does better holding while the other yields, gaining its whole stake at no cost of holding, than when both yield to a compromise worth half its stake. *[from l:hold-yield-holder-gains, ax:prefer, d:does-better]* {#l:hold-yield-whole-over-half}
   1. **Lemma.** Each group does better when both yield to a compromise worth half its stake than when both hold. That holds because a deadlock both hold brings half its stake on average, less a cost of holding. *[from l:hold-yield-both-hold, ax:cost, ax:prefer, d:does-better]* {#l:hold-yield-half-over-deadlock}
   1. **Lemma.** Each group does better when both yield to a compromise worth half its stake than when it yields while the other holds, which brings none of its stake. *[from l:hold-yield-holder-gains, ax:prefer, d:does-better]* {#l:hold-yield-half-over-none}
   1. **Lemma.** A group whose cost of holding is less than half its stake does better holding through a deadlock both hold than yielding while the other holds. That holds because half its stake on average, less that cost, leaves it better off than none of its stake. *[from l:hold-yield-both-hold, l:hold-yield-holder-gains, ax:prefer, d:does-better]* {#l:hold-yield-cheap-holds}
   1. **Lemma.** A group whose cost of holding is more than half its stake does better yielding while the other holds than holding through a deadlock both hold. That holds because half its stake on average, less that cost, leaves it worse off than none of its stake. *[from l:hold-yield-both-hold, l:hold-yield-holder-gains, ax:prefer, d:does-better]* {#l:hold-yield-costly-yields}

Second number: group B's rank. 4 is best. † marks a Pareto-optimal cell.* {#t:hold-or-yield}
