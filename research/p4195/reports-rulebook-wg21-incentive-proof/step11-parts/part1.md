## 2. Who decides, and with what moves

1. **Proposition.** The participants present decide a design's content: chairs call consensus on their votes, counted under the 2:1 norm, in the design rooms and the study groups feeding them. SD-4 assigns no one to judge a design's quality, wording rooms check only that wording expresses the design, and national bodies ballot only on whole documents. *[from f:one-vote, f:two-to-one, f:design-forward, f:plenary-owns, f:plenary-no-design, f:sg-initial-proposal, f:vehicle, f:no-merit-ruling, f:spec-subgroups, f:wording-forward, f:wording-refer, f:iso-ballot, f:ballot-no, f:no-conditional-yes, d:rooms]* {#p:who-decides}
1. **Proposition.** Each person in a subgroup's room casts one vote, guests a convenor invites included, while no user votes in any WG21 poll or in the ballots on its drafts. *[from f:one-vote, f:guests, f:all-nbs-vote, f:iso-ballot, d:participant, d:user, d:national-body]* {#p:who-votes}
1. **Proposition.** The players are result-seeking authors, design-bound authors, regulars, study group chairs, design room chairs, the convenor, national bodies, sponsors, and users. The proof takes as given the choices of wording room chairs, guests, participants who are not regulars, and the direction group, and the convenor's appointments, study groups, and guest invitations. *[from p:who-decides, p:who-votes, d:settled-pattern, d:author, d:result-seeking, d:design-bound, d:regular, d:rooms, d:national-body, d:sponsor, d:user]* {#m:players}
1. **Proposition.** Each player has these moves under the rules. *[proved by m:moves-author, m:moves-regular, m:moves-chair, m:moves-convenor, m:moves-nb, m:moves-sponsor, m:moves-user]* {#m:moves}
   1. **Lemma.** An author can submit papers, including papers that each propose part of a design, and present them or have a qualified and prepared presenter do so. An author can also revise them and ask the chair for a poll or the design room for a ship vehicle, which the chair and the design rooms decide. *[from f:no-paper, f:present-required, f:invested, f:chair-polls, f:vehicle, d:author]* {#m:moves-author}
   1. **Lemma.** A regular can attend or not, study a paper or not, speak, and vote Favor, Neutral, or Against, or not at all. A regular can also write a paper, raise a concern privately with a chair, and ask their national body to take a national position. *[from ax:cost, ax:attention, f:negative-welcome, f:one-vote, f:not-vote, f:no-paper, f:followup-papers, f:private-concern, f:escalate-nb, d:regular]* {#m:moves-regular}
   1. **Lemma.** A chair can choose the polls, order the agenda by SD-4's priorities, time-box a new proposal and poll whether to go on, re-poll after discussion, ask Against voters for reasons, and allow limited exploration of alternatives. A design room or wording room chair also makes the consensus call that forwards a proposal, a design room chair can delay a proposal one meeting for a rival without a paper, and a study group chair polls which design leaves the group as its initial proposal. *[from f:chair-polls, f:prioritization, f:direction-group, f:agenda-papers, f:timebox, f:two-to-one, f:against-reasons, f:explore-alternatives, f:design-forward, f:wording-forward, f:no-wait-alternatives, f:sg-initial-proposal, d:rooms]* {#m:moves-chair}
   1. **Lemma.** The convenor can appoint chairs, form study groups, invite a guest for up to two meetings, and make plenary consensus calls. In plenary the convenor can ask whether Against votes are personal or national body positions. *[from f:chairs-appointed, f:sg-formation, f:guests, f:plenary-owns, f:plenary-consent, f:plenary-nb]* {#m:moves-convenor}
   1. **Lemma.** A national body can appoint experts, vote Yes or No or abstain on ballots, comment, state a national position in plenary, and appeal. It can also object in SC22 and JTC 1 to action taken on a topic not on the agenda. *[from f:experts-appointed, f:iso-ballot, f:all-nbs-vote, f:nb-abstain, f:comments-national, f:plenary-nb, f:agenda-notice, f:appeal]* {#m:moves-nb}
   1. **Lemma.** A sponsor can pay for participation and choose whom and how many it pays, and a category C liaison's representatives act for it. Unless it is an ISO member body or a liaison organization, a sponsor has no vote of its own in WG21's polls or in the ballots on WG21's drafts. *[from d:sponsor, ax:who-pays, f:no-sponsor-cap, f:one-vote, f:experts-appointed, f:experts-personal, f:iso-ballot, f:all-nbs-vote, d:national-body]* {#m:moves-sponsor}
   1. **Lemma.** A user casts no vote in WG21's polls or ballots but can give feedback on a TS or white paper or write a paper a qualified and prepared participant presents. A user can also raise their interests with a national body, which ISO asks to take account of all interests concerned at the national level, or become a participant. *[from p:who-votes, f:ts-feedback, f:present-required, f:iso-early, d:user]* {#m:moves-user}

## 3. What a vote costs

1. **Lemma.** Under the 2:1 norm each Against vote raises by two the Favor votes a proposal needs, while Neutral votes and abstentions leave the count unchanged. One Against vote changes the result only at one or two votes above the line, and at least a third of the Favor-plus-Against votes cast Against keeps the norm unmet. *[proved by l:against-double, l:neutral-outside, l:room-size, l:one-third-against]* {#l:vote-count}
   1. **Lemma.** Under the 2:1 norm, each Against vote raises by two the number of Favor votes a proposal needs. *[from f:two-to-one, d:positions]* {#l:against-double}
   1. **Lemma.** Neutral votes and abstentions do not enter the 2:1 comparison, though SD-4 says a Neutral majority of those voting can be concerning. *[from f:two-to-one, f:not-vote, d:positions]* {#l:neutral-outside}
   1. **Lemma.** One more Against vote changes whether the 2:1 norm is met only when the Favor votes exceed twice the other Against votes by one or two. *[from f:two-to-one, d:positions]* {#l:room-size}
   1. **Lemma.** Any group casting at least one third of the Favor-plus-Against votes as Against prevents the 2:1 norm from being met. *[from f:two-to-one, d:positions]* {#l:one-third-against}
1. **Proposition.** For a regular, an Against vote on a colleague's proposal costs more than a Neutral vote or none. It risks the colleague's later support no less, and only Against voters can be asked for reasons, which take time to prepare. *[from ax:support, ax:repeat, ax:prefer, ax:review-cost, f:against-reasons, f:no-reasons-other, d:regular, d:standing]* {#p:against-costs-more}
1. **Proposition.** A regular's doubt about a colleague's proposal enters the 2:1 count only if it is worth the extra cost of an Against vote. That vote changes the result only when the count sits one or two votes above the line, though it can also win changes to the design. *[proved by l:vote-below, l:vote-above]* *[from l:vote-count, p:against-costs-more, ax:prefer, f:against-reasons, f:direction-implement, d:stake, d:does-better]* {#p:doubt-outside-count}
   1. **Lemma.** When an Against vote is worth less to the regular than its extra cost, the regular ranks a Neutral vote or none above it. *[from p:against-costs-more, ax:prefer, d:does-better]* {#l:vote-below}
   1. **Lemma.** When an Against vote is worth more to the regular than its extra cost, the regular ranks it above a Neutral vote or none. *[from p:against-costs-more, ax:prefer, d:does-better]* {#l:vote-above}

*Table: One regular's vote on a colleague's proposal that the regular doubts. Columns: what an Against vote is worth to the regular, compared with its added cost. Each cell: the regular's rank of the vote. 2 is best within each column.* {#t:vote-choice}

| | Worth below cost | Worth above cost |
|---|---|---|
| **Neutral or abstain** | **2** | 1 |
| **Against** | 1 | **2** |

1. **Proposition.** With many Favor votes cast, one objector's Against vote changes the result only if enough others also vote Against, so its worth to each objector depends on the others' votes. The same objectors can then form either of two settled patterns: just enough of them vote Against to block, or none does. *[from l:vote-count, p:doubt-outside-count, p:against-costs-more, d:stake, d:settled-pattern]* {#p:objector-coordination}
1. **Proposition.** A participant can argue for another design and still vote Neutral, which leaves the count unchanged and draws no request for reasons. *[from f:negative-welcome, l:vote-count, f:against-reasons, f:no-reasons-other]* {#p:prefer-vs-stop}
1. **Proposition.** Two regulars with pending proposals each risk the other's support for their own by opposing the other's proposal. When each regular's doubt is worth less than that risk and the time for reasons, neither opposes, and both do better than if both opposed. *[proved by l:mutual-refrain, l:mutual-own-vs-other]* *[from ax:support, ax:prefer, p:against-costs-more, p:doubt-outside-count, l:vote-count, d:does-better]* {#p:mutual-support}
   1. **Lemma.** In the case the table below describes, each regular ranks refraining above opposing, whatever the other does. That holds because an Against vote on the other's proposal is then worth less to them than its extra cost. *[from p:doubt-outside-count, l:vote-below, ax:support]* {#l:mutual-refrain}
   1. **Lemma.** In that case, each regular ranks opposing while the other refrains above refraining while the other opposes, since the other's opposition costs them more than their own opposing does. *[from ax:prefer, d:does-better]* {#l:mutual-own-vs-other}

## 4. What the record keeps and how objections count

1. **Proposition.** A proposal can advance in WG21's rooms and plenary with objections unresolved, since it needs a consensus call, which the 2:1 norm normally supports, not every objector's persuasion. *[proved by l:address-not-resolve, p:advance-unanswered]* {#p:persuasion-not-required}
   1. **Lemma.** Under ISO, the obligation to address a sustained opposition is no obligation to resolve it, and a sustained opposition is not a veto. Still, ISO's general principles say consensus requires the resolution of substantial objections. *[from f:not-veto, f:resolve-substantial]* {#l:address-not-resolve}
   1. **Proposition.** A proposal can advance while discussed objections to it remain unresolved, because advancing requires a consensus call, which the 2:1 norm normally supports, and consensus need not imply unanimity. *[from l:address-not-resolve, f:design-forward, f:wording-forward, f:plenary-owns, f:two-to-one, f:consensus-def]* {#p:advance-unanswered}
1. **Proposition.** The record SD-4 and the searched ISO clauses require of a WG21 poll need not show whether an objection was answered or outvoted, or why consensus was called over it. *[proved by l:record-content, l:record-checks, l:iso-register-level]* *[from f:iso-no-reason-record]* {#p:record-indistinct}
   1. **Lemma.** SD-4 requires minutes and lets poll questions and numeric results be quoted publicly, but requires no record of how an objection was answered or why a consensus call was made. Plenary may ask a subgroup chair to summarize the reason for a decision. *[from f:minutes, f:protected, f:no-reason-record, f:plenary-reason]* {#l:record-content}
   1. **Lemma.** ISO lets participants comment on or object to minutes before they are confirmed, and has the chair of SC22 see that points of view at SC22's meetings are summarized. It bars participants from commenting publicly on others' contributions. *[from f:minutes-objections, f:views-summarized, f:no-public-comment]* {#l:record-checks}
   1. **Lemma.** The chair of SC22 judges consensus at SC22's committee stage, and ISO 2.5.7 recommends steps for a sustained opposition, including registering it, to the leadership. It does not say whether a convenor applies them inside a working group, and under either reading SD-4 adds no step for assessing or registering an opposition in subgroup polls. *[from f:chair-judges, f:pmember-consensus, f:committee-stage, f:iso-register, f:leadership-judges, f:sustained-def, f:no-sustained-procedure, d:national-body]* {#l:iso-register-level}
1. **Proposition.** SD-4 adopts ISO's definition of consensus, which turns on sustained opposition, but names no step for judging whether an opposition is sustained or for registering one, and no criteria for an important part of the concerned interests. Whoever ISO's leadership is for a working group, SD-4's own steps are the 2:1 norm after discussion, reasons from Against voters on request, escalation, and plenary's 3-way poll and question about national positions. *[from f:wg-consensus, f:consensus-def, f:sustained-def, f:two-to-one, f:against-reasons, f:escalate-deadline, f:plenary-consent, f:plenary-nb, f:iso-register, f:no-sustained-procedure]* {#p:definition-without-procedure}
1. **Lemma.** Under SD-4's definition, an objection prevents consensus only if it concerns a substantial issue, is maintained, and comes from an important part of the concerned interests. The leadership judges that part case by case, none of the three conditions asks whether the objection is right, and every view must still be heard. *[from f:consensus-def, f:sustained-def, f:concerned-interests, f:majority-listen, f:views-heard]* {#l:consensus-test}
1. **Proposition.** When an objection's Against votes are too few to stop the 2:1 norm, the cheapest response keeping it met at that poll is no change or a partial change. Finding out whether those objectors are right leaves that count unchanged, and SD-4 expects any change the room directs to be made or defended with new information. *[from ax:prefer, ax:redesign-cost, ax:review-cost, l:vote-count, f:direction-implement]* {#p:least-cost-response}
1. **Proposition.** SD-4 asks an Against voter to be ready to name the changes that would win them over, or to say that none would. Concerns stated as changes give authors items to grant one at a time, while opposition in any form offers none and weighs in the poll only as a vote. *[from f:against-reasons, m:moves-author]* {#p:change-list}
1. **Proposition.** Of minutes, non-public email lists, meeting records, ballot comments, and papers, only a paper puts an objection's argument outside the password-protected materials without anyone else's help. Writing one takes time. *[from p:record-indistinct, f:protected, f:minutes-objections, f:comments-national, f:late-info, f:escalate-paper, m:moves-regular, ax:review-cost]* {#p:dissent-needs-paper}
1. **Proposition.** A proposal's case lasts for decades in its required paper, while an unwritten reason survives only with those who heard it and, once they all leave, only by rediscovery. Outside the protected records, an objection's argument outlasts its hearers only in writing, such as the objector's paper or an author's paper showing the alternative or defending a declined change. *[from f:no-paper, ax:turnover, p:dissent-needs-paper, p:record-indistinct, f:show-alternatives, f:direction-implement]* {#p:reasons-unrecorded}
1. **Proposition.** SD-4 describes an objector who presses on after a consensus call, without new information or allowed escalation, as not accepting a group decision, whether or not anyone answered it. It says the same of a national body comment repeating a decided objection, unless the comment brings new information or the body warned all along that it would vote No. *[from f:minority-accept, f:accept-decisions, f:escalation-allowed, f:timely, f:ballot-decided, f:ballot-new-info, f:no-surprise]* {#p:repeat-objection}
1. **Proposition.** SD-4 gives an objection weight at plenary only if its holder escalated it by 5pm the evening before, on the email lists and within their national body. There, by default, it is not a national position, and after the deadline even new information goes to the email lists or a ballot comment, with a discussion paper. *[from f:plenary-weight, f:escalate-deadline, f:escalate-nb, f:late-info, f:escalate-paper]* {#p:late-objection}
1. **Proposition.** No design is considered on merit alone: a proposal needs an on-time paper and a presenter, a rival needs a paper to exist, and objections need fixed forms and times. Participants learn these rules partly by attending, some only that way, and other things equal, whoever knows them has the better chance of adoption, whatever their design skill. *[from f:late-paper, f:present-required, f:no-wait-alternatives, f:one-vote, p:late-objection, f:ballot-new-info, ax:learning, m:moves]* {#p:fluency}
1. **Proposition.** Other things equal, a newcomer, lacking what earlier meetings teach, spends more effort getting a paper considered than a returning participant, though SD-4 expects everyone to know its procedures. Where WG21 runs study groups that incubate early proposals, such as EWGI and LEWGI, a newcomer's early proposal can start there. *[from ax:learning, d:newcomer, f:familiar, f:sg-initial-proposal, d:rooms]* {#p:newcomer}
