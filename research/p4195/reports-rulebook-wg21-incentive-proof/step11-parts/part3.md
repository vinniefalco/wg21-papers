## 8. Chairs, the convenor, and plenary

1. **Proposition.** Every consensus call has a cost: declaring consensus over Against votes risks later national body opposition, and declining delays work SD-4 says subgroup polls should favor. Departing from the 2:1 norm can cost the caller's post: chairs serve at the convenor's choice with no fixed term, and the convenor for terms SC22 may decline to renew. *[from f:favor-progress, f:plenary-nb, f:iso-ballot, f:escalate-nb, m:moves-nb, f:chairs-appointed, f:convenor-term, ax:prefer, d:national-body]* {#p:chair-threshold}
1. **Proposition.** Above the 2:1 line after discussion, declaring consensus follows SD-4's norm, though a Neutral majority can be concerning, and at or below it SD-4 allows a re-poll after discussion. A chair whose stake is worth less than what departing can cost makes the call the norm gives. *[proved by l:call-above, l:call-below]* *[from f:two-to-one, l:vote-count, f:against-reasons, p:chair-threshold]* {#p:chair-default}
   1. **Lemma.** A design room chair whose stake is worth less than what departing can cost ranks declaring consensus above declining or re-polling on a count above 2:1 after discussion. This holds when Neutral votes are not a majority of those voting. *[from f:two-to-one, p:chair-threshold, ax:prefer, d:does-better]* {#l:call-above}
   1. **Lemma.** On a count at or below 2:1 after discussion, such a chair ranks declining or re-polling above declaring consensus. *[from f:two-to-one, p:chair-threshold, ax:prefer, d:does-better]* {#l:call-below}

## 9. Users, quality, and disclosure

1. **Proposition.** No user votes, and taking part costs the participant or their sponsor, while a better design's gain is spread across all users and seldom turns on one user's effort. Users' interests reach decisions only through what participants themselves value, papers a participant presents, TS feedback, and national bodies, which form positions only where that is worth their members' time. *[from p:who-votes, m:moves-user, ax:users-affected, ax:prefer, f:iso-early, f:escalate-nb, ax:hours]* {#p:users-out}
1. **Proposition.** Fees are barred and everyone in a subgroup's room has one vote, yet only those who bear the cost of taking part, or whose sponsors do, vote in WG21's polls. ISO can remove inactive national body experts, and participants propose for needs they and their sponsors know, which, since users differ from them in what matters, need not match users'. *[from f:no-fee, f:remote, f:iso-cost, f:in-person-expected, f:registered, f:inactive-experts, p:who-votes, ax:cost, ax:who-pays, ax:prefer, ax:users-affected, f:present-required]* {#p:who-takes-part}
1. **Proposition.** An author gains at adoption, while a flaw that implementation and use have not yet exposed costs users only after adoption, and can fall on any user of the feature. *[from ax:prefer, ax:unknown, ax:users-affected, d:author, d:user]* {#p:reward-timing}
1. **Lemma.** Implementation and use reduce uncertainty about how well a design works, though even then a room cannot fully judge it at a poll. SD-4 describes a TS or white paper as a way to gain that experience, noting that its users typically should not rely on it in production code. *[from ax:unknown, f:ts-experience, f:ts-feedback]* {#l:implementation-informs}
1. **Proposition.** Winning a consensus call and producing the design that serves users best coincide only as far as voters can judge that at the poll and weigh it above other values. SD-4 assigns no one to judge it for them. *[from ax:prefer, ax:unknown, p:who-decides, l:consensus-test, l:implementation-informs, f:no-merit-ruling]* {#p:merit-gap}
1. **Proposition.** Close study costs the reviewer, while the gain of a better design is spread across all its users. So review worth more to users together than it costs, but less to any one participant, is review no participant does better doing. *[from ax:review-cost, ax:users-affected, ax:prefer, p:review-share, d:stake, d:does-better]* {#p:review-shortfall}
1. **Proposition.** Papers approved separately at one meeting first come together when the project editor merges their wording, which the editor's report records and a pre-ballot editing committee verifies. No SD-4 step checks whether the designs work together, while each author gains from their own design's adoption and a conflict costs users and later rework. *[from f:approval-polls, f:wording-baseline, f:editors, f:no-integration-check, ax:prefer, ax:unknown, ax:users-affected, ax:rework]* {#p:integration}
1. **Lemma.** SD-4 relies on the presenter, an expert in the paper's domain, to educate a room of general C++ experts. It also counts a feature's designers among the best experts on any concern about it. *[from f:presenter, f:concern-authors]* {#l:author-knows-more}
1. **Proposition.** Stating a drawback in the paper before an early poll can cost Favor votes when incumbency is decided, while a better-informed vote's gain is spread across the design's users. SD-4 says a good paper should show the alternatives considered, but it asks no paper to state its design's drawbacks, and a paper is considered without them. *[from ax:attention, p:early-polls, l:author-knows-more, ax:users-affected, ax:prefer, f:show-alternatives, f:no-drawbacks-asked, f:late-paper, f:present-required]* {#p:disclosure}
1. **Proposition.** No rule requires a written answer to an objection, apart from an author's defense of declining a directed change and ISO's response to each enquiry ballot comment. For the author, a written answer makes the objection easier to raise again, but also shows later what would not be new, the test SD-4 applies to repeated objections. *[from p:record-indistinct, f:no-paper-objections, f:direction-implement, f:respond-comments, f:comment-report, ax:turnover, p:repeat-objection, f:ballot-new-info]* {#p:written-answer}

## 10. The schedule and national bodies

1. **Proposition.** A proposal that misses an edition's feature cutoff waits at least for the next edition, planned three years later. It can ship sooner as a white paper, which has no prescribed schedule, and the same schedule keeps an unfinished feature from delaying the features that are ready. *[proved by l:cutoff]* *[from f:white-paper-process, f:plenary-owns, ax:schedule, d:edition]* {#p:miss-cost}
   1. **Lemma.** Once an edition's draft goes to ballot, SD-4 treats a ballot comment that requests adding a feature as out of scope, and WG21 plans the next edition three years later. *[from f:ballot-no-new-features, d:edition, ax:schedule]* {#l:cutoff}
1. **Proposition.** At the feature cutoff, a one-meeting delay for a paperless rival sends a proposal to the next planned edition. So does an Against vote keeping it below the 2:1 line, when the chair follows the norm. *[from p:miss-cost, f:no-wait-alternatives, l:vote-count, f:two-to-one, f:plenary-owns, d:edition]* {#p:cutoff-effect}
1. **Proposition.** A TS delays IS adoption by about one to two years, and its experience can bring incompatible changes first: a gain to result-seeking authors, a risk to design-bound ones. The design room can still require a TS, and ISO and SC22 now recommend the lighter white paper. *[from f:ts-lag, f:ts-experience, f:ts-feedback, f:is-hard-to-change, f:vehicle, f:white-paper, f:white-paper-process, l:implementation-informs, d:result-seeking, d:design-bound, ax:prefer]* {#p:prefer-is}
1. **Lemma.** Every national body may vote on enquiry and final ballots, which pass when two thirds of the P-members' votes are positive and at most a quarter of all votes negative, not counting abstentions or negative votes without technical comments. ISO encourages a body without national consensus or expert input to abstain, sustained opposition does not apply to these votes, and a final draft that fails goes back to SC22. *[from f:all-nbs-vote, f:enquiry-count, f:iso-ballot, f:no-conditional-yes, f:nb-abstain, f:not-ballots, f:failed-fdis, d:national-body]* {#l:ballot-rules}
1. **Proposition.** A national body's No opposes every feature in the document at once, including ones its own experts support, while WG21 considers its comments whatever its vote. A body valuing the rest of a document above blocking one feature does better voting Yes with comments, and one wanting the work in no form does better voting No. *[proved by l:rank-rest-yes, l:rank-rest-abstain, l:rank-oppose-no, l:rank-oppose-abstain]* *[from p:who-decides, f:experts-personal, f:no-means-no, f:respond-comments, f:comments-noted, l:ballot-rules, ax:prefer, d:does-better]* {#p:nb-comment}
   1. **Lemma.** A body that values the rest of a document above blocking any one feature ranks Yes with comments above abstaining. That holds because only the Yes counts toward the approval it prefers, and its comments are considered either way. *[from l:ballot-rules, f:no-means-no, ax:prefer]* {#l:rank-rest-yes}
   1. **Lemma.** Such a body ranks abstaining above No, since only the No counts against the approval it prefers and its comments gain nothing from a No. *[from l:ballot-rules, f:no-means-no, ax:prefer]* {#l:rank-rest-abstain}
   1. **Lemma.** A body that wants the work in no form ranks No above abstaining, since only the No counts against approval, which is what SD-4 says No is for. *[from l:ballot-rules, f:ballot-no, ax:prefer]* {#l:rank-oppose-no}
   1. **Lemma.** Such a body ranks abstaining above Yes with comments, since only the Yes counts toward approval of work it wants in no form. *[from l:ballot-rules, f:ballot-no, ax:prefer]* {#l:rank-oppose-abstain}
